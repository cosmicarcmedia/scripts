3
Three questions to ask any electrician
The thing the big competitor will not say
▾
Electrical work ends up behind walls, so a homeowner should ask these before anyone opens a panel. Gary can answer each with something already on the site: a license, free estimates, and before and after photos of service upgrades.
Spot C
Before an electrician touches your panel, ask three questions. One, are you licensed and insured? Two, can I get an estimate before you start? Three, who is going to be at my door? If you don't get a straight answer, keep looking. Those aren't trick questions. A good electrician won't mind being asked.
Spot A
Yes, we're licensed and insured. [OWNER: read your license number out loud so people can check it] Texas licenses electricians, and you can look up a license online. Yes, you can get an estimate, and ours are free. Ask what the estimate covers, and ask for it before the work starts. Residential or commercial, I answer the same way. [OWNER: who will be at the door, you or someone from your crew, and how long they've been with you]
Spot B
Electrical work ends up behind your walls where you can't see it. A mistake in there can sit quiet for years. So I want you to see the work before it disappears. Take a service upgrade, which is one of the jobs we do. Once it's finished, most of it is out of sight. On our website we post before and after photos of service upgrades, so you can see what a panel looks like when we start and when we finish. Look them up under the gallery. You can ask any electrician to show you photos of past work. It's easier to ask good questions when you've seen the work. [OWNER: one thing you check on every job before you close up the wall]
4
Signs your electrical panel needs a look
The useful one
▾
Service upgrades are on Gary's residential list and in the site's photo gallery. A homeowner who learns what to watch for remembers who explained it, and no one is asked to buy anything.
Spot A
Your electrical panel works every day and you never think about it. Here are some signs it may need a look. The panel is the box where your breakers live. A breaker trips on purpose when too much power is going through it. A breaker that trips again and again. One that trips once in a great while is doing its job. One that keeps tripping is telling you something. Lights that dim when the air conditioner or the dryer starts up. [OWNER: the sign you see most often in San Angelo houses]
Spot B
A fuse box in an older house. Extension cords running everywhere because there aren't enough outlets. Any of those can mean your service is working harder than it was built to. If you smell something burning near the panel, or the panel feels hot, leave it alone and call an electrician right away. If you see smoke or fire, call nine one one. And don't open the panel cover yourself.
Spot C
Upgrading the service on an existing house is something we do. We also run new service for new construction. [OWNER: about how long a service upgrade takes from start to finish] If you're unsure what's going on in your panel, call us. Please don't just keep resetting the same breaker and hope. Estimates are free, and I'll tell you what I see. I'm Gary Moore with Moore Electric in San Angelo.
