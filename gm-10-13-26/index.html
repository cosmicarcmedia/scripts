1
Pilates, your way
The origin and the face
▾
There is no owner story on the site, and the reviews keep praising the owner's knowledge and passion for Pilates. This script gives the owner a place to say why, in her or his own words, and lets customers' own phrases describe the studio.
Spot A
I'm [OWNER: your name], and this is Good Movement here in San Angelo. We teach reformer Pilates, mat Pilates, yoga, and barre. [OWNER: what first got you into Pilates] Our approach is modern and mindful. It's low impact strength training, with more flexibility and more mobility. In one line, it's Pilates, your way. We're a San Angelo studio, and we're easy to find.
Spot B
Our classes are built for people of all fitness levels. They're safe, they're effective, and they're low impact. A good class should strengthen, lengthen, and tone your whole body. It should build your endurance and your overall health. If you've never set foot in a Pilates studio, that's fine. Start here. Our mat class is called Time to Shred. Our reformer class is called Find Your Core. [OWNER: the year you opened and what the first class looked like]
Spot C
You don't have to take my word for it. A customer wrote that it's a beautiful space. Another said the workouts are just the right amount of challenging. Another said there's always a modification if you need one. And one wrote that Pilates here is for every body. Customers have given us five stars on Google. We have two locations, downtown on Chadbourne Street and over by the university on Johnson Street. Booking is on our website, goodmovement.net. Come try a class.

The local anchor
▾

Good Movement has two homes in San Angelo. Downtown, we're at 30 North Chadbourne Street. Over by the university, we're at 2609 Johnson Street. One phone number covers both, and it's 325 518 0337. Downtown is where you'll find our reformer classes. That one's called Find Your Core. The university location is where we teach mat classes. That one's called Time to Shred. [OWNER: who you see most at each location]
Spot A
In September, we put on a block party on Chadbourne Street. It ran from nine to one on a Saturday morning. [OWNER: what happened at the block party and who showed up] We love being downtown. 3
Three questions to ask before you sign up anywhere
The thing the big competitor will not say
▾
Anybody shopping for a studio compares rooms, teachers and cancellation rules, and the big franchise studios rarely volunteer the details. Answering all three out loud, with your own fine print, gives the owner something to say that a franchise script can't.
Spot C
Before you join any Pilates studio, ask three questions. How many people will be in the room? Who's teaching, and will they know my name? What happens if I can't make it? I'll answer all three for Good Movement.
Spot A
Our classes are small and intimate. A customer wrote that's why she didn't feel so nervous walking in. Another wrote that after a three week unlimited pass, she knew right away the membership was worth it. [OWNER: your class size limit] Customers here know their instructors by name, Raquel and Selena. A modification is always there if you need one. One customer called our instructors kind and informative. Another wrote that the knowledge and passion for Pilates shines through our carefully curated classes.
Spot B
Cancel at least six hours ahead, and there's no penalty. If you miss a class, it's a twenty dollar no show fee. Cancel inside six hours, and it's fifteen. Our schedule and our rules are on the website, with a Book Here button. Members can take extra classes at eighteen dollars each. Autopay members get a guest pass each month, so you can bring a friend. To end a membership, we need thirty days notice. It's all on our website, so nothing surprises you. We want you to know what you're signing up for before you sign. [OWNER: anything else a new client should know before signing up]
4
What to wear and what to expect at your first Pilates class
The useful one
▾
A nervous first timer wonders what to wear, what the machine is and whether anyone will judge them. Answering those three costs nothing and is exactly the push a new client needs to book.
Spot A
If it's your first Pilates class, here's everything you need to know. Wear a comfortable workout outfit. Pilates toe socks are optional. Pull long hair back. That's it. Show up a few minutes early so you can meet your instructor. Bring a water bottle. Check the schedule to see which class fits your day. When you book, say it's your first time, so the instructor knows.
Spot B
You'll take a mat class or a reformer class. A mat class is on the floor, using your own body weight. A reformer is a machine with a sliding carriage and springs, and you push and pull against them. Your instructor sets it up for you. Our mat class is Time to Shred, and our reformer class is Find Your Core. Pilates is known for core work, so expect to feel it. Both are low impact, and your instructor can show you a modification. Tell them before class if anything bothers you. [OWNER: how you decide which class a first timer should take]
Spot C
Book online at goodmovement.net. If plans change, cancel at least six hours ahead. [OWNER: what new clients get on their first visit] Take your time on the first class. Everyone in that room started somewhere. One customer wrote that she loves how much Pilates has helped her feel stronger and more confident. Ask the instructor anything you want. They'd rather you ask.
