1
I've trained dogs in San Angelo since 2002
The origin and the face
▾
His page has a name, a credential and no face doing the work. A trainer on camera saying who he is and what the credential took is the first thing a worried owner wants before handing over a dog.
Spot A
I've trained dogs professionally since 2002. That's more than twenty years of working with dogs and their owners. My name is Robert Broussard, and I train dogs here in San Angelo. I hold a master's degree, and I'm a Certified Professional Dog Trainer, Knowledge Assessed. [OWNER: what your master's degree is in and how it shapes the way you train]. Dog training has no license requirement, so a credential like mine is how you can tell a trainer who's been tested. I passed a comprehensive exam, and the credential takes at least 300 hours of dog training experience. You keep it with continuing education.
Spot B
I got into this because [OWNER: the real reason you started training dogs]. Today I work with puppies, adult dogs, and homes with more than one dog. I teach obedience and good manners. I train competition obedience. I work on behavior problems like fear, anxiety, and aggression. I run a puppy imprinting program for new puppies. And I use humane, up to date methods.
Spot C
I work with owners in San Angelo, and out to Abilene, Odessa, and Midland. [OWNER: which vets send you clients, or what a vet has said about your work]. A good trainer answers your questions. Ask me as many as you like. So if you've got a puppy, or a dog that needs work, call the number on my page. Tell me what your dog is doing, and I'll tell you what I'd do about it.
2
San Angelo gives your puppy a deadline, and the learning window closes first
The local anchor
▾
The city's own rules put a four month deadline on every dog, and the behavior experts say the best socializing window is the first three months. Putting those side by side gives a new puppy owner a local reason to call early.
Spot B
If you just got a puppy in San Angelo, the city gives you a deadline. By four months old, your dog needs a current rabies vaccination, a microchip, and a city registration. Dogs also have to be spayed or neutered, unless the city code makes an exception. But the veterinary behavior experts say the most important time to socialize a puppy is the first three months. That window closes a month before the paperwork is due.
Spot A
So the clock starts earlier than most people think. The experts say puppy classes can start as early as seven to eight weeks old. Ask your vet what shots your puppy needs first. They also say puppies should start socializing before they're fully vaccinated. I run a puppy imprinting program, and [OWNER: what puppies do in it and the ages you take]. Whatever you do, start before the paperwork does.
Spot C
A few other San Angelo rules are worth knowing. A dog can't be chained for more than two hours in a 24 hour period. If you have more than seven dogs over four months old, you need a multi pet permit. Homes with more than one dog are part of what I do. I work with owners from San Angelo out to Abilene, Odessa, and Midland. If your puppy is new, call me before the paperwork is due.
3
Dog training has no license, so ask these three questions
The thing the big competitor will not say
▾
Anyone can call themselves a dog trainer. This script gives owners three questions to check any trainer, drawn from the certification council's own advice, and lets his credential speak without naming anyone.
Spot C
Anyone can hang a sign that says dog trainer. Dog training has no licensing requirement and no regulatory oversight. So before you hire anybody, me included, ask three questions. First, what credential do you hold, and who gives it? Mine is the CPDT-KA, from the Certification Council for Professional Dog Trainers.
Spot A
It takes a comprehensive exam and at least 300 hours of dog training experience. The exam tests humane, science based training, and you earn continuing education to keep it. Second, how do you handle a dog that misbehaves? Be careful with a trainer who talks about dominance, or being the alpha, or who relies mostly on punishment. The certification council lists both of those as red flags. Ask what the trainer does with a dog that gets it wrong.
Spot B
Third, have you worked with my kind of problem? A puppy, a dog with fear or anxiety, a house full of dogs, competition obedience. Those are the dogs I work with. The certification council also says a trainer should answer your questions clearly and be patient with them. And ask yourself whether you feel comfortable with the trainer, because the certification council lists that too. So ask me the same three questions. I'll answer every one.
4
The three things a well behaved dog gets from you
The useful one
▾
These are the three keys to success from his own listing. Spelling them out in plain language gives owners something they can use tonight and tells them what working with him looks like.
Spot A
I've trained dogs professionally since 2002. I've learned three keys to a well behaved dog. I teach obedience and good manners, and I train competition obedience too. Whether you've got a new puppy or an adult dog, it starts in the same place. Leadership skills. Clear communication of rules and commands. And proper management of your dog's environment and routine. And every one of them is your job.
Spot B
Leadership starts with you being calm and consistent, and [OWNER: what leadership looks like with a dog, in one example]. Dogs learn from what happens every day, so what you do on a Tuesday matters as much as the lesson. Clear communication means each command has one word, and everyone in the house says it the same way. It also means the rules stay the same from one day to the next. A dog can't follow a rule that changes.
Spot C
Management is the third key, and it's easy to overlook. It's the setup around the dog, the places it stays and the routine it lives by. Meals, walks, and bathroom breaks go on a schedule. If something in the house keeps causing trouble, [OWNER: one example of fixing the setup around the dog]. If you have more than one dog, the routine matters even more. Get those three right, and most of the work gets easier. If your dog has fear, anxiety, or other behavior problems, call me. Tell me what's going on, and we'll start with the three keys.
