1
We bought a bakery that has been here since 1982
The origin and the face
▾
A chocolate shop that adds a bakery is a story every San Angelo birthday cake has a stake in. Told in the family's own voice, with the fresh start said out loud, it introduces the people before the product.
We're Charlie, Summer and Misty Mullin. Our family has been slinging chocolates since 2019. The shop on East Concho Avenue was established in 2008, and we took the reins eleven years later. [OWNER: one line on what each of you did before the shop] In October of 2025, we bought Halfmann's Cake Cottage. That bakery has been part of San Angelo birthdays, weddings and sweet celebrations since 1982. Odds are you have eaten a slice of it at somebody's party. That is a lot of other people's memories to take care of. So we said it plainly when we announced it. Some customers did not get the experience they deserved. We are putting the focus on fresh products, real customer service, and a welcoming room every time you walk in. This is a fresh start, and we would love for you to come back and give us another try. The remodel is happening while the doors stay open, so thank you for your patience. We are looking for experienced cake decorators and bakers. [OWNER: one line on why you wanted a bakery] Halfmann's is getting its own Facebook and Instagram page, so you can watch the remodel happen. The chocolate shop is still right here on Concho Avenue, still making the Texas Truffle. Two shops, one family. Come see both, and tell us what you think.
2
Please leave your horse outside
The local anchor
▾
It puts the shop on its own downtown street, with the humor the website already has and the Texas flavors a visitor can taste. Locals hear their own town in it, and gift buyers hear a reason to pick this box.
If you have walked downtown San Angelo, you have walked past our door at 6 East Concho Avenue. Our website asks you to leave your horse outside, unless he likes chocolate. That is about how we run the place. Real chocolate, a little humor, and nobody in a stiff collar. The shop was established in 2008. In 2021, we bought our downtown location and won a small business competition that paid for equipment to make more chocolate. We are known for the Texas Truffle. Then there is the Yellow Rose collection, made in honor of all the ladies in Texas. It has Mesquite Bean, Bananas Foster, Cappuccino Tiramisu, Margarita and Dulce de Leche. [OWNER: the flavor locals ask for first] Texas Highways has written about us, and so has Shop Across Texas. Still, the best review is somebody walking out of the shop on Concho Avenue with a box under their arm. If you cannot get downtown, we can come to you. San Angelo To-Go delivers for us, and DoorDash is on the website too. So whether you are on Concho Avenue or across town, there is chocolate on the way. We have opened shops in Granbury and Glen Rose too, and the first one stays right here. Right now the website has a Gingerbread House Advent Calendar and a Poinsettia Assortment.
3
Ask who is making your birthday cake
The thing the big competitor will not say
▾
It hands the owner a question to put to any cake counter in town, and gives a bakery that is starting over a fair way to say it wants to be known by name. The gift box line does the same for the chocolate.
Here is a question worth asking at any cake counter in town. Who is making this, and can I talk to them? At a lot of big counters, the answer is a form and a pickup time. At Halfmann's, our family owns the place. We are bringing in experienced cake decorators and bakers. [OWNER: how a custom cake order works, and who you talk to] So when you ask who made it, you get a name. Chocolate works the same way. A gift box from a big national brand holds the same flavors in every state. Ours holds a Mesquite Bean truffle. It holds a Yellow Rose, made in honor of all the ladies in Texas. When somebody opens that box, they know it came from San Angelo. Texas Highways wrote that the shop pays homage to San Angelo's frontier history. We have been in Shop Across Texas, and in Hill Country Passport magazine. We ship gift boxes too, so a San Angelo family can send a piece of home to somebody far away. All of it started with a family that wanted to make fun, high quality chocolate. Now we are putting that same care into birthdays and weddings. Ask us anything about the chocolate, and ask us anything about the cake. Come in, ask your questions, and watch us answer them.
4
Where to keep your chocolate in a West Texas summer
The useful one
▾
A tip that costs nothing and saves somebody's gift. It shows the shop knows chocolate, and it ends on the gift card, which is a real product on their site.
Chocolate has two enemies in West Texas, and one of them is your car. Heat and sunlight are what hurt it most. Do not leave a box of truffles on the seat while you run errands. A parked car gets hot fast, even in the shade. If chocolate melts and sets again, it turns gray and chalky. It is still safe to eat, but it will not look like a gift. Heat does that fast in a West Texas August. The second enemy is the refrigerator. A fridge can pull moisture onto the surface and leave the chocolate dull and sugary. A cool, dry cabinet is a better home for it. Chocolate also picks up smells, so keep it away from the onions. [OWNER: how you tell customers to chill chocolate, and how long to let it warm back up] If you are giving a box as a gift, pick it up the same day you give it. If it has to travel across town in August, bring a small cooler bag. Keep it out of the sun on the dashboard, and out of the trunk. Here is the one chocolate gift that never melts. Our gift cards are on the website. Nothing says I am thinking of you quite like one. Somebody gets to pick their own truffles, and you did the thinking.
