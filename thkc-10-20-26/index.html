1
From prep cook to the kitchen on Rio Concho Drive
The origin and the face
▾
A diner at Rio Concho sees a buffet line. The chef's story, from prep cook to a team of thirty to Dallas to a snow cone trailer turned barbecue truck, is what makes a stranger trust this face with a party.
Spot A
I started as a prep cook here in San Angelo. I climbed to culinary manager, with more than thirty people on my team. Then I spent seven years in Dallas, and I was an executive chef there. And I walked away from it in three weeks to cook barbecue out of a snow cone trailer. I'm Courtney Hampton, a Brooklyn native. [OWNER: one line on who taught you to cook, or the first dish you ever made]
Spot B
Over the years I've trained under chefs with James Beard Awards and Michelin stars. The days in Dallas were long, and every one of them was for somebody else's restaurant. Jaton and I had talked for years about opening our own place. Then her dad found a building for rent on North Chadbourne. Within three weeks we had quit our jobs, packed up the house, and moved home. We had no money in the bank. We sold everything.
Spot C
We opened in September of 2017 as Rustic Field to Table BBQ. Jaton will tell you I smoked fourteen to sixteen hours a day. In June of 2022 I opened The Hampton's Kitchen and Catering at Rio Concho Manor. The McGowens fed this building before us. I said then that I want to leave a legacy the way they did. Everything in my career led to this kitchen, from that first prep station to the smoker. Come see it.
2
Lunch inside a retirement community, open to anyone
The local anchor
▾
A dining room inside a retirement community looks closed to outsiders, so this script says it is open, what lunch costs, and what a week of menus looks like, all in the chef's own voice.
Spot B
The lunch inside this retirement community is open to anyone in San Angelo. It's on the first floor of a high-rise on Rio Concho Drive that opened in 1964. Two hundred twenty two apartments upstairs, and a dining room downstairs. The campus sits on seventeen acres overlooking the Concho River. The Rio Concho Trust started it in 1962 to give seniors a place to live. The public is always welcome. You don't have to know anybody who lives here.
Spot A
We serve lunch from eleven to one thirty, Sunday through Friday. It's twelve dollars for the public, and kids twelve and under eat for five. The weekly menu goes up on our Facebook page. A recent week had chicken fried steak with cream gravy on Monday. Fajitas and pulled pork asado on Tuesday. Honey sriracha wings and Italian sausage and peppers on Wednesday. Teriyaki chicken and pork eggrolls on Thursday. Cornmeal catfish and jalapeno hushpuppies on Friday. Then Sunday was roasted turkey, pot roast, southern fried chicken, and cornbread dressing.
Spot C
Some of the folks at the next table live upstairs. Neighbors and people from across town eat together in that room. Rio Concho calls it a place for fellowship among neighbors and the public. If you're in a hurry, we pack entrees to go. The address is 401 Rio Concho Drive. This is the day the Lord has made. Come hungry.
3
Ask who is standing at the stove before you book a caterer
The thing the big competitor will not say
▾
Who is cooking is the question every host should ask a caterer. This script answers it with a real customer's praise from the Pearl on the Concho birthday, and it names no one else.
Spot C
Before you book a caterer, ask who is going to be standing at the stove. At a big operation, the person who sells you the event is rarely the person who cooks it. I know how those places run, because I managed more than thirty people in a big restaurant kitchen. Then I owned a barbecue place on North Chadbourne, where every order was mine to get right. Here at The Hampton's, my name is on the door.
Spot A
In July of 2024 a man planned his mom's seventy fifth birthday from Washington, DC. A hundred and fifty guests, at the Pearl on the Concho. We started with sweet and sour meatballs and served smoked brisket with all the fixings. He wrote that every guest loved the food, and that I was remarkable at getting it right.
Spot B
[OWNER: who a customer talks to when they book catering, and who is at the pans on the day] Our cooking is made from scratch and homestyle, the way I've cooked for years. The menu is posted every week, with the price on it. Before you sign with anybody, ask for the menu in writing and ask where it gets made. Read it, count the sides, and see what the price covers. If you want to see how we cook before you book, come to lunch. It's open to the public, eleven to one thirty. If you're planning something, call the kitchen at 325, 655, 1480.
4
How many dishes does a party menu need?
The useful one
▾
Anyone planning a party wants to know how many dishes is enough, and the answer is already on the weekly lunch menu. It costs nothing to give away and sends people to the Facebook page.
Spot A
A party menu needs two or three mains and three to five sides. That's the pattern on our weekly lunch menu, every day. Take a recent Tuesday, with fajitas, pulled pork asado, and shredded beef taquitos. Then charro beans, a hominy medley, and yellow Spanish rice. Wednesday was honey sriracha wings and Italian sausage and peppers. Then brown sugar carrots, green beans, and a summer squash medley. That's a full table.
Spot B
Pick mains that taste different from each other. A recent Monday was chicken fried steak with cream gravy, and lemon pepper chicken. One beef, one chicken, one fried, one seasoned. A recent Friday had two mains, cornmeal catfish and BBQ glazed sausage, and five sides, from hushpuppies to creamy coleslaw. Add a starch, like garlic herb mashed potatoes. Then give people vegetables with some color, like peas and carrots, roasted brussels sprouts, and collard greens. Now the plate looks like a meal.
Spot C
If the crowd is big, add a starter. For the hundred and fifty guests at that birthday at the Pearl on the Concho, it was sweet and sour meatballs. Then the smoked brisket and the fixings. Write your list and count your mains and your sides. If you land close to the pattern, you have a menu. The weekly menu is on our Facebook page, so go borrow it.
