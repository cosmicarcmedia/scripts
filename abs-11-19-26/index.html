Opened in the building boom, still here after the oil bust
The origin and the face
▾
The site tells a real survival story, a 1970s start, an oil bust that thinned out the competition and a storm that rebuilt the product line, and none of it is on camera. Putting James's face on it turns a hardware shop into a name people recall.
This shop opened in the seventies, when San Angelo was in a building boom. Back then we supplied hollow metal doors, restroom partitions and replacement garage doors to the builders putting this town up. That's how a shop gets started in a boom. You show up with what the builders need. [OWNER: how you came to run the company and how long you've been here] Then the eighties hit. The oil business went bust and building slowed way down. A lot of businesses in town didn't make it through. We did, on loyal customers and the referrals they sent us. Folks told their neighbors, and their neighbors called. So when the economy came back in the nineties, we were still standing. Then a big storm in 1995 blew out hundreds of skylights, and we expanded to help get this town put back together. We've kept adding to the product line since. Today that means steel doors for schools, the military and industry, architectural replacement windows, and wood finish garage doors backed with steel reinforcement. I'm James Wood, owner and manager here. Garage doors, entry doors, windows, skylights, all out of 18 West Highland Boulevard. We got through the eighties on word of mouth, and we still run this place the same way. If we did right by you, tell somebody. [OWNER: one customer who has called you for decades]

4
How long your garage door should last, and how to get there
The useful one
▾
This answers the question every homeowner eventually types into a search bar, straight from the shop's own FAQ, and ends on the phone number. It costs nothing to give away and it shows what the shop knows.
How long should a garage door last? Somewhere between fifteen and thirty years. That's a wide gap, and where you land depends on how often you use it and what Texas weather does to it. You can push yours toward the long end. Have the door adjusted twice a year. Lubricate the moving parts. Wipe off the dirt and grime before it turns into rust. That costs a lot less than a new door. Put it on the calendar, twice a year, so you don't have to remember. Now think about the heat. A closed garage can get hotter than the air outside, and everything you store in there gets hotter with it. If the sun hits your garage door all day, ask about an insulated one. Insulation keeps it cooler in the summer and warmer in the winter, and it saves some energy too. If you're replacing the door anyway, you've got choices. Steel, fiberglass, wood or aluminum. The one you pick changes how it handles the weather, and how it looks on your house. Wood gives you the look, steel gives you strength, and we can tell you which one fits your house. You can add an opener you run from your phone, or one with a battery backup so the door still works when the power's out. Call 325 655 9350 and we'll walk you through it.
