1
Dad bought his route on August 1, 1981
The origin and the face
▾
A family that has been in the trucks for forty five years is the opposite of a franchise van. This puts the founders and the sons on screen with a real start date and a real turning point.
Spot A
My name is Kevin Johnson, and my dad, Robert, started as a pest control technician in Bandera in 1974. In 1977 he came to the Junction area, after his company bought a pest control business in Menard. He became a certified applicator. Then the man who owned the company got sick. He offered his technicians the chance to take over their routes. On August 1, 1981, my dad bought his.
Spot B
My mother, Janice, got her own applicator license. She worked out in the field with my dad, and she ran the office. My brother Joshua and I grew up working with him. We rode along, we learned the trade, and then we both came back as full time technicians when the business needed us. [OWNER: your first memory of a route with your dad.]
Spot C
Our office has been at 1922 North Main since 2006. My wife, Sharlotte, joined the office in 1991. Joshua's wife, Samantha, joined in 2013. Mom and Dad are semi retired now, and my brother and I run more of it every year. We started with Junction, Sonora, and Menard, and we've grown out into the counties around them. It's Johnson's Pest Control, and it's a family business. If you live out here, there's a good chance we've been in your neighborhood a long time.
2
Scorpion country starts at the county line
The local anchor
▾
Hill Country homeowners fight scorpions, spiders and roaches in old ranch houses, and a customer review already says so. This puts the family in the middle of the problem people in Junction and Sonora have.
Spot B
If you've lived in the Hill Country a while, you know the feeling. You reach into a boot, or you pull a towel off the shelf, and something is in there. Scorpions, black widows, brown recluses, and the occasional tarantula. Fleas, big American roaches, fire ants, and centipedes. We've seen all of it. If it crawls and it lives in West Texas, it's on that list.
Spot A
We started in Junction, Sonora, and Menard. Over the years we added Rocksprings and the counties around them. We know these houses. Stone and limestone, old ranch homes, houses with gaps nobody has looked at in forty years. One customer wrote on Google that quarterly service cut the scorpions in her old ranch house way down. That's the kind of thing we hear.
Spot C
The most common scorpion in Texas is the bark scorpion. It can climb walls and ceilings, and it can squeeze through a very small gap. That's why a plan beats a one time spray. We offer monthly, quarterly, and seasonal service, and plenty in between. [OWNER: how often most ranch houses out here end up on a schedule.] Call 325 446 3462. Tell us what you're seeing and where. We'll set up a plan that fits your place.
3
Ask who's coming to your house, and who owns the company
The thing the big competitor will not say
▾
This is the one built to get forwarded. Anyone who has had a stranger in a branded van show up for a quarterly visit will feel it, and it names no competitor.
Spot C
Before you sign up for pest control anywhere, ask three questions. This is a service you invite into your home, so it matters who walks in. Who is going to show up at my house? Will it be the same person next time? And who owns the company?
Spot A
At Johnson's, the answer to the last one is easy. It's the Johnsons. My mom and dad started it in 1981, and my brother and I are in the trucks today. You can call 325 446 3462 and one of us, or someone in the office, picks up.
Spot B
Our work is route based. That means each customer is on a route, and we run the route. [OWNER: is it the same technician at your house every visit?] Customers tell us about it. One wrote that our technician Shane is the best of the best. Another said that when something turned up, somebody came out within a day or two. Another wrote that Shane plays the banjo. We'll take that one too. Ask the next company for a name. A name is somebody you can call back. Ask for that name before you let anyone near your house. It takes one phone call, and it tells you a lot.
4
Seven things to do tonight to keep scorpions out
The useful one
▾
Free, specific, seasonal help from a pest company that gives away the part homeowners can do themselves. It earns the follow and positions the family as the ones who know the work.
Spot A
Here's what to do around your house tonight. It costs almost nothing. First, move firewood and debris away from the walls. Scorpions hide in them during the day. Same goes for rocks, mulch, and the storage boxes in your garage. Second, trim any branches that touch or hang over your roof.
Spot B
Third, mow the grass that touches your foundation. Fourth, look at the weather stripping around your doors. If it's worn, replace it. Fifth, if your house is brick or stone, look for the little weep holes near the bottom. Plug them with steel wool or copper mesh, so a scorpion can't walk through. Sixth, check the screens. A torn screen is an open door.
Spot C
Seventh, shake out your boots and your shoes before you put them on. Use a flashlight when you walk outside after dark. Scorpions come out at night. Texas A and M's entomologists give this same advice. [OWNER: the one spot in a house you check first on every call.] Do all seven and you'll keep most of them out. That won't fix a bad problem. A real infestation takes a schedule and a professional. When you've done the list and they're still showing up, call us at 325 446 3462. We'll come take a look.
