1
Four generations in this trade before I ever opened these doors
The origin and the face
▾
J.D. Harper is the fourth generation of his family in funeral service. The story of his grandfather, the sale to a corporation, and the decision to start over is the reason a family can trust who is across the table.
Spot A
My great-grandfather ran a funeral home in Goldthwaite, Texas. That's where our family's part of this story begins. My grandfather, Billy Ray Harper, had a father-in-law who told him to look for work in San Angelo. So he came here and stayed in the trade. He built a reputation for service in this town, and he ended up owning a funeral home here. My dad and my uncle joined him later.
Spot B
So I grew up around it. I worked at the funeral home all through high school. [OWNER: one job you did there as a teenager] Then I went to Texas A&M. Since I was a teenager, I've watched families come through the door on the hardest day of their lives. I learned early what that asks of a person. It asks you to show up, and to stay steady for them. [OWNER: one thing you learned from your dad or your grandfather about sitting with a family]
Spot C
The funeral home my grandfather owned was eventually sold to a corporation. My wife, Ashley, and I decided to start our own. We built this place from the ground up, on Southland Boulevard. [OWNER: the year you opened the doors] Now it's my turn to continue the legacy of service that began with my family four generations ago. I want to help people carry the burden when they lose someone. Four generations. Goldthwaite, then San Angelo, and now me.

3
Ask who owns the funeral home
The thing the big competitor will not say
▾
J.D. can say what a corporate chain cannot: his grandfather's funeral home was sold to a corporation, and he started over. The three questions are fair to any funeral home and let families draw the line themselves.
Spot C
Before you choose a funeral home, ask who owns it. Plenty of funeral homes keep a family name on the sign long after the family is gone. I know how that happens. The funeral home my grandfather owned was sold to a corporation. Ours has my name on it, and I'm the one you will meet. [OWNER: confirm you personally meet every family]
Spot A
Then ask who is going to sit across the table from you. At Harper, every funeral director is listed by name on our website. Myrtis Acuavera. Jacob Perry. Pastor Brad Griffin. Shoshawna Bentley. And me. You can look us up before you ever call. You will know our names and our roles. You can see who we are, and you can decide if that's who you want beside you.
Spot B
Last, ask what happens to a pre-planned funeral if you move. Ours can move with you. If you plan ahead with us and relocate, we can send the plan to another funeral home. If you started a plan somewhere else, we can bring it here. The State of Texas publishes your rights on prepaid funerals at prepaidfunerals dot texas dot gov. Three questions. Who owns it, who will I meet, and can my plan move. Any funeral home should be able to answer all three. Those are fair questions for me too, so ask them.
4
What happens with Social Security when someone dies
The useful one
▾
Families rarely know whether they have to call Social Security. This explains what the funeral home reports and what the family still has to do, which answers a question the owner hears at the arrangement table.
Spot A
One of the first questions families ask us is about Social Security. Do I have to call them? In most cases, the funeral home reports the death for you. That is part of what we do. If it doesn't get reported, a family member needs to tell Social Security as soon as possible. You can't do that online. You have to call 1 800 772 1213.
Spot B
The next question is whether there's any money. There's a one-time death payment of $255. It goes to the surviving spouse, if that person was living with the one who died. If there's no surviving spouse, an eligible child can receive it. Other benefits can go to a widow or widower who is 60 or older, or to a child under 18. Social Security decides who qualifies, so ask them when you call.
Spot C
When you come in to arrange a service, bring what you have. The person's full legal name, their date and place of birth, and their parents' names, including their mother's maiden name. If they served in the military, bring their discharge papers. A recent photo helps too. [OWNER: one thing families often forget to bring] Don't wait until you've found everything. If you can't find something, come in anyway. We will help you work out the rest together.
