2
Come on in: a jiu jitsu school that fits around your day
The local anchor
▾
A schedule with a noon class every weekday and a 6 a.m. class three days a week tells downtown workers this gym is built for them, and the Midland visitor's review shows how a stranger gets treated. It is local and specific without leaning on anything about San Angelo's history.
Spot B
If you work downtown, you can train jiu jitsu on your lunch hour. We're at 133 East Concho Avenue, and there's a class at noon, Monday through Friday. One hour. Tuesday, Wednesday and Thursday, we're on the mat at six in the morning, before the workday starts. Monday, Tuesday and Thursday evenings, the kids train at five thirty and the adults follow at six thirty. Friday evening is competition class, kids at five and adults at six.

Saturday at noon and Sunday at two, we run open mat. We get visitors too. One fella who trains in Midland was in town for a weekend. He found us on Facebook, and we told him to come on in. Afterward he wrote that we met him at the door. That's how it works here. Whoever walks in off Concho Avenue gets met at the door.
Spot C
Conrad grew up in this town. Patrick and I grew up out here in West Texas too. A student named Cody wrote that he's grateful to have a high level program like this in his hometown of San Angelo. [OWNER: what that review meant to you] So come to the noon class sometime, and bring somebody from the office. Check the schedule page first, because times can change.
3
Three questions to ask before you pay any jiu jitsu school
The thing the big competitor will not say
▾
This is the sharpest of the four because it gives the viewer a checklist they can carry to any school in town. Each answer comes from the site's own promises: a free week, plain pricing, a fundamentals start, and a free year for San Angelo police. No one is named.
Spot C
Before you hand any jiu jitsu school your credit card, ask them three things. One. Can I train a full week first, before I pay anything? Here, the answer is yes. The first week is free. A free week of classes, so you find out if you like it before it costs you a dime.
Spot A
Two. Will you tell me what it costs, and what fees come with it, up front? We say it plainly. You will never feel blindsided about pricing, fees, or what you're going to learn. [OWNER: what a membership costs per month, and whether any other fees apply] Ask that question anywhere you visit. You'll learn a lot from how fast they answer, and how much they leave out.
Spot B
Three. Where does a beginner start? Here, adults spend their first three months in fundamentals, then move up to the advanced class. You don't have to buy a gi to find out if this is for you. Show up in comfortable workout clothes. We have everything else. The people who started this place are the ones on the mat. Conrad, Patrick and me. And one more thing, because it says a lot about who we are. San Angelo police officers train with us free for a full year. [OWNER: why you made that offer to San Angelo police] Ask the next school if they'd do the same.
4
Gi or no gi? How to pick your first class
The useful one
▾
Gi or no gi is the first question every beginner and every parent asks, and the site answers it in pieces. This turns those pieces into one straight answer with the real class times, which saves a nervous first-timer a phone call and shows the school is easy to start with.
Spot A
The first question every beginner asks is gi or no gi. Here's the short version. The gi is the uniform, the jacket and pants. You learn grips, you learn control, and you learn the manners of the mat. No gi is shorts and a rash guard, stretchy athletic clothes. There's no jacket to grab, so it moves faster, and you have to think quicker. Plenty of people feel no gi is closer to a real situation.
Spot B
Which one should you start in? Whichever fits your week. Monday at five thirty is adult fundamentals in the gi. Tuesday at five thirty is adult fundamentals no gi. Kids work the same way. Gi kids on Monday, no gi kids Tuesday and Thursday, all at five thirty. And our littles, ages three to five, train Tuesday and Thursday at five. That's thirty minutes of games that build balance, coordination and listening. Times change sometimes, so check the schedule page before you come.
Spot C
My advice is to try both during your free week. See which one you want to come back to. You don't need to buy anything first. Comfortable workout clothes and a positive attitude is all it takes to start. Walk in, tell us it's your first day, and we'll point you to the right mat.
