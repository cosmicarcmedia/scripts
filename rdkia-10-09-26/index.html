1
I could have sold it
The origin and the face
▾
The agency has sat on Main Street since 1942, and almost nobody in Junction knows the owner took it over in her early twenties and chose to keep it. This puts a face and a decision behind the name on the door.
Spot A
I came home to Junction after college in 2009 and went to work at my family's insurance agency. It had been on Main Street since 1942. Then there was a change in who would lead it. And the question on the table was a simple one. Do we sell this place, or does somebody keep it going. I was young, and I didn't have some big plan. I did have policyholders who'd been with us for decades. I wasn't going to hand them off to a stranger.
Spot B
So I kept it. I hired good people, and those first two years the agency grew faster than I had any right to expect. Today Dalia and Sam are agents here with me. I've sat on the Junction chamber for ten years. I served on the Economic Development Board. When this town does well, the agency does well. So I show up for both.
Spot C
My name is Jessica Gardner, and this is R.D. Kothmann Insurance Agency, at 1701 Main Street. [OWNER: who R.D. Kothmann was, and how he's tied to your family.] We're still locally owned, and we're still independent. That means I don't work for one insurance company. The door is on Main Street.
2
Junction knows what the Llano can do
The local anchor
▾
Everyone in Junction remembers October 2018, so opening on the river makes this the one script only a Junction agency could make, and it moves straight to the flood question that sells a policy.
Spot B
In October of 2018, the Llano River came up past thirty feet in Junction. Flood stage here is sixteen. The power went out across the whole town. DPS and our police were pulling people out of the water. If you live here, you remember where you were that Monday.
Spot A
Here's the question I ask people now. Do you have flood coverage? A standard homeowners policy generally leaves flood out. Flood is its own policy, and it usually has a waiting period before it starts. So the day the river is rising is too late to ask about it. The time to ask is on a dry, quiet afternoon like today. If the answer is no, that's a fair thing to fix. If the answer is yes, good, and let's make sure it covers what you think it covers.
Spot C
We write flood coverage here in Junction and across Kimble County, along with homes, farms, and ranches. I'm at 1701 Main Street. Bring me your address, and we'll look at it together. [OWNER: a Junction street or low water crossing you watch when the river comes up.] Quotes are free, and nobody's going to pressure you. You can look at the numbers, go home, and think it over. The river will do what it does, and your policy should already be waiting.
3
Who does your insurance agent work for?
The thing the big competitor will not say
▾
This is the one built to get forwarded. It asks a question that makes a customer of a captive agent uncomfortable, and it names no competitor and no company.
Spot C
Here's a question to ask whoever sells you insurance. Who do you work for? Some agents work for one insurance company. That company sets the price, the coverage, and the rules, and that's all they can show you. If their company happens to be high on your house, then the high price is the only price you'll see.
Spot A
We're independent. I can look at [OWNER: how many companies you write with] different companies for the same house, the same truck, or the same ranch, and show you where they land side by side. Rates move at renewal. When yours moves, you're allowed to ask what else is out there.
Spot B
Ask about that before you sign anything, anywhere. That goes for us too. A good agent will like the question. Ask what happens to your price next year. Ask who answers the phone when you have a claim. Here, it's somebody who knows your name. It might be me, or Dalia, or Sam. You can walk in, sit down, and ask us to explain any line of it. We've been on Main Street in Junction since 1942. You don't have to wonder where we are, and nobody has to put you on hold to find out who handles your policy.
4
Check these three things on your home policy tonight
The useful one
▾
Free, specific help a homeowner can use before ever talking to an agent. It earns the follow and shows the agency reads the fine print for people.
Spot A
Pull out your homeowners policy tonight and check three things. Take ten minutes. It's worth it. You don't need an agent for this part. The first one is whether it pays replacement cost or actual cash value. Replacement cost pays to rebuild with new materials. Actual cash value takes off for age. On a roof, that can be a big number.
Spot B
Second, find your wind and hail deductible. Some policies set it as a percentage of your home's value. Others use a flat dollar amount. Check which kind yours is. On a three hundred thousand dollar house, a one percent deductible is three thousand dollars. Know that number before the storm, because you'll be the one paying it. A lot of people find out at the worst possible time.
Spot C
Third, look at the dwelling limit. That's the most the policy will pay to rebuild your house. Compare it to what it would cost to build today. Building costs have climbed, and a lot of limits haven't kept up. [OWNER: one thing you see in Kimble County policies that people usually miss.] If you'd like a second set of eyes, bring it by 1701 Main Street. Jess, Dalia, or Sam will read it through with you. No charge to look.
