The station wagon, the mouse powder, and the portrait in the foyer
The origin and the face

2
The San Angelo yard calendar
The local anchor
▾
Everybody in town has the same dirt, the same wind, and the same summer. A month by month calendar for local grass, taken from the BES-TEX FAQ, gives viewers a reason to follow the account and a reason to walk in.
Here's a yard calendar for San Angelo, straight from the shelves at BES-TEX. Pre-emergent goes down three times a year, in February, May, and early September. February is the big one, so get it down on time. Feed the lawn in late spring, April through June, and again in the early fall, September and October. Skip the fertilizer in the middle of summer, because the heat is hard enough on the grass already. Water about one inch a week, rain included. Water deeply but infrequently, early in the morning. Mow Bermuda at one to two inches and St. Augustine at three to four. Never take off more than a third of the blade at once. Brown spots can mean drought, chinch bugs in St. Augustine, or brown patch fungus. Armyworms cause sudden brown patches, usually in late summer. Come in with a picture, and we'll help you figure out which one you have. Aerate in March and April, or in September and October. We rent core aerators, power rakes, and sod cutters, so you don't have to buy one for a weekend. If you want to know what's in your soil, send a sample to Texas A&M AgriLife Extension, and come see us when the results come back. Every bit of this is on our website, and it's written for West Texas conditions. Call 325-653-1300 or stop by 4512 Adobe Drive.

4
Your lawn is asleep. Here's the winter list.
The useful one
▾
It is almost December, so this is the question every homeowner has right now. It comes straight from the BES-TEX Weekly Insight wrap-up from October 25, and it ends with a reason to bring equipment in during the slow season.
Your grass just went dormant. Here's what to do about it, and what to leave alone. Leave the fertilizer in the bag. Fertilizer on brown Bermuda or St. Augustine doesn't help the lawn. It feeds winter weeds. Water your trees. They still use water with no leaves on them. If we go weeks without rain, give them a deep, slow soak. Watch for henbit and rescuegrass. They show up in winter, and they're easiest to control while they're small. Mark February on the calendar. That's pre-emergent month. Get it down on time, and you'll have a lot fewer weeds when the grass wakes up. Putting a mower away for the winter? Change the oil, clean the deck, and drain the old fuel. Store it dry and ventilated. We recommend ethanol-free VP Fuel, so the gas doesn't break down while it sits. Last thing, take your mower, trimmer, or blower to the shop now. Winter is our slow season, and the spring rush is coming. We service the brands we sell, and you can drop it off at 4512 Adobe Drive, Monday through Friday. Your grass is back in February. Our Weekly Insight wrapped up the season on October 25, and the next issue comes out on February 28. Call 325-653-1300 with any questions.
