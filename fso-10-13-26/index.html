1
From a pest control company to wild quail, since 1999
The origin and the face
▾
Hunters book an outfitter on trust in the two people running it. This tells them who Mike is, why he started, and that Monica's cooking is in a book, three things the site mentions and no video has ever shown.
Spot A
In 1999, I started First Shot Outfitters in Coleman County. Before that I worked in the oil business after high school, and later I owned a pest control company, which I sold. Quail hunting is my first love. We were known first for wild bobwhite quail and blue quail. Those are one hundred percent wild birds, and we run with eight or more bird dogs on the truck. [OWNER: the moment you knew it was going to work]
Spot B
Today we hunt a lot more than quail. Whitetail, turkey, dove, ducks, sandhill cranes, and hogs. But a lot of the repeat business is still quail. A book called The World's Best Shoots picked us for the best wild quail hunting in the world. And another one, Grazing Across Texas, put six of my wife Monica's recipes in print. It has photos of our operation, too. Our job is to know where the birds and the deer are, and put you in the middle of it.
Spot C
I'm Mike Wyatt. Monica runs the kitchen. Most likely, one of us is personally taking care of your group. Every hunt is free roaming, one hundred percent wild, and there are no high fences. Hunting is our passion, and it's how we make our living. [OWNER: the one thing a group says to you on the last morning] That's what twenty seven years of hunts in Coleman County turned into.
2
Seventy five miles northeast of San Angelo
The local anchor
▾
A San Angelo hunter may not know there is an outfitter hunting their own county line. The site's own map puts Coleman seventy five miles northeast and lists Tom Green County among its ranches. This puts the whole map in one video.
Spot C
We're in Coleman County, seventy five miles northeast of San Angelo. That's thirty five miles northwest of Brownwood and forty five miles southeast of Abilene. The ranches we hunt are native pasture, rolling grassy plains, mesquite flats, and a few prickly pear patches. There are more than seventy two freshwater lakes out there too. And there are no high fences.
Spot A
Our ranches add up to more than six hundred thousand acres across sixteen counties. That includes Tom Green County, where San Angelo sits, plus Irion, Concho, Schleicher, and Sterling. We have four lodges. One is a remodeled ranch house on a twenty thousand acre working ranch outside Garden City. One is on the back side of Santa Anna Mountain. The biggest one sleeps sixty, right in the middle of the dove fields. The Coleman Log Lodge sleeps eight and has a floor to ceiling wood burning fireplace. Book it for eight and you have the whole lodge to yourselves.
Spot B
If you fly in, it's Dallas to Abilene, then about forty five minutes to the lodge. If you fly private, the Coleman airport is about fifteen minutes away. [OWNER: the farthest a group has traveled for a hunt with you] And a heads up. Verizon doesn't work well out here. Most of the other carriers do. [OWNER: your favorite spot on the ranch, and why]
3
What a Texas hunt costs and what it leaves out
The thing the big competitor will not say
▾
Hunters shopping outfitters online mostly see a trophy photo and a Contact Us button. First Shot publishes what is included, what is not, the deposit, the tip and how to pay. Saying that out loud draws a fair line without naming anyone.
Spot B
Before you book a Texas hunt with anybody, ask what the price leaves out. Here's what ours leaves out. Meat processing and taxidermy aren't included. We have a local processor, and we'll share a favorite taxidermist's number. We'll help you get your game and your trophy to them. You pay them directly. Shells aren't included either, or alcohol, or airport pickup. Pickup from the Abilene airport is priced per vehicle, and each vehicle carries three or four hunters.
Spot C
Here's what is included. Lodging at one of our four lodges. Ranch style meals and homemade desserts. Professional guides with all terrain rigs, and iced drinks and snacks on the truck. On a quail hunt, your birds come back processed, in a cooler bag, with an orange cap. A quail hunt takes at least three hunters, three to a truck.
Spot A
A few more things most outfitters save for the phone call. We take a fifty percent deposit when you book, and it isn't refundable. The balance is due on or before you arrive, by check or cash. We don't take credit cards. Tips are customary at twenty percent or more, split between your guide and the cook. You'll know every line of that before you send a deposit.
4
What to pack for a free range Texas hunt
The useful one
▾
This is the packing list from the outfitter's own Q&A page, turned into something a hunter can hear in a minute. It costs nothing to give away and shows a new hunter exactly how the hunt will run.
Spot A
If you're hunting with us this season, start with the rifle. Have it sighted in before you get here. For deer and hogs, wash your camo in scent free detergent. For turkey, wear camo from head to toe, with gloves and a face mask, plus layers. Bring a kickstand turkey vest or a turkey lounger chair. Snake guards are a good idea on most hunts.
Spot B
Quail hunters, bring an orange cap, and wear some orange on your shirt too. For turkey loads, a TSS number seven, eight, and nine blend is great, but number five through nine shot by itself works fine. For dove, some experts like a modified choke, depending on the wind, the surroundings, and the bird. Quail days run on a schedule. A sunrise hunt, a break for lunch around noon, then back out around two thirty until you hit your limit or the sun goes down.
Spot C
Check in is after five, so we have time to get the lodge ready for you. Dinner is served the night you arrive, between seven and eight. On a guided hunt you ride with your guide to the hunting areas. The lodge doesn't provide alcohol, so bring your own if you want it. Cell service is thin out here. Verizon doesn't work well, though most carriers do. Look up the license rules on the Texas Parks and Wildlife website before you come. [OWNER: the one thing hunters forget most often]
