2
San Angelo's rodeo started as a stock show on a polo field
The local anchor
▾
PK is a past president of the Stock Show and Rodeo. Telling its real history, with the dates, makes him the person people in San Angelo already trust to tell it.
Spot B
The San Angelo Stock Show started in 1932, in the middle of the Great Depression. A man named Cub Deal, who ran the Chamber of Commerce, thought the town would do better with a spring stock show than a fair. The first one was held on the Polo Field, February 29 through March 2. Fifteen Texas counties took part, and the auctions brought in about ten thousand dollars.
Spot C
In 1934 it moved to the fairgrounds, and they added a little rodeo. That year twenty one counties took part, twelve hundred head of livestock were shown, and the auctions brought in twelve thousand five hundred dollars. A fire in 1943 destroyed most of the facilities. The rodeo went professional in 1955. The Coliseum was finished in 1959. The association calls it one of the largest livestock shows in the nation.
Spot A
This year the San Angelo Stock Show and Rodeo was named to the 2026 class of the ProRodeo Hall of Fame. The executive director of the rodeo called it the biggest economic impact event for the city of San Angelo. I served as president of that association. Ask your agent how many companies they can shop for you

4
The first hour after a fender bender
The useful one
▾
Everyone in San Angelo has either had a minor wreck or will. A plain checklist for the first hour is the kind of post that gets saved and shared, and it puts the agency's phone number in the right hands.
Spot B
If you're in a fender bender, here's what to do in the first hour. First, check on everybody. If anyone is hurt, call 911. Then move the cars out of traffic if you can do it safely. Call the police and get a report number.
Spot C
Second, take photos. Take pictures of both cars, the license plates, the road, and anything that's broken. Get the other driver's name, phone number, and insurance information. Write down the time and the street. Don't argue about whose fault it was, and don't guess in front of the other driver. Be polite, and leave the question of fault to the insurance companies. Even if you feel fine, get checked out if your neck or back starts to hurt later.
Spot A
Third, call your insurance. Call your agent, or your company's claims line, as soon as you can. Tell them what happened, and send them the photos. Keep the police report number and every receipt in one place. Be careful what you post about it online. If you don't know where to start, you can call us at 325 224 8000 and we'll point you the right way. [OWNER: one thing you tell customers on the phone right after a wreck]
