2
Dig a hole in the Concho Valley and you'll meet caliche
The local anchor
▾
Caliche, nineteen inches of rain and a March that cannot make up its mind are things every San Angelo yard owner has already lived through, so the video reads as someone who knows this exact ground.
Spot B
Dig a hole anywhere in the Concho Valley and sooner or later you hit caliche. It blocks drainage, and it fights back when a root tries to get through. San Angelo gets about nineteen inches of rain a year, about half the Texas average. So the rain you do get has to drain somewhere, and caliche makes that hard. We plan for the caliche first, before anything goes in the ground.
Spot A
We work all over town, from Knickerbocker Road to Sherwood Way to Loop 306. Houses, and oil sector offices, medical campuses, retail centers and office parks. Different yards, same ground. A designer from here knows the soil, which natives hold up and what the weather does, because we're out in it too. Heat is the other one. Pavers and concrete swell and shrink between a summer high and a winter cold snap, so the base and the joints get extra attention. [OWNER: the worst caliche you ever dug through]
Spot C
Then there is March. The daffodils bloom. The redbuds flirt. Then March throws a 29 degree morning and a 40 mile an hour wind at you. In the fall we have pansies, stock and mums. Come see what's in the greenhouse. We're on South Bryant Boulevard. Ask us what survives a San Angelo summer. Happy planting.
3
Ask who's licensed to plant your yard
The thing the big competitor will not say
▾
Each question has an answer the shop can give from its own licenses and its own team, and the line about mowing shows the kind of honesty customers remember.
Spot C
Here is something most people never ask a landscaper. Who's licensed? I hold a Texas irrigator's license, a commercial pesticide applicator license and a state nursery license. Then ask if the person who designs your yard is the person who builds it. Here, one team handles the whole project in house, starting with a look at your site, so nothing falls through the cracks.
Spot A
Ask if the plant was grown for West Texas. We carry a lot of Texas natives and plants that suit this climate, and a whole greenhouse of succulents. [OWNER: how much of what's on the benches you grow yourself] Slide any plant out of its pot and look at the roots. A plant that has sat in its pot too long starts circling its own roots. Ask how big it will get, and where it will be in three years.
Spot B
Ask whether anyone will try to sell you more than you need. One customer wrote that our people answer every question and never push. The same customer called our collection of Texas natives insane, and said some are hard to find. And here's one more thing. We don't do regular residential maintenance. No mowing, no leaf cleanup. We design it and we install it, and we say so up front. Ask the same things anywhere you shop for plants, and ask us too.
4
Fill a hole with water before you buy a tree
The useful one
▾
A free test any homeowner can run in ten minutes, built on how caliche behaves, and it ends with the shop as the place to bring the answer.
Spot A
Before you buy a single tree, dig one hole. Dig it right where the tree is going. Fill it with water and walk away. All it takes is a shovel, a hose and a free afternoon. This is the cheapest test in gardening. Caliche is the hard layer under a lot of San Angelo yards, and you won't know it's there until you dig.
Spot B
Check on it in [OWNER: how long water should take to drain]. If the water's gone, the soil is draining and you're in good shape. If it's still sitting there, you've met caliche, and a tree set in a hole that holds water can drown. Caliche blocks drainage and resists roots. That's when you plan around it, before the tree goes in. Break through it, move the spot, or choose a plant that can live with it.
Spot C
Then choose plants that grew up in dry country. Texas natives and Desert Southwest plants handle nineteen inches of rain better than most. Rock, boulder and hardscape can fill in the rest. When it goes in, water it with drip. Our irrigation designs use pressure zones and drip emitters, so you get even coverage without wasting water. Tell us what you found in that hole, and we'll tell you what makes it here. Happy planting.
