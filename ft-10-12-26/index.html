The local anchor
▾

3
Three questions to ask any carrier before you hand over a load
The thing the big competitor will not say
▾
Shippers and brokers already worry about who answers when a load runs late. These three questions let them test any carrier, and a one-truck family outfit can answer all three with a name and a phone number.
Spot A
Before you hand a load to any carrier, ask three questions. First one. Who do I call when it's running late, and do they pick up? Here, the numbers are on our website, with a name next to each one. Dispatch is open twenty four seven. No call center. No phone tree. And the voice that quotes your rate is the voice you hear at the dock.
Spot B
Second question. Who checks the load? Every strap checked. Every tarp secured. We haul steel, pipe, and oilfield equipment, so we use chains, straps, corner protectors, and dunnage. Open deck freight gets full tarping, strapping, and chain-down. Ask any carrier to walk you through how they'd secure your freight. If the answer is vague, you've learned something.
Spot C
Third question. Can I see your paperwork? Our USDOT number is 4112825, and our MC number is 1571259. You can look both up yourself. A certificate of insurance is available on request. Rates come with no hidden fees. And you get live updates from pickup to drop, so you always know where your freight is. When we accept a load, we deliver it. Weather, traffic, or tight timelines, we find a way to make it work. Most quotes go out within the hour, and for an urgent load you call and get a number right away. [OWNER: what you say to a broker who's comparing you against a bigger carrier]
4
Five things to have ready before you ask for a flatbed quote
The useful one
▾
Anyone shipping steel, pipe or equipment for the first time does not know what a carrier needs. A short list saves them a round of phone calls, and it matches the quote form on the site.
Spot C
If you've never shipped on a flatbed, the quote starts with a few questions. Have these five answers ready, and you'll get a number fast. One. What is it? Steel, pipe, oilfield equipment, construction materials, machinery. Two. What does it weigh? Weight decides whether a load is legal on one truck. A 53 foot aluminum flatbed handles standard legal loads up to forty eight thousand pounds.
Spot A
Three. Does it need a tarp? Say so up front, and say what size. Lumber, trusses, and building products usually want covering. We carry four foot and six foot tarps. Four. Where does it pick up, and where does it go? If it's going to a drill site, tell us. Lease roads change what we need to know. Include a phone number for whoever is receiving the load.
Spot B
Five. When does it need to move? A pickup date up front saves a phone call. That's the whole list, and it's what our quote form asks too. Send it in, and most quotes go out within the hour during business hours. If it's urgent, skip the form and call. And if you're a broker, it's the same list. We work with brokers and shippers alike. Put those five on a sticky note by the phone.
