The scripts

A first pass. I made some assumptions about your business to write these. On a shoot we'd dial them in to your goals.

1
Everywhere I went, people said we miss your pizza
The origin and the face
▾
Customers already tell this story for him. Hearing Steve say it himself, along with the partners behind the counter, turns a pizza parlor into people downtown can root for.
Spot B
Everywhere I went, somebody said the same thing. We miss your pizza. I heard it so many times that I finally did something about it. I'm Steve Stango, and I brought the pizza back downtown. It gave me something to do, too. I will admit that.
Spot C
This pizza has a history. It grew out of the pizza we first made at Stango's Coffee and Ice Cream, the shop I owned downtown before this one. [OWNER: the year you started making it and where the recipe came from] New York style. Thin, crispy, simple, and built to fold. Simple recipes, quality ingredients, nothing overcomplicated. The dough and the sauce get made fresh. And every sixteen inch pizza is stretched by hand when you order it.
Spot A
I didn't do this by myself. Jen Addona is my partner, and she runs operations, events, and marketing. She ran her own restaurant in Bronte before she joined me. And I made some of our longtime employees partners too. [OWNER: what that means for them, in your words] The reviews keep saying the same things. Friendly staff. A family feel. One person wrote that the building felt like home. I will take that. It's laid back and family friendly, and you can eat in the dining room or out back in the courtyard. Book your next party with us. I hope everybody comes back. We're at 208 South Oakes, downtown, and we're glad you're here.
2
What happens in our courtyard on Fridays
The local anchor
▾
A weekly jam session is a reason for people to come back, bring a friend, and film something. The page shows the owner explaining it in his own words, with the bigger Downtown Music Showcase date attached.
Spot C
Every Friday at six, our courtyard on Oakes Street turns into a jam session. It runs from six to eight, weather permitting. People show up with an instrument, a voice, a song, a riff, or just a groove. Every style is welcome, and every skill level. When I brought the pizza back downtown, I told people we would have a little music venue out back. That was the plan from the start. Pizza and live music in the same place.
Spot A
Zo Rachel hosts it, and there is sound on the stage. If you have never played in front of anybody, a jam is a friendly place to start. If you just want to listen, grab a table out back. Or eat in the dining room and walk out when the music starts. We are open eleven to nine on Fridays, so come early, get dinner, and stay for the jam. Bring the kids too. We have an old style jukebox inside, and the kids love it.
Spot B
We're at 208 South Oakes, downtown, one street over from Chadbourne. There's a bigger music day coming too. The Downtown Music Showcase is set for Saturday, April 24th, 2027. It is one day, with venues across downtown hosting San Angelo area musicians. [OWNER: what part Stango's plays in the showcase] The idea is simple. Support musicians. So come out on a Friday. Bring a friend who plays something, and eat a slice first.
3
Ask who stretched your pizza
The thing the big competitor will not say
▾
Two questions anyone can ask a big chain, answered with what the shop already does: fresh dough, hand stretched to order, and partners who have been here for years. It lands without naming anybody.
Spot A
When you order pizza from a big chain, ask two questions. Was this dough made today? And did a person stretch it by hand after I ordered? Here, the answer to both is yes. Our dough and our sauce get made fresh. Every sixteen inch pizza is hand stretched to order.
Spot B
A New York pizza is meant to be thin and crispy and fold in your hand. We keep the recipes simple on purpose, with quality ingredients and nothing overcomplicated. And you don't have to buy a whole pizza. A slice here is a quarter of a sixteen inch pizza, and we sell slices all day. Tuesday through Friday, eleven to two, there is a lunch slice special. Students and seniors get a better price on it. The soft drinks come with free refills. And the Caesar comes with croutons and bread we make ourselves.
Spot C
Then ask who owns the place. Here it's me and Jen Addona, and you can find us. A few people who have worked here a long time own a piece of it too. I'm proud of that. So next time you're deciding where to get dinner, ask where the dough came from. We're locally owned, family friendly, and at 208 South Oakes. Then come see how we do it.
4
Calzone or stromboli, here is the difference
The useful one
▾
A plain explainer built from the shop's own menu. It costs nothing to give away, and it puts the rolls, the calzone, and the gluten free pizza in front of people who are still deciding what to order.
Spot B
Calzone or stromboli. Here is the difference, using what is on our menu. A calzone gets folded over into a half moon. Ours is mozzarella and ricotta, with marinara on the side, and you can add toppings. A stromboli gets rolled up like a log. Ours is mozzarella, with marinara for dipping. Or you can get one with pepperoni and Italian sausage rolled inside.
Spot C
Then there are the pizza rolls, which are their own thing. They come four to an order, made with pepperoni and mozzarella. We also make a jalapeno popper roll, with cream cheese, jalapeno, and bacon. Both come with marinara. Cheese sticks come ten to an order, with marinara. Add pepperoni and jalapenos and you have loaded sticks. Garlic breadsticks come ten to an order too. If you can't decide, the sampler has two of each.
Spot A
If you came for pizza, we have that too, thin and New York style. By the slice, it is cheese or pepperoni, all day. We also make a ten inch cauliflower crust pizza that is gluten free. We have salads, house or Caesar, large or small. And for dessert, we have cannoli and a New York style cheesecake. So if you are standing there looking at the menu, now you know.
