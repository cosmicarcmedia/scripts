4
How to keep dust down in a dry San Angelo house
Useful customer education
▾
Dust is the one cleaning problem every San Angelo homeowner shares, and the fixes cost nothing to give away. Everything here comes from the company's own blog post on dust-proofing a dry-climate home, and it earns the follow from anyone sweeping the same shelf twice a week.
Spot A
Want to cut the dust in your house? Start with your air filter. A standard HVAC filter may catch only the large particles. A HEPA filter can capture up to 99.97 percent of particles as small as 0.3 microns, including dust, pollen and pet dander. Change your HVAC filters every two to three months, and more often in the windy seasons.
Spot B
Clutter is a dust magnet, because every object is one more surface for dust to land on. Soft furnishings hold dust too, like curtains, rugs and upholstery. In dry air, static makes fabric hold on to it, so tightly woven and synthetic fibers trap less. Hardwood and tile floors clean more thoroughly than wall to wall carpet.
Spot C
Baseboards, ceiling fans, air vents and the space behind big furniture collect the dust nobody sees. The American Lung Association says cleaning those hard to reach spots regularly improves indoor air quality and cuts allergens. Seal the gaps. Weatherstripping and caulk around doors and windows keep outdoor dust from drifting in, and it's worth checking for new gaps after storms or high winds. Keep the windows closed on windy and dusty days, and open them on calm days or after a rain, when dust levels are lowest. If dust still wins, that's what a regular cleaning schedule is for.
