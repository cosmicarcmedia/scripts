1
I have a marketing degree, and I build houses
The origin and the face
▾
Most people picking a builder never learn that this one studied marketing, quit the desk, and learned the trade riding job sites with his father-in-law. That path is the reason a customer can trust the man drawing their house.
Spot A
I have a marketing degree, and I spend my days on a job site. I graduated from Midwestern State in Wichita Falls in 2012. That's where I met my wife, Lindsey, on the tennis team. I tried the office life, and working nine to five behind a desk just wasn't for me. Her dad, Eddie Holcomb, is a custom builder in the Wichita Falls area. So I started riding job sites with him.
Spot B
Eddie gave me three rules, and I still build by all of them. Keep getting better at everything you do. Make every house as energy efficient as you can. And work with a team of top quality subcontractors. In 2013 I moved back home to San Angelo and started building homes with him. [OWNER: the first job Eddie put you on, in one sentence]
Spot C
Somewhere along the way, Lindsey and I bought land and built two houses at once. I was working for a supply company back then. We never lived in the first one. It sold before we finished it. The second one sold not long after. A few years of that, and I formed my own company to put my own ideas on the street. That's Circle M. [OWNER: where the name Circle M came from] I'm happy with how far it's come, and I know we're still working toward our full potential.
2
Every spring, San Angelo can walk through our houses
The local anchor
▾
The Parade of Homes is the one week a year when strangers walk through a builder's work. Putting the Enclave Court home and the neighborhood job list in Chad's own voice tells the town he is a local who can be seen.
Spot B
Every spring, San Angelo gets to walk through brand new houses without an appointment. That's the Home Builders Association's Parade of Homes. It's one weekend, and then it's over. In 2025 it was eleven homes from seven builders. Mine was on Enclave Court. The builders give the tour themselves, and sometimes the subs are standing right there. You can ask your questions to the people who built it.
Spot A
I'm on the board of the San Angelo HBA, and I was born and raised in this town. [OWNER: the neighborhood you grew up in] My job list reads like a map of San Angelo. A Bentwood add-on. A remodel in Santa Rita. A pool house. A bathroom remodel. Add-ons, remodels, and whole custom homes, all over town. It's the town I grew up in, and I want it built well.
Spot C
Part of what the Parade brings in goes to Sleep in Heavenly Peace, the group that builds beds for kids here. Here's the other thing I think about. San Angelo runs short of skilled workers, and every house in this town depends on them. That's why I back Howard College building up trades training. So if you're thinking about building, go walk the Parade. Look at the work, shake the hand that built it, and tell me what you'd change.
3
Who draws your house? Ask before you sign.
The thing the big competitor will not say
▾
It is a question a customer can take to any builder, and the honest answer separates a catalog of plans from a house drawn from your own sketch. It makes Chad's in-house design the reason to call him first.
Spot C
Before you sign with any builder, ask one question. Who draws my house? A builder who sells the same few plans over and over can't stop and redraw one for you. That's just how their business is set up. So listen to the answer. If it's "pick one of our plans," you've learned what you needed to know. If it's "bring me your ideas," keep talking.
Spot A
Here, I draw it. We use architectural design software, and we draw the blueprints in house. You can show up with a sketch on a piece of paper, or a Pinterest board. I spend plenty of time on Pinterest and Houzz myself, so go ahead. I give you feedback right then. Then you get a 3D rendering, so you can see the house before a nail goes in. And then a full working set of blueprints. Clean lines, lighter colors, wood and stucco and rock and brick on the outside. If you like it, we draw it.
Spot B
The second thing to ask is who you'll be talking to. The phone number on my website is listed under my name. The email address starts with Chad. My goal is simple. The best product at a fair price. [OWNER: how fast you call people back] I want people to walk through the front door and say wow. Ask every builder on your list these two questions. Then ask me.
4
Your house is an Igloo cooler with windows and doors
The useful one
▾
It is a lesson from his mentor and a picture of his own, and it gives a homeowner one plain way to judge any builder on energy efficiency. It costs nothing to give away and shows how he thinks.
Spot A
The man who taught me to build, Eddie Holcomb, taught me to make every house as energy efficient as I can. The picture I use for it is simple. Your house is an Igloo cooler with windows and doors. Think about why a cooler keeps ice for days in July. Thick walls, and a lid that seals. That's the whole trick.
Spot B
Now think about where the ice gets lost. It's the lid and the seams. In a house, that's your windows, your doors, and every gap where a pipe or a wire goes through the wall. You can have the best finishes in town. If those spots leak, you're paying to cool the backyard. Nobody falls in love with a window seal on a tour. They fall in love with the kitchen. Ask about the seal anyway.
Spot C
So when you talk to a builder about energy efficiency, skip the buzzwords. Ask what goes in the walls. Ask what kind of windows they use. Ask how they seal the gaps. You don't have to know the right answer yourself. You just have to hear whether they have one. Every house I build, I try to make as energy efficient as I can. [OWNER: one thing you do on every house to find the leaks before the drywall goes up] In a West Texas summer, you'll see the answer on your electric bill.
