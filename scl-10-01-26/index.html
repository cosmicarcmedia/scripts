4
Why your lawn looks patchy even though you mow every week
The useful one
▾
It costs you nothing to give away and comes straight from your own showcase page on running mowing, fertilization, pre-emergent and irrigation together, so homeowners get a checklist they will save.
Spot B
You mow every week, and the lawn still looks patchy. Mowing and edging are the part everybody sees. Weed prevention, fertilizer, and irrigation decide how it grows. A lawn does best when all four are managed together. Start with the weeds. Pre-emergent keeps them from coming up, so it has to go down before you see a single one. That's what properly timed means. [OWNER: the month pre-emergent goes down in San Angelo] Once they're up, you're chasing them. Seasonal treatments are how we stay ahead of that.
Spot C
Then there's food. A lawn needs nutrients to grow, and that takes regular fertilization. Ask whoever takes care of yours when it was last fertilized. And water. Is your system giving every part of the lawn the coverage it needs? Walk the yard and look for the spots it misses. Check the corners and along the walk. If the ground is packed hard, water and fertilizer have a hard time getting down. Aeration relieves that compaction. Roots grow better, and nutrients absorb better.
Spot A
Put all four together and you get a healthier lawn and a much more consistent look. If somebody is only mowing, that's your gap. On the yards I maintain, I handle all four. If the lawn is thin or damaged enough, it needs a fresh start. That means preparing the area and laying new sod. I'll tell you which one your lawn needs.
