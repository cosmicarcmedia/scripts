3
Ask for the license, the insurance certificate and the payment schedule before you sign
The thing the big competitor will not say
▾
The About page is already a checklist a customer can hold any contractor to, and the payment policy is the part few contractors will say out loud. Reading it on camera gives viewers a list worth forwarding and shows Angelo Elite passing its own test.
Spot C
Before you sign with any contractor, ask for the license number. Ours is 4135, and we're licensed with the City of San Angelo. Then ask for a certificate of insurance. If a company says it's self insured, don't let that fool you. That just means they have no insurance. Ask for three references too, and call them. Ask each one what happened when the crew found something unexpected. [OWNER: a reference you're proud of, and what they'd say]
Spot A
Then ask for a line item estimate. Ours is free. It's broken down into labor, materials, subcontractor allowances, and a material selection allowance. From that estimate to a detailed line item contract, you know exactly what you're investing in. One customer wrote that the cost stayed within the estimate. [OWNER: what customers usually ask about on the estimate]
Spot B
Last, ask how you pay. If a contractor wants money up front to begin work, be careful. You shouldn't pay for work that hasn't started. Most vendors and stores offer line of credit accounts to responsible, reputable contractors. We don't ask for up front payment. We take a small deposit to reserve your spot on the schedule. Then we bill weekly, for the work done and the materials used. Every material receipt is attached and hand delivered with a copy of the invoice. Final payment is due when the job is finished and you're happy with it.
4
The order a remodel gets done in, from the phone call to the final walk through
The useful one
▾
A homeowner who has never remodeled cannot picture the sequence, and the Process page already lays it out step by step. Walking through it costs nothing to give away and tells viewers they will know what happens next.
Spot A
A remodel gets done in a set order, and it starts before anybody swings a hammer. It begins with a phone call and a free estimate. Then we sit down with you to go over the estimate, and the 3D models if you want them. That step matters, because it's how we tailor the estimate to your budget. Once you accept, we write up a contract, you sign it, we collect a deposit, and we set a start date and a finish date. [OWNER: the step that surprises homeowners the most]
Spot B
First we prepare the space and seal off the entries, so the dust doesn't travel through your home. Then demolition, and we put a dump bin on site. Framing comes next, with doors, windows, siding and masonry if the job calls for them. Then the rough in for roofing, electrical, plumbing and HVAC. Then insulation, and then drywall, with tape, float and texture.
Spot C
Cabinets and trim get primed and sealed before they go in, and stain grade pieces get stained and clear coated. Tile goes in with the shower waterproofed. Then flooring, and then the carpentry: cabinets, interior doors, casing, baseboards and crown molding. Paint comes after that. Then the electrician, the plumber and the HVAC crew come back to hang the fixtures. Last is the punch out, with the knobs, pulls, mirrors and towel bars. Then the final walk through with you.
