2
Sonora waited twenty one years for its first train
The local anchor
▾
Sonora started a railroad in 1909 and did not see a train until 1930. Anita telling that story is local interest from the woman who has sold land here since 1990.
Spot B
Sonora didn't get a train until May 13, 1930. Track work started in 1909, and then the money ran out. The Santa Fe took over the line in 1928 and finally got it finished. That's twenty one years from the first shovel to the first train.
Spot C
The town itself started around 1885, when a rancher and merchant named Charlie Adams settled near a place called Winkler's Well. He named it after a family servant from Sonora, Mexico. In 1890 Sonora became the Sutton County seat. Adams offered free lots to get people to come. A man named R. W. Callahan gave the land for the courthouse, which was finished in 1893.
Spot A
During the Depression, local sheep and goat raisers formed a marketing cooperative. In its first year it sold more than two million seven hundred thousand pounds of wool. The National 4-H Wool Judging Contest has been held right here since 1938. Today we sit where Interstate 10 meets Highway 277, sixty five miles south of San Angelo. [OWNER: a place in Sonora or Sutton County you love, or a story your family tells about it] I've been selling land around here since 1990. Call the office at 325 387 6115.

4
Four questions to ask before you buy river or hunting land
The useful one
▾
Every buyer falls for the view first. Four plain questions from an agent who has sold riverfront and hunting land for thirty years is the kind of post a buyer saves and a seller forwards.
Spot A
If you're looking at land out here, you're going to fall in love with the view. Go ahead and do that. Then ask four questions before you make an offer. First, how do I get in. Is there a legal road all the way to the gate, and who maintains it. A road on a map isn't always a road you can drive.
Spot B
Second, where does the water come from. Is there a well, a creek, a river, and how does it run when it hasn't rained in months. Third, who owns the minerals. In Texas that can be a different owner than the surface, so ask before you sign. Ask what was kept back, and read the deed. A good rain year can hide a lot of problems, so ask about the dry years.
Spot C
Fourth, what does the river do when it comes up. On the Devil's River, the Pecos, the San Saba, the North Llano or Lake Amistad, ask how high the water has been and what that does to the place. [OWNER: one thing river or hunting buyers miss that you always point out] On a hunting place, ask what lives there too, whitetail, turkey, axis, and who has hunted it before. If you want somebody to ask those with you, I've sold land in this country since 1990. The number is 325 387 6115.
