1
Four generations, and now it's my turn
The origin and the face
▾
A funeral home is the most personal choice a family makes, and J.D.'s own history from Goldthwaite to San Angelo is why a family would trust his name. The script is plain and steady, with no jokes and no sales language.
My family has been in funeral service for four generations. My great-grandfather owned a funeral home in Goldthwaite, Texas. My grandfather, Billy Ray Harper, followed him into the business. Later my father and my uncle joined, and they carried it on for San Angelo and the communities around it. [OWNER: one thing the family has told you about your great-grandfather] I grew up around the funeral home. I worked there while I was in high school. Then I went to Texas A&M, and when I graduated, I chose to follow my father into funeral service. [OWNER: the first job you remember doing there as a kid] My wife Ashley and I built a new funeral care facility here in San Angelo. We grew this business from the ground up. I'm J.D. Harper, owner and funeral director. Today that means personalized funeral services, burial services, cremation, and help planning ahead. I watched my father and my grandfather sit with grieving families. I saw what it meant to those families to be treated with care. A funeral home that treats people well makes a difference in a town. To me this work is a way to help people carry the burden of loss. Now it's my turn to continue the legacy of service that began with my family four generations ago. When you call us on the hardest day, that name on the building is mine.

How my grandfather ended up in San Angelo
The local anchor

My grandfather came to San Angelo to find work. His father in law suggested it. He'd grown up in a funeral family in Goldthwaite, and this town had a place for him. [OWNER: what year, and what you know about his first days here] Billy Ray Harper built a strong reputation for service in San Angelo. He became the owner of a funeral home here. Later my father and my uncle joined him, and they kept the focus on high quality care for families in this town and the ones around it. That reputation is the reason you'll find us today at 2606 Southland Boulevard. We also have a second location in Eden, at 231 North Main Street. When someone in this part of Texas passes away, we want that family to meet somebody they can trust. That's what my grandfather started here.
3
Three questions to ask before you choose a funeral home
The thing the big competitor will not say
▾
Ownership, a funeral director in charge and what happens after the service are the questions a grieving family never thinks to ask. The script answers all three from the site and names no one else, and it makes no price claim.
When someone dies, you call a funeral home. Most people don't stop to ask three questions first. Who owns it? Is the owner a funeral director? And what happens after the service? Here's how we answer. I own Harper Funeral Home and Crematory, and I'm a funeral director myself. My family has done this work in Texas for four generations. The third question matters most, and it gets asked the least. The service lasts a day. Grief lasts much longer than that. So we offer a year of grief support messages that you can sign up for, and resources for the weeks and months after the service. [OWNER: a family that stayed in touch with you for years] We also build an online memorial with the obituary and photos, where people can leave condolences, share memories and light a virtual candle. We can make a tribute video from your family's photos, and you can get a copy on DVD or digitally. If you'd rather plan ahead, our advance planning specialist can sit down with you. Flowers can be ordered through local florists from the obituary page. Price is a conversation for a table. It depends on what your family wants. Call us at 325 340 1850 and ask your three questions. We'll answer all of them.
4
What to do in the first hours after someone dies
The useful one
▾
A family in the first hour has no idea what is next, and this gives them the order of operations. It comes straight from the shop's own guide, it costs nothing to share, and it is the post a neighbor forwards.
If someone you love has just died, here is what comes first. The first step is a legal pronouncement of death. In a hospital or nursing home, the staff takes care of that. If your person was under hospice care, the hospice staff can do it. At home without hospice, call emergency officials right away. They'll take your loved one to a hospital to be declared. Nothing else can start until that's done. Next, find out whether they planned ahead. If they did, start with their choices. If they didn't, you'll meet with a funeral director to decide burial or cremation, the kind of service, the date, the time and the place, a casket or an urn, the obituary, and who will speak. If your person served in the military, call the Veterans Administration about benefits. [OWNER: the one thing families most often forget on that first day] You'll need some information for the death certificate. Full name and address, date and city of birth, marital status, parents' names. Then gather the paperwork. Life insurance policies, account statements, military discharge papers called a DD 214, and the Social Security number. And take care of the home. Secure the house and the car, arrange care for any pets, and forward the mail so it doesn't pile up. You can call us at 325 340 1850. We'll help you through it.
