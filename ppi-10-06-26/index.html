If you're buying a house, a stranger is going to walk through it with a flashlight before you close. You ought to know who he is. I'm Michael Nichols. 2
If the roof is older than May 21, 2020, ask what happened to it
Local anchor
▾
The National Weather Service write-up of the May 21, 2020 hailstorm says it damaged roofs across much of San Angelo, which gives Michael a local reason to teach buyers what to ask about a roof.
Spot B
If you're looking at a house in San Angelo, ask how old the roof is. If it's older than May 21, 2020, that roof was here for the hailstorm. The National Weather Service wrote that one up. Hail the size of golf balls and tennis balls, some of it up to three inches across. The storm sat over town for about ninety minutes and did considerable damage to roofs on homes and businesses across much of San Angelo.
Spot A
I've lived here 25 years, and the roof is on my list for every house. I look at the roof, the chimney, the flashings, and the valleys. Flashings are the metal where the roof meets a wall or a chimney. Valleys are where two roof slopes come together. Those are two of the places water likes to find a way in. Hail damage can be hard to spot from the driveway.
Spot C
So before you fall for the house, ask the seller three things. How old is the roof? What's been done to it since May 2020? And is there paperwork for that work? Write down the answers. If it's been replaced since then, good. Get the date. If the answer is "I'm not sure," have somebody look at that roof before you close.
3
Three questions to ask before you hire any home inspector
Competitor contrast
▾
A buyer choosing between inspectors has little to go on, so three questions they can forward to a friend or an agent let Michael's construction background and plain reports do the comparing for him.
Spot C
Before you hire a home inspector, ask him three questions. It's the one professional look you get at the house before it's yours. First, what have you built? A license is where it starts. Every inspector you call should have one. What he's built, remodeled, and maintained decides what he notices once he's in your house. I've spent 37 years building, remodeling, and maintaining houses and commercial buildings. Unlike a lot of inspectors, I know exactly how a house is built. That goes for a four-plex too. I inspect those.
Spot A
Second, will your report make sense to me? Mine is detailed and easy to understand. When I find a problem, I look at what's causing it and what it means for you. A buyer, a seller, or an agent should be able to read it and make a decision.
Spot B
Third, will you answer my questions? I take the time to answer every one and explain what I found, so you feel confident about what comes next. Call, text, or email me any time. Ask every inspector on your list the same three things, then hire the one you'd want walking through your own family's house. It's your house, and it's worth the five minutes. I'm Michael Nichols, and I'll answer all three.
4
If your builder gave you a warranty, the date to mark is month eleven
Useful customer education
▾
The eleven month warranty inspection is on Michael's own service list, and a deadline tip like this gives a new home owner a reason to follow him long before they need him.
Spot A
If your builder gave you a one-year warranty on a new house, the clock is already running. Find the date it ends and count back one month. That's when you want somebody to go through the whole house. It leaves you time to hand your builder a list before the warranty runs out.
Spot B
That's what an eleven month warranty inspection is for, and it's on my list of services. You can book one through the scheduler on my website. I go through the house the way I'd go through any other, inside and out. A house goes through its first year of heat, cold, and settling, and some things only show up then. I write up what I find, clear and detailed, so you can hand it to your builder with a date on it.
Spot C
Pull out your warranty paperwork and read what it covers. Find the end date and put month eleven on your calendar. If you can't find the paperwork, call your builder and ask for a copy. Between now and then, keep a list. A door that sticks, a crack in the drywall, a stain on the ceiling. Write down what you saw and when, and take a picture. After the warranty runs out, a repair your builder might have covered is yours to pay for.
