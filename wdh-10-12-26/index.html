1
My parents met on a job site, and I started with a wheelbarrow
The origin and the face
▾
Wesley's family builder story is told on his site in general terms, and a 2017 San Angelo LIVE profile holds the specifics: the wheelbarrow at eight, the grandfather, the parents who met on a job site. In his own voice it turns a legacy line into a person.
Spot A
I'm Wesley Doss, and I'm a fifth generation builder. My grandfather, Jim Clark, started Clark Construction here in San Angelo in 1981. He got into the trade at sixteen, laying cinderblock part time. His father and his grandfather built houses too. My parents met on a job site. And my earliest memories are of being eight years old on one, picking up bricks and shovels and pushing a wheelbarrow.
Spot B
In high school I worked summers for my grandfather. Then I went to Angelo State and studied business. I started full time with him at twenty. I'll tell you what I learned. Time swinging a hammer beats time in the classroom. That man has done every single trade, and he taught me a lot.
Spot C
Today I run W Doss Homes, out of Foster Street here in San Angelo. We do custom home design, luxury home construction, and renovations and additions. [OWNER: the year you started W Doss Homes, and what you build now] I've served on the board of the Home Builders Association of San Angelo since 2018. Building is in my blood and San Angelo is in my heart. I grew up here, and I'm building here because of the people who raised me in this trade. Call us at three two five, two three eight, six one eight oh.
2
The house on Cedar Hill that three builders built together
The local anchor
▾
The 2025 Parade of Homes coverage in San Angelo LIVE lists Wesley, Gary Floyd and Chad Decker as the team behind the HBA Showcase Home at 3142 Cedar Hill. It puts him inside a spring tradition the whole town knows, and shows a builder working with other builders.
Spot B
Every spring during rodeo season, San Angelo builders open their doors for the Parade of Homes. In 2025 it was eleven homes from seven local builders. Builders and their people stand in the house and walk you through it. Some of the subcontractors who built it can be there too. One house on the tour is the official Home Builders Association Showcase Home. In 2025 that was 3142 Cedar Hill.
Spot C
Three of us built it together, Gary Floyd, Chad Decker, and me. Three builders, one house. The tour billed it as affordable yet high end. [OWNER: what you remember most about building Cedar Hill with Gary and Chad] The builders pay entry fees to be in the Parade. A portion goes to the San Angelo chapter of Sleep in Heavenly Peace.
Spot A
That's how the Home Builders Association works here. In 2010 my grandfather and I were at a builders show in Dallas. I ran into an old friend, a veteran, who became one of the first people helped by a group called Operation Finally Home. It builds houses for injured veterans. My grandfather heard about it and said, I think I want to do it. The Home Builders Association of San Angelo adopted the project. A year later we built the first Operation Finally Home house in San Angelo.
3
The code says eighteen inches. Ask your builder how deep they go.
The thing the big competitor will not say
▾
The 2017 profile puts numbers on the part of a house nobody sees: thirty inch steel footings against an eighteen inch code minimum, and an eight inch caliche pad. Any builder can be asked the same question, and few will have an answer ready.
Spot C
How deep are your footings? That's the first thing I'd ask any builder before you sign anything. The code minimum is eighteen inches. When I worked with my grandfather, we poured steel rebar footings thirty inches deep. That's almost twice the minimum. The foundation is the part of the house everything else sits on. [OWNER: the footing depth you pour on your homes today]
Spot A
West Texas has a wide variety of soils. So we also set an eight inch caliche pad under the foundation, to stabilize the weight of the house. You never see any of it once the floor goes in. That's why you ask before the slab is poured.
Spot B
Second question. How often will you be on my job site? At my grandfather's company, we visited every job site at least once a day. His standard was that when the crew leaves, there is no reason to call them back. Third question. Who handles permits, inspections, and the budget? At W Doss Homes, we handle every part of that, from permits and timelines to inspections and budgets. That leaves you free to focus on the house you're picturing. Fourth question. How fast do I hear back from you? Our approach is clear communication and quick responses. Ask a few builders those four questions, and compare the answers.
4
Why new houses have taller ceilings than the one you grew up in
The useful one
▾
The reason ceilings and floor plans grew in West Texas comes straight from the builder's own profile: better insulation and stronger air conditioners. It costs nothing to explain and gives a buyer four questions to ask.
Spot A
If you're planning a custom home in West Texas, you probably want high ceilings. Here's why you can have them now. For a long time, the size of a house was limited by how much space an air conditioner could cool without costing a fortune. Eight foot ceilings were the standard. Our average house used to run about eighteen hundred to two thousand square feet.
Spot B
Then two things changed. Insulation got a lot better. And air conditioners got more powerful and more energy efficient. Now you can build a bigger house with higher ceilings, and the hot air rises up to the ceiling and circulates. The ceilings we build are typically nine to twelve feet. Sometimes they go as high as twenty one. We regularly build homes over twenty four hundred square feet. [OWNER: the typical size and ceiling height of the homes you build today]
Spot C
So if you're planning a house, ask your builder a few things. What ceiling height do you recommend for my lot? What does the air conditioning have to handle? How good is the insulation? And what will it cost to cool in August? Nobody sees the insulation once the drywall goes up, so ask about it first. If you want to talk it through, call us at three two five, two three eight, six one eight oh.
