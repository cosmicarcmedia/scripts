1
A kid from Grady, Arkansas who bought a San Angelo clinic
The origin and the face
▾
Pet owners pick a vet they trust as a person, and Dr. Russell's path from a small Arkansas town to Oklahoma State, to San Angelo in 1999, to owning North Concho in 2005 is all on his own staff page. Added to his house full of animals, it makes him easy to remember.
Spot A
I grew up in Grady, Arkansas. Small town. I got my veterinary degree from Oklahoma State in 1999. That same year I moved to San Angelo. I worked at several clinics around town. Then in 2005 I bought North Concho Veterinary Clinic. It's still a family run clinic.
Spot B
This clinic opened in 1970, so it was here a long time before I was. I wanted to keep what it was built for. Complete, compassionate medicine, surgery, and preventive care for pets of all ages. We aim for complete care, with an emphasis on your pet's quality of life. Part of the mission here is teaching you about your pet's health and how to prevent problems. Since 2005 I've put money into education and technology, so we could grow and still feel personal. Digital x rays. Blood work in the clinic. Dental cleanings. Surgery. [OWNER: the equipment or training you're proudest to have added]
Spot C
At home, my wife Kresta and I have more animals than a person should admit. Dogs, cats, ferrets, horses, chickens, ducks, toads, a rabbit, and a sheep. [OWNER: the name of the sheep, or the pet that runs your house] So when you tell me your pet is family, I understand. I live in that house. Away from the clinic, I'm camping, hiking, jet skiing, or reading a book. I'm Dr. John Russell. This is North Concho Veterinary Clinic, on West 14th Street in San Angelo.
2
Fifty six years on West 14th Street
The local anchor
▾
The clinic has been on the books since 1970, closes at six on weekdays and sits behind a local emergency association on nights and weekends. Naming the street, the hours and who covers when the doors are closed answers the question every San Angelo pet owner asks at some point.
Spot B
What happens when your dog gets sick on a Saturday? We're open Monday through Friday, eight to six, and closed Saturday and Sunday. We're a member of the Concho Valley Veterinary Emergency Association. [OWNER: how a Saturday or after hours emergency call works in San Angelo] North Concho Veterinary Clinic has been taking care of San Angelo pets since 1970. That's fifty six years of this town's dogs and cats. We're at 730 West 14th Street.
Spot A
Summer in West Texas is hard on animals, and on the people who love them. If you board with us, it's an indoor facility with central heating and air, and an experienced kennel staff. Boarding is for our existing clients, so if you're new, come in for a visit first. We also microchip pets with Home Again, a pet recovery service, so a lost pet has a way home.
Spot C
Our staff is local too. We see pets of all ages, from puppies and kittens to the mature and geriatric ones. Teresa and Chad are our veterinary technicians. Carey and Victoria are part of the team. And a few residents don't get paid. Finnick the cat, Scotty the dog, and Xena the cat. They each get a spot on our staff page, and they earned it. Come see us at 730 West 14th Street.
3
Ask who owns your vet's clinic
The thing the big competitor will not say
▾
Corporate groups are buying independent clinics around San Angelo, and most pet owners never ask who owns the one they use. Dr. Russell bought this clinic in 2005 and says so plainly, with the payment and appointment rules that go with it.
Spot C
Here are three questions you can ask any veterinary clinic. Who owns it? Is my appointment time held for my pet? And how do I pay, and when? More and more clinics get bought by groups based somewhere else. The sign on the door stays the same, and the owner changes. It's worth asking, because the answer tells you who makes the decisions about your pet's care.
Spot A
I bought this clinic in 2005. That's twenty one years under one owner, in a family run clinic that has been here since 1970. When you call 325 653 1391 and book, that time is reserved for your pet. Once you make an appointment at our office, that time is yours. If you can't keep it, call and we'll reschedule. You can reach us by phone, or by text through the link on our contact page.
Spot B
Now the money part, because everybody wants to know. Payment is due at the time of service, unless we've made other arrangements ahead of time. We take cash, checks, and the major credit cards. We also take CareCredit, which lets you pay a bigger bill over time. You'll know all of that before you ever sit down in the exam room. [OWNER: one thing you can do at your own clinic that a corporate clinic would need approval for]
4
What to bring to your first vet visit
The useful one
▾
New pet owners and people switching vets want to know what to bring and what it costs. The answers are on the clinic's own Patient Info page, so this gives away nothing but time and sends a first visit in prepared.
Spot A
Bringing a pet to a new vet? Good news. You mostly just need your pet. Call us at 325 653 1391 and set the appointment. When you book, ask which records we'll need from your last veterinarian. That one question saves everybody the most time. We're open Monday through Friday, eight to six. Once you book, that time is held for your pet.
Spot B
If you want to save even more time, you can print our New Client Form from our website and fill it out at the kitchen table. If your pet's having a procedure, there's a Pre Anesthesia Release Form there too. [OWNER: whether to bring a stool sample to a first visit] Bring whatever records you have, even the messy ones. Vaccine records, surgery records, lab results, anything from your last vet. Cats, bring a carrier. Dogs, bring a leash. It sounds obvious until a cat walks into the waiting room loose.
Spot C
Payment is due when the visit is done, unless we arranged something ahead. We take cash, checks, and the major cards. If a bigger bill is coming, ask us about CareCredit. It lets you pay over time. We're at 730 West 14th Street, Monday through Friday, eight to six. If you'd rather text, there's a link on our contact page to text the clinic.
