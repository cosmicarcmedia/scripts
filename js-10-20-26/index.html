1
Almost eleven years in my uncle's shop before I opened my own
The origin and the face
▾
Todd's name carries the shop, and a buyer spending thousands on a saddle wants to know who learned the trade where. His uncle's shop, the 2004 start at home and the four people who work for him tell that in one minute.
Spot A
I moved to Texas in November of 1992 to learn how to build custom saddles. My uncle had a saddle shop, so that's where I went to work. I stayed almost eleven years. [OWNER: the first job your uncle gave you in the shop] Tooling, stitching, finishing, every step of a saddle is something you learn by doing it. [OWNER: one thing your uncle taught you that you still do the same way]
Spot B
In April of 2004, I went out on my own. Jeys Saddlery started out of my house. Then I moved into the shop on North Chadbourne Street, and that's where we are today. [OWNER: the first saddle you built under your own name] There are four of us working here full time now. Every one of them was picked for the skill they bring to a saddle, and I train every one of them the same way. Between us we handle the tooling, the stitching and the finishing, all to your specifications.
Spot C
I build every saddle as if it were my own. My kids spend time in the shop too, learning the work. In the summer of 2010, we ended up on RFD-TV. [OWNER: what the RFD-TV crew asked you about] Today there's no fancy equipment in the shop and no assembly line. It's people who know leather, building one saddle at a time. That's how a saddle maker from [OWNER: where you grew up] ended up on North Chadbourne Street. If you're ever in San Angelo, come by the shop and see it for yourself.
2
What walks through the door of a saddle shop on North Chadbourne Street
The local anchor
▾
Customers know the shop for saddles, but the praise is for a belt, a purse and a knife case. Naming those puts the everyday work next to the saddles and tells locals Jeys fixes small things too.
Spot B
A lot of what comes through our door on North Chadbourne Street isn't a saddle. It's a belt that needs a quick fix, and we take care of it while you're standing here. It's a custom knife case somebody's sister made them, and it needs a fix. It's a purse a dog got hold of. That lady figured it was headed for the trash. We cut both corners down to match, and you can't tell it ever happened. [OWNER: the strangest thing anyone has ever brought in to be fixed]
Spot A
We're at 1112 North Chadbourne Street here in San Angelo. Open Monday through Thursday, eight to five. Fridays can vary, so call ahead if you're coming on one. This town has been putting on a stock show and rodeo since 1932. That's ropers, barrel racers, cutters and ranch hands, all in one place. Those are the people we build for. Cutters, ropers, barrel racers, reiners, ranch sorters. We build trophy saddles for all kinds of events too. And yes, we make cow counters for the penners and sorters.
Spot C
Then there's the work that waits around for a while. A man brought in his grandfather's belt to be restored. It took him half a year to come pick it up. We held onto it the whole time. If it's built out of leather, we can build it, and we can usually fix it too. Come by the shop and see us.
3
Ask who built your saddle, who it was built for, and what happens if it is wrong
The thing the big competitor will not say
▾
Every buyer has the same three worries and nobody says them out loud. Answering them from Todd's own policies page, with no one named, is the sharpest thing he can say in a minute and the one a rider forwards to a friend.
Spot C
A saddle off a rack was built for nobody in particular. Mine are built for one rider. So before you spend three thousand dollars on a saddle, ask who built it. At my shop, there are four of us, working by hand, one saddle at a time. No fancy equipment and no assembly line. Herman Oak leather, stainless steel hardware, and top quality trees.
Spot A
Then ask who it was built for. We take the time to get to know our customers before we cut any leather. A Jeys saddle starts at thirty four hundred dollars, and the price climbs with the options you pick. You choose every detail on the order worksheet, and then you sign off on it. Double check the spelling. Double check the details. Half down when you order, and the rest when you pick it up. [OWNER: how long a custom saddle takes from deposit to pickup]
Spot B
And ask what happens if it's wrong. Once the leather is cut, it's cut. Every saddle is made for one person, so I can't resell it, and that's why we don't take returns. But if the mistake is ours, we fix it, repair it, or replace it at no cost. I make that call myself. I build it for you, you know the price before I start, and I stand behind the work.
4
Which saddle fits the work you do, and what to bring when you order
The useful one
▾
A first time buyer sees eleven saddle types and freezes. Matching the work to the saddle, straight off Todd's own pages, gives him a minute that helps any rider and makes the first phone call easy.
Spot A
We build eleven kinds of saddle. Picking the right one comes down to a single question. What are you doing on that horse? Let's start there.
Spot B
If you're working cattle, a ranch cutter is built for that. It has Buster Welch front roping bars. It's designed for cutting cows, plus some light roping and ranch work. If you rope in the arena, you want a roper. That one has the Will James Modified Association Olin Young front, and it's made for arena roping. If you run barrels, our barrel racer is built on a modified Martha Josey tree. The tree under every saddle depends on the type of saddle, the size of the horn, the cantle, and the size of the saddle itself. [OWNER: the question you ask first when somebody walks in for a saddle]
Spot C
Then there's the small stuff that saves everybody a trip. Bringing in a belt? Know your waist size exactly. Don't guess. Bringing in a brand? Draw it out, bring a picture, or email us the exact one. Having something repaired? Tell us what you want done. Rewooled, new strings, oiled, a new zipper, new straps. The more specific you are, the better it comes out. And if it's a saddle, print the order worksheet off our website and mark the features you want before you call. Call the shop at 325 653 3515, or come by and talk it through.
