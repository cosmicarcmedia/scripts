The scripts

A first pass. I made some assumptions about your business to write these. On a shoot we'd dial them in to your goals.

1
I started making ice cream at twelve. Then I went to Penn State.
The origin and the face
▾
Nobody standing in line knows the man behind the scoop took a Penn State ice cream course, ran a bed and breakfast that served his ice cream, and learned the family recipe from his father, and every one of those facts is a reason to trust what is in the cone.
Spot A
Ice cream is cream, sugar and a freezer. Except I spent eight days at Penn State, fourteen hours a day, learning how to make it. My classmates worked for some of the biggest names in ice cream. I still had a head start, because I'd been making it since I was twelve years old.
Spot B
My father taught me the family recipe. Ice cream has been made in my family for about three generations. Before the shop, I ran a bed and breakfast, and for years I made homemade ice cream for the guests. [OWNER: the first flavor you ever made on your own] The year before we opened, I took a lemon ice cream to Austin's ice cream festival, and it won best of show in the lemon category.
Spot C
In 2016, Judy, Scott and I opened The Latest Scoop out of a trailer by the river. The name tells you the plan: we're always bringing out new flavors. I try to be inventive, and I try to be original when I can. My time in Brazil gave me the idea for an avocado ice cream. Texas Country Reporter even put us on TV. These days it's all made right here, ice cream, sorbet and every waffle cone. Come see what's new this week.
2
Look for the big 37 on Concho Avenue
The local anchor
▾
A trailer by the river that grew into a shop in the old bus station is a story the whole town can repeat, and the big 37 gives a first-time visitor something to look for.
Spot B
We started in a trailer on the 300 block of South Irving, down by the river. We were open noon to nine, selling dips, and asking San Angelo what flavor to make next. People left their ideas on our Facebook page. Now we're inside the old bus station on West Concho Avenue, between Irving and Chadbourne. [OWNER: what you remember about the day you moved in] We've still got the same plan: always something new.
Spot A
If you've never found us, look for the sign with the big 37. That's the number on the building. Walk in and people tell us the smell of fresh waffle cones is the first thing they notice. We bake those right here, same as everything else on the board. We make sorbet too, and real fruit smoothies, fresh squeezed lemonade and fruit teas. Hot chocolate when it's cold. And pints and quarts, if you want to take some home.
Spot C
We open at two, Tuesday through Sunday. We close Mondays, because Monday is for making ice cream. Most weeks there's something new on the board. One week it was Amaretto Cherry, with sweet dark cherries all the way through. Planning a birthday party, a meeting or a get together? Ask us. We book those. We do catering and events too. [OWNER: how the trailer gets used for events now] West Concho, between Irving and Chadbourne. Look for the 37.
3
Ask any ice cream shop who made it
The thing the big competitor will not say
▾
Questions anyone can ask at any counter, each one answered by something the shop already does on site, so the contrast lands with no one named.
Spot C
Next time you're at any ice cream counter, ask one question. Who made this, and where? Here the answer is easy. Ice cream, sorbet and waffle cones, all made from scratch in this building. On Mondays we close to make ice cream. Ask if the cone is baked in the shop. Ours is. Ask if the person who made it ever studied ice cream. I did, at Penn State.
Spot A
Then ask what's in it. Ask about the pecans. We roast our own. Ask about the strawberry. It is real strawberry puree. Ask about the vanilla. Most people use Madagascar bourbon vanilla. I use Tahitian, and my Tahitian Vanilla Bean took first prize at the Austin ice cream festival. [OWNER: how Tahitian tastes different, in your own words]
Spot B
Ask the person scooping what's in the flavor you picked. Our staff know the flavors, and they'll tell you. You don't have to take my word for any of it. Yelp put this shop at number 39 on its 2025 list of the top 100 ice cream shops in the country. They looked at reviews, star ratings and health inspection scores. Ask how long that flavor has been on the board. Ours change most weeks, because we keep making new ones. Ask the same questions at every shop you walk into. Go ahead and ask us first.
4
How to order, straight from our customers
The useful one
▾
Customers already write about the cones and the pairings, so this one is half written by them, and it teaches a first-timer how to order.
Spot A
Here's an order tip from the people who've stood in our line. Ask what flavors of waffle cone we have today. We bake them here, and customers write about the cones more than anything else. So pick your cone while you wait, because it's half your order. The flavors change often, and some favorites come back around, so ask what's in the case before you decide.
Spot B
Here are some pairings customers have written about. Blueberry lavender in the homemade cinnamon waffle cone. Cookie butter in a maple bacon cone. Roasted strawberry in a bacon waffle cone. A few people write about the pistachio cone too. And for a first visit, one customer went with Mexican vanilla and soft chocolate, and wrote that it was so good. [OWNER: the pairing you'd order yourself]
Spot C
If you can't decide, ask the person behind the counter. They know the flavors, and they know how the shop works. Coming with a big group? One party of thirteen, in town for a graduation, wrote that we got all of them served in under ten minutes. Decide in line, order at the counter, and you're sitting down with a cone before your friends finish arguing. We're on West Concho, in the old bus station. Look for the 37.

How a cone gets made

Film one cone from the moment it is baked to the first scoop going in, with the sound of the shop as the only audio for the first few seconds. Then ask the person holding it which flavor goes in and why. Deana B.'s review gives you the opening line: the smell when you first walk in.
