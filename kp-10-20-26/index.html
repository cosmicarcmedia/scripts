The scripts

1
I'm the Krammer in Krammer Plumbing
The origin and the face
▾
A customer who can't see you has to guess who is about to walk through the door. A license number, a start date and a plain statement of how he treats a house tell a stranger who this is, and the two open lines carry what only he can say.
Spot A
The hard part of calling a plumber is that you don't know who's about to walk through your door. So here's who. I'm the Krammer in Krammer Plumbing. The State of Texas has had me licensed as a master plumber since 2018. My license number is 42614, and you can look it up. I started this company in December of 2019. Before that, [OWNER: what you did before you started your own company].
Spot B
Our crew has over sixty years of plumbing experience, all of us put together. [OWNER: who taught you the trade, and one thing they taught you]. We do toilets, water heaters, drains, gas lines, leak detection and water softeners. We work in homes, and we work in businesses. We're licensed, insured and bonded. And we don't call the people we work for customers. We have family. That line is on the front page of our website, and I mean it.
Spot C
I'm a Christian, and I love my kids. I want every house I walk into treated the way I'd want mine treated. That means I show you what's wrong, tell you what it costs, and lay out your options before I touch anything. It means if something I fixed doesn't hold, I come back and make it right. Every house gets the same respect and care I'd want for my own family.
2
A hundred miles around San Angelo
The local anchor
▾
A plumber who names the towns he drives to tells people past the city limits that this video is for them. Christoval, Water Valley and the hard water point come from his own site, and the farthest-job slot is the story only he can tell.
Spot B
Take a map and put a pin in San Angelo. Now draw a circle a hundred miles out in every direction. That's where we work. Tom Green County, Christoval, Water Valley, and all the little places in between. This is a local company. The people on this crew live here, work here, and raise their families here. When your house has a problem, you want somebody close.
Spot A
Anybody who lives around San Angelo knows our water is hard. It builds up sediment in a water heater, and it cuts into how well the heater works. So when we put in a new water heater, we recommend one built for it, with a glass lined tank and an anode rod, and a regular maintenance schedule. We install water softeners too, and we test your water before we size one.
Spot C
On most drain calls, we get to you within an hour. One customer wrote that we were at their house by eight o'clock in the morning. That's the kind of review we work for. We work Monday through Friday, eight to five. Out past the city limits, the nearest plumber can be a long drive. [OWNER: the farthest job you've driven to, and what the problem was]. We'll make that drive, and we offer same day service whenever we can.
3
Ask any plumber where he draws the replace line
The thing the big competitor will not say
▾
The replace-or-repair number, the estimate before the work and the come-back promise are all stated on his own pages, and most homeowners never think to ask a plumber for any of them. Laid out in order, they give a viewer three questions to put to the next plumber without naming anyone.
Spot C
If a plumber tells you your water heater needs replacing, ask him where his line is. Mine is this. If it's eight to ten years old, and the repair costs more than half of what a new one costs, replacing it usually saves you money. If it's younger, or the repair is cheap, we repair it. We work on all the major brands, Rheem, Bradford White, A. O. Smith, Navien and Rinnai. That's the number, said out loud, before anything gets sold.
Spot A
Second, the price. You get the estimate before the work starts, with no hidden fees and nothing added that you didn't ask for. For drain cleaning, the estimate is free. Gas line work gets pressure tested, and we coordinate the inspections the city requires and hand you the paperwork.
Spot B
Third, if my work doesn't hold, I come back. If the same drain clogs again within thirty days from the same problem, we clean it again at no charge. If we can't find your leak, or our fix doesn't hold, we make it right at no extra cost. And ask for his license number. Mine is 42614. You can look it up with the Texas State Board of Plumbing Examiners.
4
Six signs of a leak you can't see
The useful one
▾
A hidden leak does its damage before anyone knows to call. This hands a homeowner six checks they can run today, and the 2 to 4 hour detail and the tools come from his own leak detection page.
Spot A
Most leaks don't show up as a puddle. Some of them are inside a wall or down in the ground, and you won't see them until the damage is done. So here are six signs to look for. Your water bill jumps and you can't explain it. You hear water running when everything in the house is off.
Spot B
There's a wet spot on a floor, a wall or a ceiling. There's a musty smell you can't trace. Cracks show up in your foundation. Or one patch of your yard is greener than the rest. Any one of those can mean a leak somewhere you can't see. If you see one, call somebody before it gets bigger.
Spot C
When we get the call, we start with questions. When did the bill jump, and where do you hear the water. Then we use electronic sensors, thermal cameras and listening equipment. It works on water lines, sewer lines, gas lines and irrigation systems. Most of the time we find the leak without tearing into your walls or digging up your yard. We show you where it is and what your repair options are. After the repair, we pressure test the system. Most leak jobs at a house take two to four hours.
