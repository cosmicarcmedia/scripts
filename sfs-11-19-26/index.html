Before you pay a contractor, get a W-9
Useful customer education
▾
W-9s, the January 31 mailing date and record keeping are all from the firm's own FAQ page, and most small business owners ask them every year. The dollar threshold for 1099s is left out on purpose, because the number changed.
If you pay anybody for work in your business, here's something to do before the first check. Get a W-9 filled out. It's a form from the IRS website, and it takes a minute. It saves a lot of scrambling in January. You'll need their legal name, the way it appears on their tax return. A mailing address. And a federal ID number if it's a business, or a Social Security number if it's a person. Why does that matter? Because at the end of the year, you may owe them a 1099. Rent, contract labor and repairs are the payments that most often trigger one. Those forms usually have to be mailed out by January thirty first. [OWNER: the number of 1099s you prepare in a typical January] Not sure whether a payment needs one? That's one of the things we sort out with our clients every year. One more habit worth having. Keep your tax records for at least three years. Some people keep five, and there's nothing wrong with that. And file by the right date. Partnerships and S corporations are due March fifteenth, a month ahead of individuals. Call us if you're not sure which one you are. The forms are on our website too, under commonly used forms.
