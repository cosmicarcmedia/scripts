Who owns the company that comes into your mother's house
The origin and the face
▾

Spot A
Before a stranger comes into your mother's house, you ought to know who owns the company that sent them. Angelo Home Care is veteran owned and operated. I'm [OWNER: your name], and I served in the [OWNER: branch and years of service]. I'm a West Texas native, from [OWNER: the town you grew up in]. So when we say we help veterans, seniors, and their families, we are talking about our neighbors.
Spot B
What we do is keep people at home. We care for veterans, seniors, and anyone living with a disability, an injury, trouble getting around, or an illness. A caregiver might help with a bath, a meal, a ride to the doctor, or a game of cards. Some days it's dementia care, which means a gentle pace and a steady routine. Some days it's a family caregiver who needs a break, and we cover the shift. It's all non-medical, and it's built around what that person wants their day to look like.
Spot C
Six words run this company. Comfort. Affection. Respect. Integrity. Nurture. Generosity. Take the first letter of each one. It spells caring. The mission is good care at home, and the wellness every client deserves. [OWNER: why you started this company, and who you were looking after when you decided to] If you're not sure what kind of help your family needs, call us. 3
Ask any home care company who the caregiver works for
The thing the big competitor will not say
▾
A family choosing an agency rarely knows the right questions, and this hands them the list. Every question comes from the agency's own services page, and the last one invites the viewer to meet the owner.
Spot B
Before you hire anybody to come into your house, ask who they work for. Is the caregiver an employee of the agency? Or are you the one paying the payroll taxes? Then ask who carries workers' compensation, liability insurance, and a bond if somebody gets hurt in your kitchen.
Spot C
Ask if every person gets a background check. Ask for a written plan of care. Ask if there's somebody to call at two in the morning. Ask how you'll know your caregiver showed up, and whether a phone app checks her in and out of the home. Ask whether the family can log in and see how things are going. Ask who supervises the caregiver, and whether you can call that person. At Angelo Home Care you get a case manager who is always available, so you can reach your caregiver and the manager both.
Spot A
Then ask the one people forget. What happens when something goes wrong? Our promise is follow up and accountability, and we keep working with the family until you're satisfied. Every attendant is carefully screened before they ever meet your family. [OWNER: confirm which of these you do, employees on payroll, workers' comp, bond, background checks, written plan of care] Ask us every one of these, and you should get a straight answer to each. If you want to meet the owner, [OWNER: how a family can reach you directly].
4
How to talk to your dad about the car keys
The useful one
▾
Almost every family with an aging parent faces this talk and nobody wants to start it. The steps come from the agency's own site, cost nothing to give away, and put the owner in the room before anyone needs care.
Spot A
Sooner or later, somebody in your family has to have the talk about the car keys. Nobody wants to be the one. Watch for dents and scratches nobody can explain. Watch for stop signs and red lights getting run. Watch for someone who's gotten nervous behind the wheel, or who won't take the highway anymore. Driving much too fast or much too slow counts. So does a failed vision test, and so does a neighbor telling you what they saw.
Spot B
Don't open with give me the keys. Start by riding along with them and watching for yourself. Then talk about other ways to get around, a friend, a family member, a senior ride service, or a caregiver. A good first step is giving up night driving. It's a smaller ask, and it gives everybody a place to start.
Spot C
If medications or memory could be part of it, bring in the doctor. A physician can write a letter, and that carries more weight than a son or daughter saying it. In Texas, driver licenses come from the Department of Public Safety, so that's who you ask about the license. If this is a conversation your family is having, call us. Our caregivers can help with rides to appointments and errands, and the first consult is free.
