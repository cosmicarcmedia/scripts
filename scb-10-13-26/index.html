The scripts

1
The mixer that took five grown men to unload
The origin and the face
▾
Nobody pulling into the lot on West Avenue N knows this cafe started in a ranch kitchen 110 miles from a supermarket, and a story like that is what a customer remembers about the person behind the pie.
Spot A
The nearest supermarket was a hundred and ten miles away. In 1985, as a newlywed, I moved to my husband's family ranch in West Texas, twenty five miles from the nearest town, and I had cowboys and deer hunters to feed. So I cooked. Big, hearty meals, and every one came with my grandmother's rolls and a dessert. The favorite was always my buttermilk pie.
Spot B
Then word got out. People from town started asking for pans of enchiladas and rolls, and the pie, of course. So I started delivering to [OWNER: the first town you delivered to], and then to towns all over West Texas. One day my father in law drove up with a brand new twenty quart mixer. It took five grown men to unload it into my tiny kitchen. I stood there looking at that mixer, and that's when I knew this was going to be my business.
Spot C
April of 1996, I opened my first restaurant right here in San Angelo. It was called Shana's Country Cuisine. Three years later I opened a little cafe downtown. I've had six different locations since, and they all run on the same idea. Cook it from scratch, with fresh ingredients. I still roll out my pie crust with my grandmother's rolling pin. Come see me at 1816 West Avenue N.
2
A picnic lunch on the Concho, packed by me
The local anchor
▾
Your own To-Go page names three river parks for a picnic, and this puts a ready-made lunch date in front of every San Angelo local who has never thought to order ahead.
Spot B
Want to surprise somebody this week and need an idea? Here's a lunch date that doesn't need a reservation. You don't even need a table. You call me and put in a to go order, and all you bring is something to sit on. I'll handle the food and the drinks. It works for an anniversary, and it works for a plain old Tuesday.
Spot A
We're at 1816 West Avenue N. Monday through Friday we're open eight to six, and Saturday from eleven to two. The boxed lunch is the easy one. A chicken salad sandwich or a homemade hamburger, with homemade potato chips and your choice of tea or lemonade. Or tell me what you want off this week's menu, and I'll pack that to go instead. The chicken salad comes by the pint or the quart, and my yeast rolls come by the dozen, so you can build your own picnic if you'd rather. Enchiladas on a picnic blanket is a fine way to spend a lunch hour.
Spot C
Then you've got your pick of spots along the Concho River. Santa Fe Park. Picnic Bend Park. Tiered Plaza. Spread out the blanket, open the box, and you're having lunch on the river. Every week I post the specials on Facebook, and I'll pack any of them to go. Call me at 325 277 6260 and tell me where you're headed.
3
Ask a chain to bring back the dish you loved
The thing the big competitor will not say
▾
Your site tells customers to just ask when a dish has left the menu, which a chain with one printed menu cannot say, and laying that out plainly draws the line without naming anyone.
Spot C
A big chain restaurant isn't going to bring back the dish you loved. Go ahead and ask them. See how that goes. Fell in love with something that came off the menu? Here, you just ask. That's what special orders are for.
Spot A
My menu changes every week, so nothing ever gets old. [OWNER: this week's specials, read straight off your Facebook post] The favorites stay, the enchiladas, the King Ranch chicken, the chicken spaghetti, the John Wayne Casserole. There are eight to ten kinds of cookies in the case at any time, and those change every day. The rest moves around, and if what you want isn't on it, call me. Coconut pie, lemon pie, chocolate meringue, those are special orders too. I only ask for 48 hours notice, and then I'll make it.
Spot B
Cakes are all special order. Birthdays, weddings, baby showers, Mother's Day, whatever you've got. [OWNER: one special order you're proud of, a cake or pie or dinner someone asked for] We have gluten free options, and if you have any dietary restriction, call and I'll tell you what's in it. A big chain usually can't do any of that. Its menu got written in an office somewhere else. Mine gets written in this kitchen on Avenue N, by the person who's going to cook it. So call 325 277 6260, and tell me what you're craving.
4
How to feed a crowd without a buffet line
The useful one
▾
Anyone planning an office lunch or a birthday party wonders what to order and how far ahead to call, and the answer costs you nothing to give away.
Spot A
Nobody enjoys standing in a buffet line. An office lunch, a birthday, a potluck supper, it doesn't matter, the line is the same. The folks at the front hold everything up, and the folks at the back watch it all get cold. Why make your guests do that? If you're feeding a group, there's an easier way, and it's a boxed lunch.
Spot B
Everybody gets their own box and walks off with it. A chicken salad sandwich or a homemade hamburger, homemade potato chips, and tea or lemonade. They can eat anywhere they like, and they can save half for later. There's a lot less mess for you to clean up afterward. Add a salad to any box if you want one. And if anyone has a dietary restriction, call me and I'll tell you what's in it.
Spot C
Here's the one thing I need from you. For any catering, tell me how much food you need at least seven days before the event. If it's a potluck supper or a birthday party, I can meet you there with the food. For an extra hourly fee, I'll even serve it, so you can relax and enjoy your own party. Call me at 325 277 6260. Tell me what the event is and how many are coming, and I'll tell you what works.
