1
Who answers when you call Pioneer
The origin and the face
▾
Nobody scrolling past a pest control ad knows who owns the company. This one puts Trey's name, his family and the crew's years in front of the viewer, so the next call goes to a person.
Spot A
Everybody in San Angelo has a pest story. A scorpion in a boot. A roach that was supposed to be gone after the last visit. I'm Trey Dunagan, and I own Pioneer Termite and Pest Control. We're locally owned and operated, and I want you to know what that looks like. It's me, and [OWNER: who else in your family works the company, Lawson and Rachel are listed as co-owners]. When you call our office, you get one of us.
Spot B
I opened this company in [OWNER: the year you opened]. Before that, [OWNER: what you did before pest control, or how you got into it]. Between me and the crew, we have over fifty years of combined experience. That's a lot of West Texas bugs. Termites here are active all year. So are roaches, ants, and scorpions. There is no off season in this business, so there is no slow time to learn the job.
Spot C
We've been BBB accredited since 2012. We work on houses, offices, restaurants, and commercial buildings all over the Concho Valley. Customers have written that the folks on our phones are cheerful, and that our people are polite and show up on time. I'll take that over any slogan. So that's the short version of who we are. A local owner, a crew with real years behind it, and a name you can look up. The next time something shows up in your house, you'll know exactly who to call.
2
We will drive out to your town
The local anchor
▾
People in the small towns around San Angelo assume a pest company skips the trip. Naming the towns lets every one of them hear their own and call.

If you live in Eldorado, Mertzon, or Big Lake, you may have been told a pest company won't drive that far. We will. Pioneer works out of San Angelo, and we serve roughly a hundred and fifty miles around it. That's Ballinger, Christoval, Miles, Bronte, and Robert Lee. Sterling City, Eden, Menard, Junction, and Paint Rock. Sonora and Ozona too. The whole Concho Valley.
Spot C
Here's why that matters out here. The soil around San Angelo holds subterranean termites, and they're active all year. They come up through the foundation, and most people never see them until the damage is done. That's true for a house in town. It's true for a ranch house outside Eden. It's true for a new build going up in Sonora.
Spot A
That last one is why we treat the soil before a slab is poured. Once the concrete is down, you can't get back to that dirt. So we work with the builder and we hit their schedule. [OWNER: a town you drive to that would surprise people, or a job that shows what the long drive looks like]. If you're not sure we cover your town, call the office and ask. Monday through Friday, eight to five. We'll tell you straight.
3
Ask every pest company this before you sign
The thing the big competitor will not say
▾
Landon's pick for the one that gets forwarded. It hands a homeowner three questions to ask any company, and Pioneer's own written answers make the case without naming anybody.
Spot C
Before you sign anything with a pest control company, ask one question. What happens if the bugs come back? Ask us. Ask everybody. Here's our answer. If pest problems return within a reasonable time after service, we come back and make it right. [OWNER: how many days or weeks "reasonable" means at Pioneer].
Spot A
Second question. What am I paying for that I don't need? We don't push services you don't need. That's our policy, and I stand behind it. If it's a small problem, you get a plan for a small problem. Most homes and businesses do well with quarterly service. Some jobs need monthly, and we'll tell you which one yours is. Roaches in particular can take more than one visit, because they multiply fast. You should hear that before the first visit.
Spot B
Third question. Who is coming to my house? Pioneer is locally owned. You can look us up. Our BBB profile has us at A plus, accredited since 2012. It shows zero complaints in the last three years. We'd rather have you check than take our word for it. Ask the other companies the same three questions. Then call us and ask again, and I'll answer all three before you sign anything.
4
What to check at home before termites find it first
The useful one
▾
Free, specific help from the person who does the job. It earns the follow from every homeowner and every buyer and seller in town, and it quietly explains why a real inspection is worth booking.
Spot A
Tap on the wall or floor near your foundation. If it sounds hollow or soft, pay attention. That's one sign of termites, and most people knock right past it. I'm Trey with Pioneer Termite and Pest Control. Here's what to look for at home, and it costs you nothing. Take five minutes and walk the house.
Spot B
Look for mud tubes, those thin dirt tunnels running up a foundation wall. Look for little discarded wings near a window or a door. Look for wood that looks damaged. Check the foundation, the crawl space, and the attic, anywhere you can reach. Those are the same places we look at on an inspection. If you find any of it, don't wait to see what happens. Call early.
Spot C
Now the scorpion side. If you keep finding scorpions or spiders, killing the ones you see won't fix it. They're following the insects, so there's something in the house to eat. Seal the gaps around your doors, windows, and utility lines. Cut down the clutter and the moisture around the house. Woodpiles, sheds, and garages are where they like to hide. And if you're buying or selling a house, you'll need a certified termite inspection. Call us before closing week so it doesn't hold anything up.
