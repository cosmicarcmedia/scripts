The scripts

A first pass. I made some assumptions about your business to write these. On a shoot we'd dial them in to your goals.

1
I worked here in high school, and I bought the place in 1985
The origin and the face
▾
Customers see a smoked meat counter and a jerky label. The real story is a man who worked in the Miles Locker Plant as a teenager and bought it from the couple who owned it. That is the face behind forty years of sausage, and he is the only one who can tell it.
Spot A
I'm Paul Sklenarik, and this is Sklenarik's Smoked Meats in Miles, Texas. I started working here when I was in high school. Back then it was the Miles Locker Plant. [OWNER: what your first job at the locker plant was] In 1985, I bought it from Bob and Betty Kvapil. They'd run it before me, and I got to know the place from the inside.
Spot B
We started out doing custom meat processing. People bring in their own animals, and we process them for their own freezers. In 1991, we added a store out front. Jerky was our first product. It took some trials and some errors to get the flavor right. We make smoked sausage, summer sausage, and ham too. Our smoked turkey has won awards, and it's one of the things we're best known for. Other processors around Texas helped me along the way. There's enough business for everybody, and we help each other out.
Spot C
In 1992, we put in our first smokehouse. That's the start of our story. Now we have three smokehouses. It didn't happen all at once. In 2022, our green chile and Swiss smoked sausage took Grand Champion at the Texas Association of Meat Processors convention. My brother Mark is my partner. [OWNER: a word about Marla and the people who've worked beside you from the start] More than forty years later, we're still here. We're just going to keep chugging along like we've been doing.
2
Forty minutes northeast of San Angelo, and people drive out on purpose
The local anchor
▾
Miles is a small town, and the reviews prove people drive past a lot of grocery stores to get here. Saying so, with a real customer's seventy five mile trip, turns a remote address into the reason to come.
Spot B
We're in Miles, Texas, at 108 South Robinson Street. That's about forty minutes northeast of San Angelo. Folks drive out here on purpose. They find us on the website too, westtexasjerky.com. One customer wrote that he found our sausage links years ago, back when he worked for Caterpillar. He's driven seventy five miles each way to buy them ever since. We're open seven days a week.
Spot A
Another fellow moved off to Colorado. He wrote that he makes a point to come back every year and stock up on our meats and seasonings. He says we've become friends. Another customer wrote that we're a great place to have your deer processed. Others wrote that we have awesome staff and products. A man from McAllen wrote that he was hooked on our jalapeño cheddar sausage the first time he tried it. [OWNER: the farthest anybody has ever come for your sausage] That's a lot of miles for a sausage.
Spot C
In September we tell folks to stop in before the Cotton Festival. [OWNER: what Miles looks like that weekend] We don't have a slow time, but deer season and Christmas are our busiest. And if you can't make the drive, we ship our jerky across the country. So Miles, Texas ends up on a lot of porches. Call us at 325 468 3501 and we'll get you taken care of.
3
Three questions to ask about any jerky
The thing the big competitor will not say
▾
A jerky rack in a grocery store or gas station hides the answers a smokehouse can give you out loud: the cut, the day it was made and the cost of shipping. Asking the questions out loud lets you name nobody and still show the difference.
Spot C
Next time you pick up a bag of jerky, ask three questions. What cut of meat is it? When was it made? And if you're ordering online, what does shipping cost? I'll answer all three for ours. Jerky was our first product, so we've had a lot of years to get it right.
Spot A
It's USDA inspected top round. That's right, round steak. Every piece is hand sliced and marinated in our own seasoning blend. Then we slow smoke it over mesquite. It took trials and errors to get that seasoning blend right. We make jerky daily, so it's fresh when it goes out the door. Our plant is USDA inspected and HACCP approved too. We belong to the Texas Association of Meat Processors, the Southwest Meat Association, and the American Association of Meat Processors. Our website is westtexasjerky.com.
Spot B
We make over twelve thousand pounds of it a year. When you order online, shipping is included in the price. No hidden costs. It usually reaches your door in two to three business days. Our jerky ships to customers all over the country. Local folks can pick it up at the store in Miles. [OWNER: your favorite flavor and why] Ask those same three questions of any jerky you buy, from anybody. If you like our answers, order a bag.
4
What to know before you bring us an animal for custom processing
The useful one
▾
First timers rarely know about appointments, cut orders or the five day pickup window, and every one of them calls to ask. Putting the answers on video saves the phone call and sends a new customer in prepared.
Spot A
If you raise your own beef, hogs, lamb, or goat, here's how custom processing works with us. Animals come in on Monday, by appointment. Call 325 468 3501 and get on the schedule. Have your animal here by ten o'clock that morning. You'll fill out a cut order form for it. We take them first come, first served, so a bigger group may mean a longer wait.
Spot B
The animal needs to be healthy and able to walk in on its own. That's where you decide how you want it cut, the steaks, the patties, the hams, the bacon, the sausage. Then we dress it, chill it, cut it, vacuum pack it, box it, and freeze it. [OWNER: the question you hear most from first time customers]
Spot C
When your meat is ready, we call you. You've got five business days to come get it. After that, there's a charge for every day the box sits. Vacuum packing means you can see what's in each package, so there's no mystery in the freezer. Prices and cut order forms for beef, pork, and other animals are on our website. Beef, hogs, lamb, and goat all have their own price lists. If you've got a question, call the shop and ask for me. We've been doing custom processing in West Texas since 1985.
