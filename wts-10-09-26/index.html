1
We bought the oldest moving company in San Angelo
The origin and the face
▾
The About page tells a real handoff: a company founded in the 1930s, bought from the original Welch family in 1988 by two partners who promised to keep the reputation. Hearing that from one of the two partners gives a faceless moving company a face.
Spot A
Welch Transfer and Storage was established in the 1930s. It's the oldest moving and storage company in San Angelo. In 1988, my partner and I bought it from the original Welch family. [OWNER: your names and what each of you was doing before 1988] When you buy a company like that, you buy a reputation. The reputation for quality and care had to be maintained, and we've been working at that for almost forty years.
Spot B
Both of us have been Certified Moving Consultants since 1999. That means we keep training. Eight hours of continuing education, at a minimum, to stay certified. State association meetings, van line conventions, seminars. We do it because the moving business changes and we want to know what's changed. [OWNER: one thing you learned in training that changed how you run a job]
Spot C
We believe in quality and care in these days of price wars. The best move at the fairest price matters most to us. Quality will not be sacrificed in favor of speed or price. When a customer calls us, they're getting a company that has moved San Angelo families for generations, and two partners who have owned it since 1988. If you're about to move, give us a call at 325 655 3751.
2
Moving in San Angelo means triple digits and old doorways
The local anchor
▾
Every local detail here, the summer heat, the older homes near Fort Concho, Goodfellow and the traffic on Bryant and Sherwood Way, comes from the company's own local movers page. It tells a San Angelo family that the crew knows these streets.
Spot C
Moving in San Angelo isn't like moving anywhere else. We regularly see triple digit days in summer. So we plan around it. Early morning moves, or a cooler month, protect your wood furniture, your electronics, and the people doing the heavy lifting. Your belongings spend less time sitting in a hot truck.
Spot A
Many of the houses around downtown and Fort Concho are decades old. They have narrow doorways and older staircases. It takes a crew that's done it before to get a couch around those without damage. And traffic on Bryant Boulevard and Sherwood Way can back up during rush hour. We know when to leave and which way to go. [OWNER: the trickiest house you ever moved a piano or a couch out of]
Spot B
San Angelo is also growing, with older neighborhoods and new development side by side. We move families from the older homes near downtown to the newer additions off Knickerbocker Road. Then there's Goodfellow Air Force Base. Military families move on a fixed timeline, and orders don't wait on anybody. We handle military approved moves, and we have a fifty thousand square foot warehouse here in San Angelo. If you've got orders or a closing date, call early. Summer is when the trucks and crews book up.
3
Who is responsible for your move?
The thing the big competitor will not say
▾
The site's own comparison table lists accountability, estimates and local ownership against brokers, containers and truck rental. Turned into a spoken question without naming anyone, it gives a family a sharp, fair test to apply to any mover they call.
Spot B
When you call a moving company, ask one question. Who is responsible if something goes wrong? Some companies are brokers. They sell you the move, then hand it to a crew you've never met. If something gets broken, the broker isn't the one who moved it.
Spot C
With us, the people planning your move are the people who work here. We're locally owned, so you know who to call. So before moving day, a trained moving consultant walks through your home or looks at photos with you, and gives you an honest quote. It's free, and there's no obligation. We don't publish an hourly rate, because a one bedroom apartment and a five bedroom house don't cost the same.
Spot A
And on a long distance move, ask for it in writing. As a Wheaton agent, we give you a documented estimate and a bill of lading for every long distance move. You should have both before the truck leaves, from anybody you hire. Wheaton has named us a Top 20 Agent twice, and a Top 5 Agent once. Ask any mover those same questions. Then call us at 325 655 3751 and ask us. We give the same answers to everybody. That's what local ownership means to us.
4
Three things to do before moving day
The useful one
▾
The tips are the ones the company already prints on its own long distance page, such as booking early for summer, packing a bag of essentials and protecting heat sensitive items. Anyone moving benefits whether or not they hire Welch.
Spot A
If you're moving, here's what I'd do before the truck ever shows up. First, book early if it's summer. Moves around Goodfellow cluster around summer, and trucks and crews fill fast. Call as soon as you have a date or a closing. And if you've got a piano, a gun safe, a golf cart, say so on the first call. Those take planning and the right equipment.
Spot B
Second, pack a bag you carry yourself. Essentials, medication, chargers. Keep it out of the truck. Many routes out of San Angelo run through long stretches of rural highway with limited services, so pack for the drive too. If your new house isn't ready when the truck arrives, you'll be glad you did. And tell your mover about anything that doesn't like heat. Electronics, wine, musical instruments. Summer trailers get hot fast in West Texas.
Spot C
Third, get the estimate in writing. A written, binding estimate before moving day. That way you know your price before the truck shows up. If your new place isn't ready, ask about storage. We have a fifty thousand square foot warehouse with sprinklers and alarms, and it'll hold your things until you're ready. Ask about storage when you book.
