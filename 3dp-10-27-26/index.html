3
Ask how they'll find the problem before they dig
The thing the big competitor will not say
▾
Homeowners dread an unneeded hole in the yard. The site describes a camera and locator that find a pipe problem and its depth before digging, which makes the question a fair test for any plumber, with no competitor named.
Spot C
If someone tells you there's a problem with your sewer line, ask one question before they dig. Ask them to show you the problem on a screen. How will you find the problem first? A hole in your yard is expensive. You'd like it to be in the right spot.
Spot A
At 3-D's, we use a camera. Our sewer scope has a stainless steel camera head with twelve LEDs, on a push rod that reels out up to two hundred feet. It can inspect lines from an inch and a half up to six inches. A locator reads out where the problem is and how deep it is. The camera can get through P traps and tight bends. We can even take it up on the roof and inspect a four inch line through the vent. [OWNER: the strangest thing you've found with the camera, with no names]
Spot B
On our digs, we use a mini camera to find the depth of the pipe. It can locate cast iron pipe about ten feet down, and clay or plastic pipe about twenty feet down. The goal is to avoid digging more than we have to, which saves you time and money. We also do NuFlow, a trenchless repair that skips the big excavation. Ask your plumber if that's an option for your pipe. [OWNER: when a camera inspection saved a customer from a big dig, with no names]
4
Signs your water heater is on its way out
The useful one
▾
Water heater repair and replacement is on the site, and the page lists the warning signs. A homeowner who learns them and what to do first will remember who explained it, long before the heater fails on a cold morning.
Spot A
Your water heater usually gives you some warning before it quits. The first thing most people notice is no hot water, or water that never gets hot enough. Pay attention if the hot water runs out sooner than it used to, or the temperature goes up and down. Listen for popping, rumbling, or banging from the tank. Those sounds are worth a call.
Spot B
Look at the tank itself. Rust or corrosion on it is a bad sign. Water pooling around the base is worse. If you see a puddle, shut off the water to the heater and call us. If you smell gas, get everyone out of the house and call from outside. Also watch the age. A unit over ten years old that's losing efficiency is a candidate for replacement, and a jump in your energy bill can be a clue too.
Spot C
We repair tank and tankless water heaters. A tank heater stores a set amount of water and keeps it hot. A tankless heater heats water as you need it, so it uses less energy and takes up less space. [OWNER: which type you recommend most often in San Angelo, and why] We can help you figure out which one fits your house. If your hot water goes out suddenly, we have twenty four hour emergency service. I'm [OWNER: your name] with 3-D's Plumbing.
