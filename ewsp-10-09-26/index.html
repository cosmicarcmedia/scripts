The local anchor

A listing shows the price. Here's what it leaves out.
The thing the big competitor will not say
▾
Anyone can post acres and a price online. The questions in this script are what a buyer finds out too late, and they make Erik the agent who asks them first.

A listing online tells you the acres and the price. Plenty of them leave out who owns the minerals. Or whether the county road reaches the gate. Or what the water well tested like. Or who holds the hunting lease, and what the taxes are. Or whether the fence on the ground sits where the deed says it does. Or which way the creek runs after a hard rain.

4
What to do the day hail hits your roof
The useful one
▾
A licensed adjuster who is also a realtor can say what happens after a storm in plain steps. It is the kind of post people save before the next hail season.
Spot A
If hail just hit your house, here's what to do before you call anyone. Take photos of everything, the roof, the gutters, the siding, the windows, and anything in the yard. Write down the date of the storm. Do it before anybody cleans up. Check the ceilings and the attic for water stains too. Don't climb up there yourself, either. A roof with hail damage can be slick, and a photo from the ground still counts.
Spot B
If water is getting in, cover it. A tarp is fine. Keep every receipt for what you spend, because you may need those later. Then read your policy for your deductible. Some policies have a separate deductible for wind and hail. Sometimes it's a percentage of the value of the house.
Spot C
Then call your insurance company and report the claim. Get an estimate from a roofer you trust, so you have something to compare. Be careful with anyone who knocks on your door after a storm and wants a signature that day. [OWNER: one thing you see homeowners get wrong on a hail claim] I hold an adjuster license, so I've seen how these claims get written up. [OWNER: confirm that line is true for the claims you have seen, or cut it]
