3
Ask your roofer what he would tell you if it only needed a repair
The thing the big competitor will not say
▾
Every homeowner shopping for a roof wonders whether the quote is bigger than the problem. This gives them one question to ask any roofer, and the 3J answer comes straight from its own three step inspection.
Spot A
Here's a question for any roofer you're thinking about hiring. What would you tell me if my roof only needed a repair? Some roofers get paid for the biggest job on the table. That's a fact of this business. So the answer tells you a lot. If somebody says you need a whole new roof before anyone has been up there, keep asking.
Spot B
Here's how I do it. First, we inspect it. Shingles, metal panels, flashing, penetrations, ventilation, and every spot that shows wear, leaks, or storm damage. Second, we show you what we found. You get a clear explanation of the roof, so you understand the problem before you decide anything. Third, we give you the right option. If a repair makes sense, I'll say that. If replacement or restoration is the better route, I'll say that too.
Spot C
Not every roof needs to be torn off right away. Sometimes the right move is a repair. Sometimes it's restoration. Sometimes it's replacement. I look at the whole roof system first, so the recommendation matches the condition of the roof. My goal is simple. Find out what your roof needs. Selling you the biggest job possible isn't part of it. On a business, a coating can restore some flat roofs, if the roof qualifies. The inspection is free, and there's no drawn-out pitch.
4
Four spots to check on your roof before you call anybody
The useful one
▾
It hands a homeowner a short checklist they can use from the yard, and it mirrors the list 3J inspects. It costs nothing to give away and shows the person doing the looking.
Spot B
You don't need a ladder to give your roof a first look. Stand in the yard, or grab a pair of binoculars. Start with the shingles. Look for pieces that are curling, cracked, or missing, and bare patches where the surface has worn away. If your roof is metal, look along the panels and the seams for rust, loose spots, or gaps.
Spot C
Then look at the flashing. That's the metal where the roof meets a wall, a chimney, or a pipe. It's one of the most common places for a leak to start. Next, look at anything that comes up through the roof. Vent pipes and exhaust fans. Roofers call those penetrations, and they're another spot where water finds a way in. Last, look at the ventilation, the vents that let your attic breathe. On a flat roof at a business, look for water that sits there for days after a rain.
Spot A
After a storm, look at the metal too. Dents in the vents or the gutters tell you hail came through. Take a few photos with the date on them. Inside the house, look for brown stains on a ceiling. That's often the first sign. If you find any of it, that's what a free inspection is for. We look at the same list, and we'll tell you what we found.
