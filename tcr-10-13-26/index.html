The scripts

A first pass. I made some assumptions about your business to write these. On a shoot we'd dial them in to your goals.

1
I spent years in the medical industry, then came home to the ranch
The origin and the face
▾
Anyone booking a wedding barn in Paint Rock is handing their day to a stranger, and a founding date of 1961 plus the owner who left a medical career to come home is the face that makes the ranch feel like family.
Spot A
The Chaparral Ranch has been a family place since 1961. That's the year Joe and Nancy Brosig founded it. [OWNER: how you're related to Joe and Nancy, in your own words] I'm Leah, and today I run the ranch with my family. It's a working ranch out in Concho County, at Paint Rock, about twenty five miles east of San Angelo. It's family owned and operated, and it always has been.
Spot B
Before all this, I spent years in the medical industry. [OWNER: what you did there and for how long] Then I came home to the ranch, to enjoy ranch life and everything Concho County has to offer. I've also spent years with Texas 4-H, county stock shows, and the San Antonio Stock Show and Rodeo. Ranch life suits me.
Spot C
Most visits to the ranch start the same way. You pull in, and the registered family boxers come out to greet you. Then you start noticing the rest. Deer and turkey. Cattle and goats. Chickens, ducks, and geese. And now and then a friendly white tail buck, hanging around near the house and the barn. We've got a red barn for weddings, anniversaries, reunions, dances, company events, and agricultural meetings, and places to stay right on the property. There's a cabin, the Cactus Inn, and more spots around the county. Call me at 830 305 1129 and come see it.
2
Twenty five miles east of San Angelo and a half mile from the creek
The local anchor
▾
Your address on FM 380 and the walk to Lipan Creek tell a San Angelo couple this is a short drive, not a trip, and nobody else in the Concho Valley can say it about this exact place.
Spot B
Need to get away from San Angelo for a weekend? You don't have to go far. We're at 11490 FM 380 in Paint Rock, about twenty five miles east of town. That's close enough for a weekend, and far enough that you feel like you went somewhere. Out here it's Concho County, and I'll tell you everything I know about it.
Spot A
From the cabin, Lipan Creek is about a half mile walk. Ask me about a picnic down there. Up at the barn, a caliche path wanders from the main house through the mesquite trees. The barn is nine hundred and twenty square feet, and the covered pavilion beside it is nine hundred. Every room at the Cactus Inn opens onto a front porch, and I'll tell you, the sunsets are good out here, and so are the stars. There's a fire pit for everyone staying with us, and I provide the wood.
Spot C
If you ever need ideas for what to do around Concho County, just ask. [OWNER: your favorite thing to do in Concho County that visitors rarely hear about] We have more than one place to stay, too, on the ranch and at other spots around the county. So if you've got a big group, call me and we'll sort it out together. 830 305 1129, and tell me what you're planning.
3
Before you book a venue, ask who answers the phone the night before
The thing the big competitor will not say
▾
Your Airbnb page already warns guests the ranch is rural and not a resort, and saying that out loud on camera is the one honest line a corporate venue or a hotel chain will never put in its own pitch.
Spot C
Before you book any venue, ask who picks up the phone the night before your event. At a lot of big places, it's a coordinator on a shift. Here, it's me. I live on the property, with my family and three boxers. Airbnb shows I answer my guests within the hour, and it lists me as a Superhost.
Spot A
Second thing to ask is what you're getting. I'll tell you straight. This is a working ranch. The animals live here, and you're the visitor. There are cattle and goats and ducks wandering near the house, and yes, there are bugs, and some days there's a snake. It's no resort, and I won't pretend it is. What it is, is a red barn, a covered pavilion, room outside for a tent and a dance floor, and a whole lot of Texas around it.
Spot B
Third thing to ask is what you can bring. [OWNER: what couples can bring, such as their own caterer and drinks, and how many hours the barn rental covers] And ask whoever you're talking to for the answers in writing. Then read the reviews. I've hosted on Airbnb for twelve years. One guest wrote that the place was ready when they arrived, and clean, with comfortable beds and plenty of towels. Call me at 830 305 1129, and ask me all three.
4
What to know before you stay on a working ranch
The useful one
▾
Guests who have never stayed on a ranch wonder how to get here and who to call, and your own listings answer both questions, so sharing them saves you the same phone call over and over.
Spot A
Never stayed on a working ranch? Here's what to know. First, you'll need a car. There's no public transportation out here, so if you're flying into San Angelo, rent a vehicle. And bring your sense of humor about the outdoors. We have bugs and thorns, and now and then a snake. That comes with a ranch, and the views make up for it.
Spot B
Where you stay depends on your group. The Cactus Inn has four separate bedrooms, and each one has its own bathroom. There's a big common area with a kitchen, a washer and dryer, a dining area, and a coffee bar. Every room opens onto a porch, so you can sit out and watch the ranch. And if you want to walk around the house and barn and see the animals, go right ahead.
Spot C
If you want to wander out to the pastures or the creek, ask me first. There's a waiver to sign, and I'd rather walk you through it myself. If you're planning an event, or you're booking more than one place for a big party, call me. I'll confirm what's open for your dates. 830 305 1129, or email chaparralranch@gmail.com. You can reserve lodging through the links on our website, too.
