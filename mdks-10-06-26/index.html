4
What termites leave behind, and where to look
Useful customer education
▾
Termite signs are the question every San Angelo homeowner has and the blog already answers it, so on camera it earns the follow and puts the owner in front of the people who will need an inspection.
Spot A
When should you start looking for termites? Before the damage shows up. Termites don't take a break in the winter. In a warm place like San Angelo they stay busy all year. They work around the clock, inside the wood, where you can't see them. So the best time to check your house is when you don't think anything is wrong.
Spot B
Walk around your foundation and look for mud tubes. They're pencil sized tunnels made of soil, running up the concrete or along a wall. Then check the windowsills and doorways for little piles of wings. They're clear, they look like fish scales, and you'll find them in spring or after a rain. Then tap the wood. If it sounds hollow or papery, something has been eating it from the inside.
Spot C
Near a door or window, look for tiny pellets that look like coffee grounds or sawdust. That's frass, the droppings from drywood termites. If you find any of it, take a picture and call someone for an inspection. Don't treat it yourself, because that can make the main colony harder to find. When we inspect, we go through the foundation, the crawl space, the attic, and every wooden structure on the property. Most homeowner's insurance policies don't cover termite damage, so finding it early is up to you.
