1
I've lived in thirty seven different houses
The origin and the face
▾
His moving history and his years in a classroom are the whole pitch for a buyer who is nervous about a move. It also gives a recognizable face to the 126 client testimonials already on his page.
Spot A
I've lived in thirty seven different houses. My dad spent twenty nine years in the Air Force, so I grew up a service brat, moving all over the country. That means I know what it's like to haul your family into a new city, in a state you've never even seen. I think I understand moving. Believe me, I've had plenty of practice at it.
Spot B
Before real estate, I spent thirty one years coaching high school basketball. I taught history, government, economics and English. I coached at seven different high schools along the way. I've seen more high school gyms than most people have seen restaurants. Some of them smelled better than others. Teaching teenagers taught me to explain things plainly, and to keep my word when I give it. Those two habits still do most of the work.
Spot C
Now I sell homes in San Angelo with the Lance Powell Team at Coldwell Banker Legacy. [OWNER: when and why you went from the classroom to real estate] I'm just an average guy, Call me Scott. I love this town. It's a big small town. If you're moving here, I know what that day feels like. Come ask me anything, and I'll tell you the truth about this place.
2
San Angelo got an air force base for a dollar a year
The local anchor
▾
A history teacher telling the story behind Goodfellow is exactly his voice. Newcomers with military orders and longtime locals both pass it along, and it sets up his moving expertise without a pitch.
Spot C
Okay, pop quiz from your old history teacher. How did San Angelo end up with an air force base. It goes back to 1940, when the country was getting ready for a war it hadn't joined yet. President Roosevelt wanted pilot training fields, and the town's leaders went after one. They didn't wait to be asked. They made an offer the Army Air Corps could hardly turn down.
Spot A
They offered utility hookups, a railroad spur, and a fifty year lease on six hundred and forty acres. The rent was one dollar a year. That field opened in August of 1940 as a flying school. It's named for John Goodfellow Jr., a San Angelo man who was killed in France in World War I.
Spot B
Flying training stopped in 1958, after almost twenty thousand pilots and cadets had come through. Since that fall, the base has run a school for cryptology, which is code breaking, for all the military services. So it's an air force base with no planes. I like to tell people that when they move here, because it surprises them. It's one of the more unusual things about this town, and I think it's great. If you're coming in on orders, welcome. You'll fit in.
3
Ask your agent who shows up when you're three states away
The thing the big competitor will not say
▾
Built from his own reviews, where out of state buyers praise him for being their man on the scene and for working a holiday weekend. It is the sharpest of the four because it hands a buyer a test, with no competitor named.
Spot B
If you're buying a house in a town you've never lived in, ask your agent one question. Who is going to be there for me when I can't be there. It sounds simple, but it decides whether the move goes smooth or sours. I've lived in thirty seven houses, and I can tell you the worst part of a move is the part you can't see from far away.
Spot A
I've had folks tell me their last agent went quiet on them, while their email filled up with listings they couldn't afford. A listing alert is just a computer sending mail. One buyer was shopping from Pennsylvania, a three day drive from here, and what he needed was somebody on the scene. So that's what I did for him. I looked at the places, told him what I saw, and let him make the call.
Spot C
I've shown houses on a holiday weekend, because that was the only time a family had. I've kept in touch on weekends while offers were coming in. I say that because clients have told me it mattered, and it's the part of this job I'm proudest of. So ask the question of whoever you hire. If the answer takes a long time to come, you have your answer. And if you ask me, I'll tell you exactly what I do.
4
What thirty seven moves taught me to do first
The useful one
▾
Practical and free, and it comes straight from the life he describes on his own page. Anybody about to move can use it, and it makes him the person they remember when the time comes.
Spot A
After thirty seven houses, I have a few rules. First, visit the town before you pick the house. Stay a night. Go on a weekday and go again on a weekend. See what the traffic does and what the neighbors do. Second, if you have kids, find the school before the street. Ask other parents what they think of it. I spent thirty one years in high schools, and I'll tell you that matters.
Spot C
Third, find out how the place you're moving to keeps its own calendar. In San Angelo, that's the stock show and rodeo, the Fourth of July concert at the Riverstage, and the painted sheep statues scattered around town. [OWNER: the one thing you do on move in day that you learned the hard way] It makes a new town feel like yours a lot faster.
Spot B
Fourth, don't try to see every house in one day. Pick a few, sleep on it, and look again. You will see things the second time that the first time hid. A home you'll live in for years deserves a second look. I tell families to start earlier than they think they need to. [OWNER: your rule for how far ahead to start looking] I'm Scott Alexander with Coldwell Banker Legacy, and if you're about to make your next move, call me.
