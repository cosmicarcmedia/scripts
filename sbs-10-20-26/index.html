1
I used to straighten the CDs at Walmart. Then I got certified.
The origin and the face
▾
A client lets someone into their closets and their paperwork, so the story of a kid who rearranged the CD racks and grew into the only Certified KonMari Consultant in West Texas is what makes a stranger feel safe saying yes to the first call.
Spot A
When I was a kid, I'd find myself organizing the music CDs at our local Walmart. In the entertainment section. Typical kid stuff, right? At home, I rearranged the little things on my nightstand, like a window dresser styling a shop window, until everything looked just so. It made me happy, and I couldn't get enough.
Spot B
I was glued to the before and afters on Trading Spaces, Hoarders, Extreme Makeover, What Not to Wear and Queer Eye. I fell in love with transformation, and that love has stayed with me my whole life. I went on to earn a Bachelor of Fine Arts. I worked as a photographer, a graphic designer, an art director and a dance fitness instructor. I loved making beautiful, meaningful work, and I wanted to make things better.
Spot C
Then I discovered Marie Kondo's book. Her method matched how I already thought about spaces, so I got certified. I was trained by Marie Kondo's organization, and I'm the only Certified KonMari Consultant in West Texas. [OWNER: the first home you ever organized for someone else] Organizing is helping people transform their spaces to match the lives they want to lead. When your spaces are organized, your mind is free to focus on what matters. If you are ready, call me. The first call is free.

You know exactly which spaces need help. The pile of paperwork you've been meaning to sort for months. The closet where you already know what you'd throw out. The corner that quietly became the put it here for now zone. And the junk drawer that spilled over into the other drawers.

You walk past those spaces knowing you will get to them eventually, and something more urgent always comes first. 3
What to ask before you hire a home organizer
The thing the big competitor will not say
▾
There is no local rival to name, so the contrast is a set of fair questions about cleaning first, who does the work, who trained her and what the price is, and her answers are already printed on her site.
Spot C
Before you hire anyone to organize your home, ask a few things. Do I have to clean first? With me, you don't. Will you do this for me, or with me? I work side by side with you, sorting, editing and making decisions at a pace that works for you. Hands-off projects are possible on a select basis, and we'd talk about that on a call.
Spot A
Ask what method they use. Mine is the KonMari Method, keeping only what sparks joy. Ask who trained them. I was trained by Marie Kondo's organization. Ask what it costs before the first visit. My prices are on my website, and the discovery call is free. A starter session is four hours, good for one small area. Fifteen hours covers several smaller spaces, or one big one like a garage. Thirty and up is a whole home reset.
Spot B
Ask what's left when they leave. I set up simple systems by category, so everything has a home, and the space stays livable long after I leave. Ask whether anyone is going to judge your closet. Nobody here is. [OWNER: what you say when a client apologizes for the mess] Ask how fast they'll get back to you. I reply within 48 hours. Ask the same questions of anyone else you call. Then call me.
4
How to organize a kid's room so it stays that way
The useful one
▾
Three client reviews already describe how she works in kids' rooms, so this one hands parents a plan they can start tonight and shows what an hour with her looks like.
Spot A
Here's what I've learned working in kids' rooms. Start by letting the kid in on it. One mom was planning a back to school clean of her daughter's room, and I worked on it with her daughter. Two months later, that little girl still knew where everything goes.
Spot B
Start with one small area. A single drawer is a fine place to begin. Sort first, and buy nothing yet. Plushies, clothes, toys. Decide what stays. Ask your kid which ones they love, and keep those. Containers come after the decisions. Then build a system your kids will follow. Group things by category. The art supplies together, the pencils and brushes, the sketchbooks and coloring books. Once everything has a home, the room stays livable.
Spot C
Keep it fun. One mom wrote that making it fun is why nobody ended up frustrated. Go at a pace that works for the kid. Bring patience. Kids decide slower than adults, and that's fine. The system should be simple enough that a kid can follow it. [OWNER: your own best tip for keeping a kid room from sliding back] Not sure where to start? The first call is free, and we can figure it out together. If your own closet is next, ask me. One mom said I had good ideas for hers too.
