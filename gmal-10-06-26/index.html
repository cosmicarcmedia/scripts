2
Six counties, six courthouses
Local anchor
▾
A lawyer who covers six counties is a local thing worth knowing, and each county seat carries a story of its own. Naming them tells a Concho Valley viewer this is their lawyer, and it sets up the deed video, since land records are kept in the county where the land sits.
Spot B
I take cases in six counties, and every one of them has a courthouse. Tom Green County's is on West Beauregard in San Angelo, and it was built in 1928. San Angelo was first called Over the River. The county's first seat was a town called Ben Ficklin, until a flood washed it away in 1882. Irion County's is in Mertzon, where voters picked the county seat in 1936, by a vote of 453 to 222. The old seat, Sherwood, faded after the railroad bypassed it.
Spot A
In Coke County, the seat is Robert Lee. Fort Chadbourne was established in that county in 1851, and a dam finished in 1969 created the E. V. Spence Reservoir. Concho County is named for the Concho River, and the river is named for the mussel shells found in it. The county seat is Paint Rock, about a mile from some of the most noted Indian pictographs in Texas. Paint Rock built a permanent courthouse in 1886.
Spot C
Schleicher County has Eldorado. In Runnels County, it's Ballinger, which won the county seat in 1888 after the railroad came through. It replaced the old seat, Runnels City. Each of those courthouses has a county clerk, and the clerk keeps the land records for that county. My office is at 331 West Avenue B in San Angelo. If you have a legal matter in any of those six counties, call my office.
3
What no lawyer should promise you
Competitor contrast
▾
Legal advertising is full of confident promises, and the honest answer is that nobody can promise how a case ends. This turns his own stated goal, a realistic overview and what to expect, into the sharpest line on his page without naming a firm or touching a fee.
Spot C
No lawyer should promise you how your case will end. Nobody can know that on the first day. A legal problem can feel overwhelming, especially when it's about your family, your property, or your estate. What I can give you is a realistic overview of your situation and what to expect. I put that on my website, and it's the way I work.
Spot A
I treat every client the way I would want to be treated. I work to provide upfront and honest service to every client. That starts with an honest conversation about where you stand. I'll explain your options in plain language, and I'll walk you through what the legal process looks like, because deadlines and paperwork can be hard to handle on your own. If you're about to sign a contract, have somebody review it first. Contracts can have terms that are easy to miss.
Spot B
Before you hire any lawyer, ask them what to expect. Ask how the process works, step by step. Ask who you'll be talking to along the way. Ask how fees work before you sign anything. A lawyer who is comfortable with those questions will answer them. I'm glad to answer them, and my office is at 331 West Avenue B in San Angelo.
4
When the landowner passes, the deed stays the same
Useful customer education
▾
Families in this part of Texas hold land and houses through generations, and the old name on a deed trips up more of them than most people expect. This is general information from the State Law Library, free to give away, and it sits in the middle of his wills, probate and real estate work.
Spot A
When somebody who owns land passes away, the name on the deed doesn't change by itself. A ranch, a house, a lot in town, it all stays in the old name until the right papers are filed. The county can't add your name to the old deed, and it can't issue a new deed with your name on it.
Spot B
A deed is the document that transfers ownership. Title is the ownership itself, the right to use, rent or sell the property. So heirs can own the property and still have a paper trail that doesn't show it. To prove it, they file certain documents with the county clerk. That's the clerk in the county where the property is located. Depending on the situation, that can mean an affidavit of death with a death certificate, or a court order admitting the will to probate.
Spot C
Some families use a special deed, called an enhanced life estate deed or a Lady Bird deed, to say who gets the property when the owner dies. For me, the property could be in Tom Green, Irion, Coke, Concho, Schleicher or Runnels County. Which papers you need depends on how the property was passed down. Every family's situation is different, so this is general information only. For advice on your own land or house, talk to a lawyer. You can talk to me, and my number is on my website.
