4
The 600 dollar rule every small business owner should know
The useful one
▾
The FAQ answers a question small business owners ask every January: who gets a 1099, and what to collect first. Costs nothing to give away.
Spot A
If you pay people to do work for your business, you may owe them a 1099. Here's the rule I want every small business owner to know. If you pay an individual or a business $600 or more in one calendar year, you send them a 1099. That commonly applies to rent, contract labor and repairs. Rent counts. Contract labor counts. Repairs count.
Spot B
Before you pay them, get a W-9. It has their legal name as it appears on their tax return. It has their mailing address. And it has either a federal ID number, if they're a business, or a Social Security number, if they're an individual. You can find the W-9 form on the IRS website. Getting it up front saves you from chasing it down in January.
Spot C
Those 1099 forms generally have to be mailed to the people you paid by January 31. Keep your records for three years plus the current year. Some people keep five years. Three is the minimum we recommend. While you're marking calendars, partnerships and S corporations are due March 15, or September 15 with an extension. Those dates can change if your year-end is different. Every business is different, so if you're unsure whether somebody needs a 1099, call us. We're on Executive Drive, at 325 944 9447.
