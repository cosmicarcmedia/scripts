3
Anybody can sell you a garage door. Ask who's installing it.
The thing the big competitor will not say
▾
The accreditation and the two master technicians are both claimed on the company's own homepage. This script turns a line buried on a 2017 style website into the question a homeowner should ask before hiring anyone.
Spot A
Anybody can sell you a garage door. The harder question is who's going to put it up. Here's something most people never ask a door company. Are you accredited? We are, through the Institute of Door Dealer Education and Accreditation. And we're the only dealer in the Concho Valley that is.
Spot B
That accreditation is the highest level of professionalism the door and operator industry awards. It says our company has been looked at by the industry itself. We also have two master technicians. The first two in the nation. That's a title we're proud of, and it took work. [OWNER: the names of your two master technicians and what it took to earn the title]
Spot C
Why does that matter to you? Because a garage door is the largest moving part of your house. It's heavy, and it has a lot of tension in it. You want somebody who has been trained on it. If you're comparing quotes, ask each company the same two questions. Are you accredited, and who is doing the install? Ask who will be at your house, and what training they've had. Write down what they tell you. Then call us and compare. We'll be glad to answer both questions the same way.
4
Three things to check before you buy an opener
The useful one
▾
Every product detail here, the optional battery backup, the lift height and the wind rated models, is on the company's own opener page. It gives a homeowner something to use tonight and costs the company nothing to say.
Spot B
If your garage door opener is on its way out, here are the things I'd check before you buy a new one. First, what happens when the power goes out? Some openers offer an optional battery backup, so the door still works during an outage. If you've ever been stuck outside your own garage in a storm, you know why that matters. Ask whether the one you're looking at has it.
Spot C
Second, how tall and heavy is your door? If you have a big door, or a carriage house door, or one that catches a lot of wind, you want an opener rated for it. Some are built for doors up to fourteen feet high. Ask what yours is rated for before you buy it.
Spot A
Third, ask about the light and the speed. A lot of newer openers have built in LED lighting, and some let you set how fast the door travels. Those are small things you'll notice every day. [OWNER: the one thing that fails first on an old opener, and how a customer can spot it] If you're not sure what you need, call us. We'll tell you what makes sense for your door.
