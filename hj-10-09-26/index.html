1
An accounting major who ended up at the jeweler's bench
The origin and the face
▾
The History page names the current owner, Brant Horner, who arrived in 1985 and worked his way to Master Jeweler before taking over a 1918 family store. That hand-off from the Holland family to a jeweler is the face this century old name does not have on its site.
Spot B
Holland's Jewelry started in 1918. That year, Chase Holland Senior bought a jewelry store from a Mr. C. C. McBurnett and put his own name on it. The store goes back even further than that. Its owners can be traced all the way to 1891. Back then we were known for fine silver, china and crystal, as well as jewelry and watches. Chase Junior came home from the Army Air Corps after World War Two. He and his wife Virginia began managing the company in 1960.
Spot C
I came to Holland's in 1985. I have an accounting degree from Angelo State. But I ended up at the jeweler's bench, and I became a Master Jeweler. Die making, hand carving, stone setting, hand engraving, and modern rapid prototyping for custom pieces. [OWNER: how an accounting major ended up at the bench]
Spot A
In 2012, I became a business partner with the Holland brothers. After we celebrated our hundredth year, Bill Holland and Chase Holland the Third retired, and I took over the store. My daughter, Evyn, started working here in 2011. Many of our customers still remember Mrs. Virginia Holland, who was a major staple in this community. The family run tradition continues. We belong to the American Gem Society and Jewelers of America. Come by 501 West Beauregard and say hello. I'd like to meet you.
2
Pearls from West Texas rivers and a spur for a president
The local anchor
▾
The Concho pearl and the 1936 Spur Clip are both on the store's own pages, and they tie a San Angelo jeweler to the river and the cowboy history of the region. A national mall chain has no story like either one.
Spot A
Did you know the rivers of West Texas grow pearls? Freshwater mussels in our rivers produce natural pearls, in pastel shades from pink to deep purple. Once in a while, one comes out round and perfect, with beautiful color. Those are the Concho pearls. The rivers around here have been home to those mussels for centuries. They're rare, and each piece we make with one is one of a kind.
Spot B
We set them in sterling silver, and in white, yellow or rose gold. Our shell pendants mimic the form of how the pearl is found. We also have loose pearls if you want something custom designed just for you. [OWNER: the most unusual Concho pearl you have held, and where it came from]
Spot C
And then there's the spur. In 1936, Chase Holland Senior patented a tiny sterling silver spur tie clasp. It was a gift for someone who had earned their spurs. The country was just coming out of the Depression. Two of the very first clips went to President Franklin Roosevelt and Vice President John Nance Garner. That clip started us making our own jewelry. Hand crafted buckles, spur jewelry, hand engraved pieces, many of them still in production. We still make hand crafted spur jewelry today. That's West Texas, right on West Beauregard.
3
Ask where your ring goes when you leave it
The thing the big competitor will not say
▾
The site says every repair is done in house with nothing shipped off, and that cleaning and inspection are free, and a reviewer complained about the opposite experience somewhere else. This script answers the question a nervous ring owner is thinking at a mall counter.
Spot C
Before you leave a ring with anyone, ask one question. Where is it going? And who is going to touch it? At Holland's, every service is done in house. We don't ship off any personal items. From ring sizing to a full restoration, it happens right here, at the bench. We repair watches here too. From a battery to a full restoration, it's done in this building.
Spot A
We repair and restore all types of jewelry. That includes pieces you bought from another store. Bring it in and get an estimate before any work starts. And if you only want it looked at, we give your jewelry a thorough cleaning and inspection, free of charge. We'll also teach you how to take care of it so it lasts.
Spot B
If you want something made, the first step is free. Send us your inspiration, a photo or a feeling. We sketch your ring and give you a quote. Then we build it to your specifications. Payment is due when you pick it up. There's a ring sizing option for arthritic knuckles too. Ask every jeweler what happens after you hand it over, and come ask us. We belong to the American Gem Society, Jewelers of America and the Better Business Bureau.
4
When did a jeweler last look at your ring?
The useful one
▾
Insurance appraisals, free inspections and pearl restringing are all listed on the store's own services page. It reminds an engaged or married customer of a chore they have probably put off, and it costs the store nothing to teach.
Spot B
Here's a question for anybody who owns a ring. When did a jeweler last look at it? A ring gets worn every single day. Stones loosen and prongs wear. We do stone tightening, prong re-tipping and stone replacement, but it all starts with a jeweler checking for it. [OWNER: the first thing you check on a ring during an inspection]
Spot C
Second question. Is it insured for what it's worth? An appraisal is how you protect an investment. Whether it's a single ring or a complete estate collection, we can appraise it and evaluate it for insurance or for replacement. If something happens to it, that piece of paper matters. We can do that for a single piece, or for everything in the jewelry box. One customer had a wedding set appraised with us and said the ring looked better afterward.
Spot A
And third, pearls. If you have a strand that hasn't been touched in years, we restring pearls here too. We also size watch links and replace batteries, and we can do a full restoration on a watch. If you bring something in, we'll clean it and inspect it for free. We do insurance replacement work too. The store is open Monday through Saturday, and Saturdays we close at four.
