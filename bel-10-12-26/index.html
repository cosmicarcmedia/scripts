1
Yards around San Angelo since 1988
The origin and the face
▾
A landscaper who has been working the same town since 1988 and was voted among the Best of the Best in 2010 has a track record most newcomers cannot match, and it is sitting on his own homepage. This puts it on camera with the owner's face on it.
Spot A
We've been creating yards around San Angelo since 1988. I'm Brent Pfingsten, and I own Bird's Eye Landscape. [OWNER: how you got into landscaping and what you were doing before] Some of what we do is plain work, and I'm proud of it. Mowing. Edging. Hedge trimming. Flower beds. Irrigation repair. Tree trimming. Sod. We take pride in how the lawns we maintain look.
Spot B
Some of what we do is design. Xeriscape, which is low water landscaping built with stone and tough plants. Or a traditional yard, if that's the look you want. We can fix up the landscape you have, or we can start over from scratch to your specifications. Stone retaining walls, patios, and walkways, too. Tree trimming for health and safety, and to open a yard up. We haul away the debris. [OWNER: the project you're proudest of]
Spot C
In 2010, we were voted among the Best of the Best in San Angelo. That came from creative, quality work at reasonable rates. That's still what we aim for. We handle small yards and big ones. Lawn maintenance is on flexible price agreements. We'll mow it, rebuild it, or both. We handle offices and commercial properties on a regular maintenance program. Our address is 4977 Welch Road. If you want to talk about your yard, call 325 656 1915. The office line is 325 651 2494.
2
Ice Cream Rock, Bull Rock and Pecos Rock
The local anchor
▾
The rock names and sizes on his own website are specific, a little funny, and unmistakably West Texas. A customer who watches this knows exactly what they can pick from before they call.
Spot B
Here are some of the rock names on our list. Big Ice Cream Rock, one to three inches. Small Ice Cream Rock, one inch only. Yes, those are real names. Don't ask me who came up with them. Pecos Rock, one to two inches. Bull Rock, the brown kind, four to six inches. Brown Rock, one to two.
Spot A
Pink granite gravel, a half inch to an inch. Crushed granite. And Slate Black Rock, two to four inches. Natural stone slabs and boulders, too, in all sizes and shapes. I know, it sounds like a menu. We carry a wide selection of stone and plants so you can choose what fits. Picking the rock is half of what makes a yard yours, and each one looks different once it's down.
Spot C
West Texas doesn't hand out much water. Lawns and gardens need a lot of upkeep all year, and a yard that drinks a lot of water costs a lot to keep. That's why people come to us for xeriscape. It's a yard designed for dry country, with stone and tough plants and less upkeep. Boulders, rock, yuccas, palms, and cacti. It can still have color. [OWNER: how a customer picks a rock with you, in the yard or at the pile] Come tell me what you want your yard to look like, and we'll start there.
3
I mow lawns for a living, and I'll still tell you to cut back on grass
The thing the big competitor will not say
▾
A 2013 cost study of San Angelo yards found that any yard with some xeriscape beat an all grass yard on cost efficiency. An owner who also mows for a living can say that credibly, and a national lawn company has little reason to lead with it.
Spot A
I mow lawns for a living, and here's what I tell people. Have less lawn. A 2013 cost study looked at yards in San Angelo. It compared grass lawns to xeriscape, the low water kind with rock and tough plants. A yard with no grass at all came out the most cost efficient. And any yard with even a small xeriscape section beat an all grass yard. Our website says it the same way. Lawns and gardens use a large amount of precious West Texas water.
Spot B
Now, we do mow. Edging, hedges, flower bed cleaning, fertilizing, irrigation checks, even gutter cleaning. It's all on our list. But if you're paying to keep a lot of grass alive in West Texas, ask what that does to your water bill. Ask anybody. Then ask me. If you love your lawn, keep it, and we'll mow it. Just know what it costs.
Spot C
Our maintenance agreements are flexible. You can have mowing only. Or mowing, edging, fertilizing, flower beds, and an irrigation check. You pick what goes in the price. We also take care of offices, big or small, on a regular maintenance program. Flower beds with color change outs for the annuals, too. And when we trim a tree, we haul the debris away, so all you do is enjoy the new look. [OWNER: a yard you turned partly to rock, and what happened to the customer's water bill]
4
How to pick the right rock for your yard
The useful one
▾
Everyone who thinks about xeriscape ends up standing in front of a pile of rock with no idea what to choose. Walking through it by size and use costs nothing to give away, and uses the exact rock names on his own page.
Spot B
Choosing rock for a yard sounds simple until you're standing at the pile. Start with how you'll use the space. If people are going to walk on it, go small. Kids, dogs, and bare feet all want something smaller. Pink granite gravel runs a half inch to an inch, and small rock is easier on the feet.
Spot C
If you want rock to cover ground and look full, go medium. The Ice Cream Rock is one to three inches. The Pecos Rock is one to two. If you want a statement, go big. Bull Rock runs four to six inches, and you can't ignore it. Boulders and slabs for retaining walls, patios, and walkways are stonework. That's a different job, but the same idea. Natural stone comes in all sizes and shapes, so you have room to choose.
Spot A
Then match the color to your house. Pink, brown, or black. Slate Black Rock runs two to four inches. Walk around the yard first and look at what's already there, the brick, the trim, and the dirt. [OWNER: the rock combination you recommend most] A xeriscape can still have color, so ask about desert plants too. And look at a handful in daylight. Rock looks different wet, dry, in shade, and in July sun.
