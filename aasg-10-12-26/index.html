1
Twenty years of San Angelo signs, and still locally owned
The origin and the face
▾
The site never says who owns the shop, and a sign company with 20 years in town should be easy to put a face on. Opening on the person who runs it, the 2001 start and the customers who have stayed for decades gives the next business owner a reason to call someone they can picture.
Spot A
[OWNER: your name, and how you came to own All About Signs] This shop has been making signs in San Angelo since 2001. We're locally and privately owned. We say we're the only locally owned, full service sign shop in San Angelo. That's on our front page, and we stand behind it. [OWNER: one thing you changed or kept when you took over]
Spot B
We employ our own designers, and our own licensed electricians. That's how a sketch of a new LED sign turns into a lit sign on a pole, without handing it off to somebody else. We've worked out of Sherwood Way, and today we're out on Old Ballinger Highway. Banners, vinyl, magnets, flat letters. Pylon signs, channel letters, digital message centers. We even do custom megaphones for local teams and window decals for schools.
Spot C
We work for businesses in San Angelo, Midland, Odessa, Abilene, Del Rio, and Fort Stockton. A local tire shop wrote that we've been a huge asset, and very reasonably priced. A customer who has used us for decades wrote that our team will help with ideas and recommendations to match your vision. I'd rather point to the work and the customers than write a slogan. We have a one hundred percent satisfaction guarantee, and over four hundred products on the list. Whatever your business needs on a wall, a pole, or a truck, start with us.
2
West Texas wind and sun are hard on a sign
The local anchor
▾
The two things every San Angelo business owner already knows about the weather are the two things the shop's materials and wrap bay are built around. A video that talks about wind and sun puts the shop on the owner's own street.
Spot B
The two things that are hardest on a sign out here are wind and sun. The sun fades a sign face, and over time it can warp and crack it. The wind is why we do storm and wind damage repair. We handle rapid-response structural repairs and cosmetic ones when the weather comes through. [OWNER: a storm or windy week you remember, and what came in after]
Spot C
That's why we build signs for this weather. Monument signs, pylon signs, channel letters, lighted cabinets, all engineered to handle West Texas wind and sun. For vehicle graphics we use a UV-laminated cast vinyl. Cheaper vinyl shrinks in the sun, and it can fade inside a year. We would rather put the better film on your truck the first time. Our designers lay out graphics on exact model templates, so your logo doesn't land under a door handle. We seal the edges carefully, and the film comes off later without ruining the factory paint.
Spot A
We put your wrap on inside a clean, controlled bay. Out in an open carport, wind and dust get under the vinyl, and you end up with bubbles and lifting edges. We cover San Angelo, and we'll drive to Midland, Odessa, Abilene, Del Rio and Fort Stockton. If your sign took a beating this season, call us. We repair commercial signs of any size.
3
Ask your sign company who is doing the electrical
The thing the big competitor will not say
▾
A sign is electrical equipment, and the shop employs three licensed technicians and owns its own crane. Phrasing it as a question any customer can ask any sign company draws a clear line against shops that subcontract that work, and it never names one.
Spot C
Before you hire anybody to build or fix a sign, ask one question. Who is doing the electrical? A sign is electrical equipment. It has wiring, power supplies, and ballasts. If the answer is a subcontractor, ask a couple more questions. What does that cost me, and how long will I wait on their schedule? And how high can your crane reach? Tall pylons and high-rise cabinets need serious equipment.
Spot A
Here, the answer is simple. We have three licensed technicians in house, and a Master Sign Electrician. We own our trucks, and we own an eighty foot crane. An eighty foot crane reaches tall pylon signs and high-rise building cabinets. No middlemen, no rental fees, and no waiting on somebody else's calendar.
Spot B
We also handle the permits. City codes, the red tape, all of it. From the design to the pole, you have one point of contact. One customer had two small projects at two locations, and she wrote that we kept her updated the whole way. Wiring that's done wrong can start a fire, so we do it to code. When you call us, you're talking to the people who do the work. [OWNER: how fast a repair call gets answered, in your experience] Ask the next company those same questions, and see what they say.
4
Look at your sign after dark. Here is what to check.
The useful one
▾
A business owner can check this in five minutes with no tools and no cost, and every item comes from the shop's own repair page. It earns trust with the owner who has a flickering letter right now, and it leads to a repair call.
Spot A
Tonight, walk outside after dark and look at your own sign. Is every letter lit? Is anything flickering? A flicker can mean a short or a dead circuit. That's an electrical problem, and it's one for a licensed electrician. A dark or broken sign turns customers away. In a busy market like San Angelo, your sign is often the first thing a customer sees of your business.
Spot B
Next, look at the face of the sign. Is it faded, cracked, or weathered? A new face can bring the sign back without replacing the whole cabinet. And if your sign still runs old neon or fluorescent tubes, LED can replace them. LED systems use less energy, and they cost less to maintain over time. If you have vintage neon you want to keep, we can troubleshoot that too.
Spot C
Then look at your parking lot. Pole lights and wall packs count. A well lit lot is safer for your customers after dark. Power supplies and ballasts wear out too, and replacing them can stop the same outage from coming back. Leave the high work to a crew with the right truck. A pylon is a long way up, and that takes a bucket truck or a crane.
