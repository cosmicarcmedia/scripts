4
Before you open a pressure vessel, read this
Useful customer education
▾
Every step comes from the company's own operations and maintenance page, so it gives away real, safe guidance that a plant operator or facility manager can use on any tank. It shows the shop answers questions after the sale.
You've got a pressure vessel with a manway or an inspection port. Here's how to look inside it the right way. First, shut it down. Isolate the feed lines and drain the vessel. Make sure all the pressure is relieved before you take off any opening. Once a year, open it up. Flush out the sediment and check the inside for corrosion. If it has a lining, look for damage. If it has magnesium anodes, check whether they need replacing. One thing people forget. If the tank has an epoxy lining, inspectors should go in wearing socks. Shoes, boots and anything abrasive can damage the lining. Know your lining. Ours come in hot dip galvanized, Epoxoline and Cementline. Put it back together with new gaskets. Clean the gasket surfaces, tighten the fasteners, and check for leaks. Refill with cold water. Check that the relief valve moves freely, and replace it if it doesn't. Then bring back the heating medium and circulation, and confirm the controls work. And if it's epoxy lined, connect it with dielectric isolators. They keep dissimilar metals apart. Never run it above the working pressure stamped on the tank. That voids the warranty. If you ever need to make a claim, have the serial number, the date manufactured and the date installed ready. That's general guidance. For your tank, read the manual or call us.
