3
We charged exactly what we quoted
Competitor contrast

One customer review says out loud what every homeowner fears about a low bid, and putting the owner behind it gives L and M the sharpest of the four scripts.

You get three bids for a panel upgrade, and one of them comes in low. Then the work starts, and the number starts to climb. One of our customers wrote that they'd been down that road before. They said contractors bid low to get the job, then nickel and dime you at the end.

And we had the work done in a day. They also wrote that we showed up on time and treated their home with respect. Spot B
We arrive on time and in uniform, and we talk through what you need before we pick up a tool. Our estimates are transparent, so you can see what fair pricing looks like. If you're getting bids for electrical work, ask every electrician the same question. What will the final bill look like if nothing goes wrong? If you can't get a straight answer at your own kitchen table, get another quote.
4
The warm wall plate and the breaker that keeps tripping
Useful customer education
▾
The FAQ page already answers when a panel needs help and what a hot outlet means, so on camera it earns the follow and tells every homeowner with a tripping breaker who to call.
Spot A
Breakers that keep tripping. Lights that flicker. A panel that's warm to the touch. Which of those is your house doing? It's easy to reset the breaker and move on. Each one of those is a reason to have the panel looked at.
Spot B
Older San Angelo homes often have a hundred amp service. Modern appliances, air conditioners, and EV chargers can need two hundred. Now walk through the house and touch your wall plates. If a switch or an outlet is hot, or the plate has changed color, you could have an overloaded circuit or loose wiring. That kind of arcing makes heat, and heat can start a house fire.
Spot C
If you find one, turn the power off at the breaker and call for a safety inspection. And if you ever smell something burning at an outlet, or see a panel spark, don't wait for an appointment. One more thing to look at is your GFCI outlets. They're required in kitchens, bathrooms, garages, and outdoors. They cut the power in an instant if moisture gets into the circuit. If yours don't have them, ask a licensed electrician. And if you're thinking about a backup generator, a licensed electrician has to install the transfer switch. For anything beyond changing a light bulb, hire one.
