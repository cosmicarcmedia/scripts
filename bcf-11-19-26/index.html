1
Everything was already here when we walked the property
The origin and the face
▾
A new owner introduces the place the way they found it, silo and donkeys included, and says out loud that they are still listening. That is the first thing a guest wants to know about a farm stay.
We took ownership of Bee Creek Farm in April of 2026. It still feels like we are holding our breath. [OWNER: your first names, and where you came from] When we walked the property, everything was already here. The farmhouse. The silo with a hand-painted bee on it. Two container cabins. And two donkeys, Rocky and Harry, out in the wildflower field. So our job is to listen to the place. We want to refine it slowly, in the directions it is already going. We bought these 32 acres because the Hill Country has a way of stretching time out. We wanted somewhere to share that with people. The farmhouse is our home, so you will see us. You will see the dogs napping on the porch, too. Rocky is a spotted mini donkey who is convinced he is in charge. Harry is full size and steady, and he decides when something is worth getting up for. He usually decides it is not. That is the whole crew that came with the farm. We are still deciding what comes next, and we are not in a hurry. It is a working farm, and we are learning it one season at a time. [OWNER: the first thing that surprised you about running it] Pull up a porch chair. Stay a couple of nights. We will leave the lights on.
2
Ten minutes from the square where the Old Yeller author grew up
The local anchor
▾
It makes Mason, and the farm's place in it, a reason to book a weekend. Everything here is already on the owners' own site, so guests hear a local say it in their own words.
We are ten minutes from Mason Town Square. The 1909 Mason County Courthouse sits in the middle of it. There are antique stores around it, and a soda fountain. The Odeon Theater is still showing movies. Out front is a bronze Old Yeller statue, because author Fred Gipson grew up here. On Saturdays, Old Yeller's Market is on the square from nine to four. Crafts, antiques and Hill Country food. [OWNER: your favorite thing to buy there] Twenty five minutes away is the Eckert James River Bat Cave. Around four million Mexican free-tailed bats live there from May through October. At dusk they stream out in a ribbon that takes more than thirty minutes to clear the cave. Mason County is also the only place in Texas where blue topaz is found in the wild. That is the state gem. Several local ranches open their land for topaz hunting, and whatever you find is yours to keep. Peters Prairie Vineyard is about twenty five minutes away, with a tasting room Thursday through Sunday from mid-May through October. Fifteen minutes east is the Llano River. It is cold, clear and slow, and a good place to wade up to your knees and stay there. Stay two nights and you will fill both days.
3
You can see where your host lives
The thing the big competitor will not say
▾
A guest booking a stay usually never meets the person behind the listing. This puts the owners on the property in plain view, and says how to book direct, which a platform will not tell anyone.
When you book a stay with us, you can see where your host lives. The farmhouse is our home. You can see it from both cabins. Come say hi. [OWNER: how you greet guests when they arrive] Both stays have a full kitchen, a BBQ, and fast Wi-Fi. Big Bend is two teal-blue containers joined by a covered deck. It has two bedrooms, three beds and one bath, and it sleeps up to six. Nocona is a black container with red door accents. It has a patio, a fire pit, one bedroom, and it sleeps up to four. You can book on Airbnb. You can also send us a note on our site. Booking direct sometimes saves you the platform fees. We get back to you within a day. Both stays sit on 32 acres along Bee Creek, so the whole property is your backyard. Best wildflower season is late March through May. Then there are the neighbors. Rocky is a spotted mini donkey who will greet you first. Harry is full size, calm, and patient with first-timers. Bring carrots. They are not formal therapy animals, and we will give you guidelines for saying hello safely. That is a farm stay, with the people who own the farm.
4
Three things to book ahead before a Hill Country weekend
The useful one
▾
Texas day-use entry and bat season trip up most Mason visitors. Giving the dates up front makes the farm the trusted local, and every fact comes off the owners' own site.
If you are coming to the Hill Country for a weekend, three things need planning ahead. First is Enchanted Rock. It is about forty five minutes south of us, a pink granite dome four hundred twenty five feet tall. Day-use entry has a daily cap, and it fills up by mid-morning on weekends. Reserve a slot ahead through Texas Parks and Wildlife. Bring water, because there is no shade on the dome. Second is the bat cave. Arrive an hour before sunset. Bring water, bug spray, a folding chair, and cash for entry. Check the days before you go, because it is only open Thursday through Sunday in season. Third is topaz hunting. Most hunts are by appointment, so call ahead. It is best in the cooler months, October through April. Wear closed-toe shoes and bring sun protection. Fourth is the Llano River. Public access is at the highway crossings on US 87 and FM 1900. Pack a sandwich and stay all afternoon. Mason Town Square has Old Yeller's Market on Saturdays, so plan around it. One more. Wildflower season runs late March through May. If that is why you are coming, tell us when you are booking. [OWNER: your favorite week for wildflowers] We will tell you what is blooming.
One more idea
The donkeys help

Film Rocky and Harry at the fence with a carrot and let the guest quote be the on screen line. Open on Rocky trotting up first, then cut to Harry deciding it is not worth getting up. Keep the voice over to two sentences.
