3
Ask where the seams will be before anybody cuts your carpet
The thing the big competitor will not say
▾
Seam layout, the pad your warranty requires, a daylight color check, and the plain admission that no carpet is stain proof are all printed on the store's own Shopping Tips and Time to Install pages. They are four fair questions for any flooring seller, and each is hard for a rack and a register to answer.
Spot C
Where will the seams be? Ask that before anybody cuts your carpet. We schedule an appointment to take an exact measurement of your home. At that visit, we give advice on how the floor will be laid out and where every seam will go. We confirm all the final decisions with you, so we're on the same page. You know before the carpet is cut.
Spot A
Second question. Which pad does the warranty require? Manufacturers recommend a specific cushion or pad to satisfy their warranty. So we go over the manufacturer's warranty with you before you buy, along with the care steps they recommend. We'd rather you ask before you buy than after.
Spot B
Third question. Can I see it in my own room? Before our installers get started, have them lay out a small portion of the new floor. Open the blinds, let the daylight in, and see how the color blends with your room. That's the moment to tell us if it's wrong. Fourth question. Is it stain proof? We'll tell you the truth. No carpet is a hundred percent stain proof. One more thing worth asking about is old flooring. Taking it out is normally a courtesy of our installers. Ask the next store all five, and compare.
4
Clean your carpet before it looks dirty
The useful one
▾
Dirt acting as grit on the fibers, and synthetic fibers hiding it, is how Aladdin's own Care and Cleaning page explains it. It costs nothing to share, and anyone who watches it learns why a once a year cleaning exists.
Spot A
Most people wait until their carpet looks dirty to clean it. By then it's late. Under a microscope, ordinary household dirt acts like grit. It wears down the carpet fibers every time somebody walks across them, and that's what makes carpet look dull. Nylon is in almost seventy percent of the carpet made in this country, and like the other synthetics, it hides dirt very well. So you can't see it happening.
Spot B
So do two things. Vacuum regularly. That's your first defense. And have a professional deep cleaning done before you notice you need one. We recommend once a year. Ours pretreats the carpet with a traffic lane cleaner, then uses hot water extraction. When something spills, blot it. Blotting keeps the spill smaller. Our cleaning comes with a hundred percent satisfaction guarantee.
Spot C
The same goes for hard floors. Vinyl, hardwood, tile, and laminate all collect that same grit. Sweep or vacuum often, and wipe spills up quickly with a clean cloth. Put small pads under furniture legs. Mats and area rugs can trap little bits of dirt and rock that scratch the surface. When you move furniture, lift it. Don't push it or roll it. For cleaning, call three two five, four eight six, two two seven three.
