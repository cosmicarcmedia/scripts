City Services

Water, sewer, animal control, and trash. What the city handles, what the county handles, and who to call when something breaks.

Water and Sewer

Starting service

The city provides water and sewer inside the city limits. To start, stop, or transfer service, come by City Hall or call and they will walk you through it.

What to bring

  • A completed application, residential or business
  • Photo identification
  • The service address and the date you want service on
  • The deposit, which City Hall will quote you when you apply

Water Conservation

Stage 1 Drought Contingency Plan

The City Council voted on 6/20/22 to implement Stage 1, mild water shortage conditions. It runs April 1 through October 31 and asks for voluntary conservation.

What Stage 1 asks of you

  • Voluntarily conserve water and follow the restrictions on certain uses from April 1 through October 31.
  • Minimize or discontinue water use for non-essential purposes.
  • The city itself follows the restrictions set out in the Best Management Practices.

What counts as non-essential use

  • Irrigating landscape areas, including parks, athletic fields, rights of way, and medians, except where the plan allows it
  • Washing motor vehicles, motorbikes, boats, trailers, airplanes, or other vehicles
  • Washing down sidewalks, walkways, driveways, parking lots, athletic courts, or other hard surfaces
  • Washing down buildings or structures for anything other than immediate fire protection
  • Flushing gutters, or letting water run or pool in gutters and streets
  • Filling, refilling, or adding to indoor or outdoor swimming pools or Jacuzzi-type pools
  • Running a fountain or pond for looks, except where it is needed to support aquatic life
  • Failing to repair a controllable leak within a reasonable period after being notified

Definition

Conservation

Practices, techniques, and technologies that reduce how much water is consumed, cut loss and waste, improve efficiency, or increase recycling and reuse, so supply is preserved for future or alternative uses.

Animal Control

Pets, strays, and the leash law

Animal control exists to protect pets and wildlife from the dangers they face on the streets, and to protect people and property from animals running loose.

What the program does

  • Operates shelter and adoption programs to protect and rehome unwanted animals
  • Licenses animals so lost pets can be identified and returned
  • Enforces ordinances, including leash laws, vaccination requirements, and humane treatment
  • Investigates cruelty reports and complaints
  • Handles animal rescue, bite investigations, and quarantines
  • Educates the public on responsible pet ownership
Found a stray, or lost a pet? Call City Hall at (325) 835-5791. Fees and impound details are set by ordinance, and City Hall has the current schedule.
