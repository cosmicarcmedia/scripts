2
In February of 2021, San Angelo stayed at or below freezing for 164 hours
The local anchor
▾
The February 2021 freeze set records in San Angelo and broke pipes and water mains across town. Opening on the record numbers tells the town this is about their pipes, then gives them the three steps from the company's own winter post.
Spot B
In February of 2021, San Angelo stayed at or below freezing for a hundred and sixty-four hours. The National Weather Service says that's the longest stretch on record. Ten inches of snow fell on the fourteenth, the most in a single day on record. That Sunday night the city's water system lost pressure, and crews repaired more than a dozen water main breaks. The next day the high was eighteen degrees. The whole day averaged eight and a half, the coldest day on record. The low hit minus one.
Spot A
If you had a leak or a burst pipe, the city could shut off your water feed until it was fixed. Water expands about nine percent when it freezes, and that's what splits a pipe. The pipes most at risk are in the attic, the crawl space, the garage and the exterior walls.
Spot C
Before the next freeze, I'd do three things. Insulate the pipes in those unheated spots. Keep the heat on. If you're leaving town, set the thermostat to at least fifty-five. And find your main water shutoff. Know where it is, know how to turn it, and show everybody in the house. In an emergency, every second counts. Do you know where yours is right now? If a pipe does go, call us.

4
What the City of San Angelo says your water does to your plumbing
The useful one
▾
The City of San Angelo's own water page says hard water is hard on plumbing and water heaters, and it spells out what a softener does and the cautions that come with one. Giving that away earns the follow.
Spot A
Is your skin dry? Is there soap scum or staining in your shower? Is your dishwasher or washer not working like it should? Those are three questions from our website. The City of San Angelo has a page about your tap water. One line on it is about your plumbing. It says water high in hardness is detrimental to plumbing and will reduce the life of water heaters. The city's chart puts San Angelo tap water at eight hundred to fifteen hundred milligrams per liter of dissolved minerals. The Edwards Aquifer on that same chart is fifty to four hundred fifty.
Spot B
A softener goes after that. The city says it typically removes 95 to 99 percent of the calcium and magnesium. It says that extends the life of your plumbing, your water heater and your fixtures, leaves less residue on dishes, and cuts the soap you need for laundry. We install water softeners. We test your water's hardness first, and the install takes a few hours.
Spot C
Three notes, and they come from the city. A softener swaps calcium and magnesium for sodium, so people on low salt diets shouldn't drink softened water. It also runs on salt. If the tank runs out, it stops removing hardness. And plumb your sprinkler system on its own line. The city says there's no reason to water your yard with softened water. If you want to talk through a softener for your house, the estimate is free.
