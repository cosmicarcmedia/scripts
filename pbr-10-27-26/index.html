3
Three questions to ask any plumber
The thing the big competitor will not say
▾
It hands the viewer three questions that are fair, easy to ask and hard for a larger company to answer as cleanly as a master plumber who owns the shop. Rick's license number and the state board's phone line are already printed on his site.
Spot C
Before you let anybody work on your plumbing, ask three questions. Who owns this company. Who is the master plumber on the license. And who do I call if something goes wrong. If you can't get a straight answer to those, keep looking.
Spot A
At Plumbing By Rick, the answer to all three is the same. It's me, Rick Barron. My license is number 39703, responsible master plumber. You don't have to take my word for it. The license number and the Texas State Board of Plumbing Examiners phone line are both on our website, so you can check me out yourself. Paula and I are family owned, and we put our name on the work.
Spot B
And if something goes wrong, you call us. Our About page says, if you have a complaint or criticism, we want to hear from you. I mean it. A customer named Bart wrote that from the beginning of his project to the end, he got individual attention and was kept informed. He called it the best experience they've had with a plumber. That's the standard I hold this crew to. I want a fair price, the job done right, and a customer who always knows what's going on. [OWNER: one way you keep a customer informed during a long job]
4
Is it a clog or your sewer line
The useful one
▾
Sewer lines are on Rick's services list, and a homeowner who learns to tell a clog from a main line problem will remember who taught them. It costs him nothing to give away and sets up the next call.
Spot A
If a drain in your house is slow, most people grab a plunger or a bottle of drain cleaner. Sometimes that's all it needs. Sometimes it's your sewer line, and no plunger is going to fix that.
Spot B
Here's how to tell the difference. One slow sink is usually just that sink. But if you run the washing machine and your shower gurgles, or two or three drains in the house slow down together, that's the main line talking to you. Backups show up first at the lowest drain in the house. Roots from a big tree in the yard are one of the usual reasons a main line backs up. [OWNER: where the lowest drain usually is in a San Angelo house] If water comes up there, stop running water everywhere else.
Spot C
Don't keep pouring drain cleaner down a line that's backing up. It won't clear a main line, and whoever opens that line next has to work around it. Call a plumber instead. We handle sewer lines, residential and commercial. No one ever said plumbing would be pretty. When you call, tell us which drains are slow and what was running when it started. That's most of what we need to know before the truck shows up. It makes the whole visit faster for both of us.
