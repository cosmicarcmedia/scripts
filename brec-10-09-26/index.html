1
I sell the kind of land I own
The origin and the face
▾
A buyer picking a ranch broker wants to know what the broker has at stake. G.L. can say his firm owns land in five counties, which gives a buyer a reason to pick up the phone before any listing does. It puts a face and a stake behind a brokerage with no video presence.
Spot A
I'm G.L. Bunger, and I'm a broker in Ozona, Texas. My firm sells ranch land, and I also sell houses and commercial property. But I'll tell you where I start from. We are landowners in Crockett, Kinney, Pecos, Terrell, and Val Verde Counties. So when I talk about a piece of ground, I'm talking about the kind of ground I own.
Spot B
[OWNER: how your family got started in Crockett County, and what the V after your name means] I own land in five counties, so I know what it asks of a person. Every ranch has its own character, and its own job. It might be livestock grazing, hunting, or just a place to get away, and each of those is a different kind of ranch. Knowing which one you need is the first part of my job.
Spot C
Our firm is built on professionalism, honesty, and integrity. That means I put your interests first, and I tell you the facts. If a property has a problem, you'll hear it from me. [OWNER: one time telling the truth cost you a deal, and why you'd do it again] I want the sale to go as smoothly as it can for the buyer and the seller. You can call me directly at 325 226 2107. Tell me what kind of ranch you're after, and I'll tell you what I've got. Tell me what you're looking for.
2
Twelve counties, one phone number
The local anchor
▾
A buyer in Del Rio and a seller in Big Lake both want the same thing, a person who knows the ground and picks up the phone. Naming the counties tells every rancher in the Concho Valley and Trans-Pecos that this broker covers their dirt.
Spot B
My office is in Ozona. I list property across twelve counties. Crockett, Edwards, Irion, Kinney, Pecos, Reagan, Schleicher, Sutton, Terrell, Tom Green, Upton, and Val Verde. That's a lot of West Texas, and I like it that way. Ozona is home base for all of it. Whether it's a ranch or a house, the process is the same.
Spot C
I list land and acreage in places like Sutton County and Val Verde County. I list houses in Ozona, Sonora, Eldorado, and Del Rio. I list commercial property in Big Lake, Rankin, Mertzon, and Christoval too. West Texas towns are small, and the people in them know each other. So a buyer in one county is often a neighbor of a seller in the next. [OWNER: one road, ranch, or town in your territory you'd point a stranger to first]
Spot A
Some of the listings I've had lately are out on Rosebud Road, Chisholm Road, and Windmill Road. They're just a few of the places I know well. If you want to see a property, I'll meet you there. If you've got land you're thinking of selling, I'll put together a marketing plan so that as many buyers as possible see it. You can reach me at 325 226 2107. The office number is 325 392 5666. Call whichever one is closer to hand.
3
Ask your ranch broker if he owns any ranch
The thing the big competitor will not say
▾
Anyone can put up a listing. A broker whose firm owns land in five counties has a stake in the ground, and that is a fact the field of ranch brokerages cannot all state. This is the video that gets sent to a neighbor who is thinking about selling.
Spot C
Here's a question to ask any broker who's selling you a ranch. Do you own land out here yourself? I mean ranch land. If somebody is going to tell you what a piece of ground is worth, he ought to have some of his own.
Spot A
I do. My firm owns land in five counties. Crockett, Kinney, Pecos, Terrell, and Val Verde. That means I know the work that goes into a place. Our promise is integrity, commitment, and perseverance. Ranch land is a long-term decision. You ought to deal with someone who has skin in it. [OWNER: one thing owning ranch land taught you that you'd tell a buyer]
Spot B
Our website says that being landowners gives us firsthand knowledge of the land. I'd say that's the point. When I show you a ranch, I tell you what I'd ask if I were the one buying. We serve West and Southwest Central Texas. And when you sell with us, we market your property to its full potential. We put together a marketing plan so that as many buyers as possible can see it. So ask your broker the question. Ask him what he owns, and where. Then call me, and I'll tell you what I own. 325 226 2107. Call me and ask me anything.
4
Five questions to ask before you buy ranch land
The useful one
▾
Buyers from out of town do not know what to ask about West Texas land, and the questions cost G.L. nothing to give away. It earns the follow and positions him as the broker who tells you the facts, which is the promise on his own site.
Spot A
Before you buy ranch land in West Texas, ask five questions. First, where is the water? Some places have a private water well, some don't. Ask who drilled it, and when.
Spot B
Second, how do you get in and out? Ask whether there's legal access, and whether the road is county or private. Third, who owns the minerals? Fourth, whose fence is it, and what shape is it in? Fifth, how is the land being used right now? Grazing, hunting, and recreation all look different on paper. [OWNER: the one question buyers forget the most]
Spot C
I list tracts from around a hundred acres on up. Some come with a cabin and a private water well. One tract I've listed has owner financing available. First tell me what you plan to do with the place. Ask about all of that. There's no such thing as a dumb question on a ranch purchase. There's a lot of money in a ranch, and the right answer can keep you out of a headache you can't see from the road. My site says our job is to present the facts. So call me, and I'll give you the facts on whatever you're looking at. We can talk through all five questions on the phone. 325 226 2107.
One more idea
Walk the well

Walk a buyer through the private water well and the cabin on a property you've listed. Open on the question buyers always ask first, then show the well and the cabin. Keep it plain and tell them what you'd check before buying.
