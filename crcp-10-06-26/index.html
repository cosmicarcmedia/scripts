1
I moved to San Angelo to do real estate. My dad was a painter.
Origin
▾
Nobody driving past a CRC truck knows the man behind it came here to sell real estate, started a window company, and kept getting asked to fix the rest of the house, all while carrying a painter's trade he learned from his father. That story is the face of this company.
Spot A
I moved to San Angelo in 2005 to do real estate. I started an insulated window company along with it. There wasn't enough window work to keep me busy, and people kept asking me to do other things to their homes. So I started doing everything. That window company was Climate Right Construction. It grew into a full remodeling business, and today it's a family of companies. Painting is the one that came first, though.
Spot B
My dad was a painter. I grew up in the Arlington area, working in construction for him and my uncle. When I was in my twenties I started my own company in 1984, and I started it painting. Then I moved into custom finish out on custom homes. Trim work, wood trim, all of it. By the time I got to San Angelo I'd run thousands of projects in several major Texas cities.
Spot C
CRC Painting started in 2008. We paint interiors and exteriors, homes and businesses, and cabinets too. Our family has been painting for four generations now. My motto is business before pleasure, prayer before business. We're members of the San Angelo Chamber of Commerce, and we've been Better Business Bureau accredited since 2018 with an A+ rating.
2
That CRC on the San Angelo Coliseum is our family
Local anchor

A cold viewer in San Angelo has driven past the Coliseum a hundred times. Telling them the name on it belongs to this family, and that the same family paints their neighbor's house, is a local tie no out-of-town painter can borrow.
Spot B
The San Angelo Coliseum seats about five thousand people, and the name on the building is ours. It's the CRC Roofers Coliseum, out on East 43rd Street. CRC Roofers is our roofing company. The building opened in 1958, and it's home to the fifth largest rodeo in the country. I spent years on the city's Planning Commission and the Zoning Board of Adjustments, so I pay attention to what gets built in this town. Our office is out on Knickerbocker Road. From there we paint in San Angelo, Wall, Mertzon, Christoval, Grape Creek, Eden, Eldorado, Robert Lee and Big Lake. We've gone further than that for ranches and larger estates.
Spot C
One customer wrote about his dad, who has called us for years to keep the house he and his wife built in 1999 in shape. Exterior paint on the whole property. Drywall repair and plastering after an attic air conditioner leak. And while we were there, we swapped out a couple of dead light fixtures and hung a ceiling fan he'd bought and couldn't get up on his own.
3
Ask any painter why they want money before they start
Competitor contrast
▾
Every homeowner has heard a painter ask for money up front. The questions in this script let a viewer test any contractor in town, and every answer on our side comes straight from the company's own site, blog and reviews, so the contrast lands without naming anyone.
Spot C
Some painters want your money before they've opened a can of paint. Ask them why. We don't take anything up front, and financing is available. I've been in this trade since 1984, and I've been called in to fix what the wrong painter left behind. An out of town painter or a brand new one can disappear as fast as he showed up.
Spot A
Who stands behind the work? Ours is a one year workmanship warranty, and the paint itself carries the manufacturer's warranty. What happens if it's windy or dusty the day they plan to paint your house? We don't start an exterior job if rain or a dust storm is likely. And we clean the surface first to get the trapped dust off.
Spot B
How much of the job is prep? A great paint job is eighty percent preparation and twenty percent application. Prep means filling the nail holes, caulking the gaps along the trim, sanding the rough spots, priming, and masking. We buy the paint and put it on your invoice, so we pick the right finish for the surface. One customer wrote that the crew spent more time prepping and keeping things organized than it took to paint the wall.
4
Before you paint your brick house
Useful customer education
▾
Painting brick is one of the most common upgrades in town and one of the least reversible. Explaining the moisture and prep rules costs the company nothing, and the metallic platinum house from a real Google review proves they will paint what a homeowner asks for.
Spot A
A customer once asked if we could paint their brick house metallic platinum, with champagne gold on the fascia. We did it. The review says we nailed the craziest paint job ever. The color was the easy part, though. The part you can't undo is painting the brick at all.
Spot B
A fresh white, charcoal or gray can make a dated brick house look new, and the paint shields the brick from the West Texas sun and the wind. But brick is porous. It soaks paint in deep, so it often takes more than one coat. Once it's painted, it's very hard and very expensive to get back to raw brick. Natural brick can last for decades with an occasional wash and some mortar repair.
Spot C
Painted brick needs a touch up or a repaint every seven to ten years, because the sun and the temperature swings will fade it, flake it or peel it. What decides whether it lasts is prep. The brick has to be completely dry before the first coat. If it isn't, or if the paint is the wrong kind, moisture gets trapped, and that's where the peeling and chipping come from. So the cracks get repaired, any water leak gets fixed first, and the paint has to let moisture escape.
