1
The first Pit BBQ was a food trailer, and the rent was free
The origin and the face
▾
Two owners and a pitmaster give a camera plenty of faces, and almost nobody eating on the patio knows how the place started. This puts Jesse's father in law, the friendship with DJ and the trailer in one story that ends at the restaurant, all from interviews the two have already given.
Spot A
The first Pit BBQ was a food trailer, and the rent was free. My father in law told DJ and me, buy a trailer and park it on my property. No rent. No water bill. No power bill. We used the money that saved us to build toward a real restaurant. That was 2015. A year in, Texas Monthly came out to the trailer and wrote us up.
Spot B
DJ and I met waiting tables at the same restaurant. I stayed in San Angelo and sold cars. I've been starting little businesses since I was young. Lawn care. Car accessories. DJ went to culinary school, then cooked barbecue in Austin. I kept telling him to move home, but there wasn't a job here for him. So we started talking about opening our own place. We figured San Angelo needed to try a different style of barbecue than the backyard kind.
Spot C
It's always been our dream to make this a restaurant. In 2019 we bought an old convenience store on Sherwood Way. The trailer kept serving in the parking lot while we worked on the building. We opened the doors in April of 2023. We went from two guys to a crew of sixteen. Now there are nineteen of us. Our homepage still says it. Started from the trailer, now we're here.
2
The convenience store on Sherwood Way
The local anchor
▾
A restaurant on Sherwood Way that used to be a convenience store, with a trailer that kept serving through the rebuild, is a story the neighbors already half know. It puts the building, the patio and the local events he sponsors on camera in the owner's own words.
Spot B
Our building on Sherwood Way used to be a convenience store. We bought it in 2019, and it needed a lot of work. While we rebuilt it, the trailer stayed in the parking lot and kept serving. Then the pandemic hit in early 2020, and the renovation stalled for several months. We kept the trailer open and we kept going. We never chased spots the way a lot of food trucks do. We stayed in one place, so people always knew where to find us.
Spot A
Now the old store is a restaurant. We added a patio with turf, a stage, picnic tables and murals by local artists. We have live music every weekend. The patio is open until nine Sunday through Wednesday, and until ten Thursday through Saturday. You can reserve the patio for a special occasion, and we cater events too. If you haven't been out to Sherwood Way yet, come sit outside.
Spot C
I've served on the board of the Hispanic Heritage Museum and Cultural Center here in San Angelo. We sponsor the Taco and Salsa Fest. We help with Toys for Tots, and with school fundraisers for San Angelo ISD. We started with one trailer and two guys, and a lot of this town has stopped by. 3
Three questions to ask any barbecue place
The thing the big competitor will not say
▾
A pitmaster who answers the wood, the cook and the timing out loud gives a diner three questions to carry to every other barbecue place without naming one. The Austin kitchens, the culinary school and the hot and fast start all come from interviews with DJ.
Spot C
I'm DJ Ridenour, the pitmaster and executive chef at The Pit BBQ in San Angelo. Before you eat at any barbecue place, ask three questions. What wood are you burning. Who cooked it. And how does the brisket start. Here's how I answer.
Spot A
First, the wood. We burn [OWNER: the woods you burn]. Austin style barbecue runs on oak and pecan. Second, who cooked it. I learned barbecue in Austin, at John Mueller Meat Co. and at Smokey Denmark's. Before that, I went to culinary school at El Paso Community College. I got my start in a kitchen when I was eight years old, helping my mom make donuts at her donut shop.
Spot B
Third, how the brisket starts. It starts hot and fast. That sears the outside, the way a steak sears on a flattop. It keeps the fat rendering and the juices inside. Then it gets a special seasoning blend and a generous amount of pepper. The menu runs from brisket to chicken, sausage and ribs, and barbecue baked potatoes with a lot of toppings. Everything gets cooked fresh here every day, and we open at ten thirty. Ask those same three questions anywhere else you eat barbecue. Then come see how we answer them.
4
What to order your first time at The Pit
The useful one
▾
First timers stare at a menu with a peanut butter sandwich on it, and this answers their question in the owner's own words. The Jail Break and Gonzo stories are the two he has already told on local TV.
Spot A
If it's your first time at The Pit, here's what I'd order. Start with the Jail Break. It's sliced brisket with peanut butter and grape jelly on a toasted bun. I know. Some people give me a funny look. Then they take a bite. It hits every spot. You get the sweet, the peanut butter and the pepper.
Spot B
The idea came from a restaurant in Buda. They used chopped brisket and strawberry jelly. So I reached out to them and asked if they'd mind if we made our own. We use sliced brisket and grape jelly. It's been our signature ever since. The jelly is sweet, the peanut butter is rich, and the pepper crust on the brisket holds it all together.
Spot C
Second, the Gonzo. It's our Pit Pie with beans, jalapeño creamed corn and sausage on top. A regular we all call Gonzo started ordering it that way. A local radio DJ asked what it was called, and I named it right there. A few days later, somebody walked in and asked for a Gonzo they heard about on the radio. It was our secret menu for a while, and now it's on the regular menu. Third, get a barbecue baked potato with everything on it. And get a side of jalapeño creamed corn.
