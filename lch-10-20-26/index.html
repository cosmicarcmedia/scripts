4
What makes real matcha, in a minute
The useful one
▾
Matcha is the center of the menu, and most people have only had it from a mix. A one minute lesson on shade growing, stone milling and what the real thing tastes like costs nothing, earns trust, and makes the ceremonial grade claim mean something.
Spot A
If you've only had matcha from a mix, here's what makes the real thing. Matcha comes from tea that's grown in the shade. The Japanese labeling standard says the plants are shaded for about two to three weeks before harvest. That shading keeps the leaf from turning bitter, and it deepens the green. That's where the strong umami flavor comes from. Matcha has about twice the amino acids of sencha.
Spot B
After harvest, the leaves are steamed and dried. That leaf is called tencha. The tencha is aged for several months, and then ground into a fine powder. Traditionally, a slow stone mill did the grinding. Real matcha has a bright green color. It's a powder, and you whisk it with water. Some cheaper powders are made from leaves that were never shaded. Those get sold as matcha, but they don't meet the standard.
Spot C
So when you order matcha anywhere, ask where it comes from. [OWNER: where your matcha comes from, and how you prepare it] At Lume, we serve ceremonial grade matcha, and we make it with intention. One guest said our lavender matcha is subtle and floral, and it doesn't overpower the earthy notes of the matcha. That's the balance I like. [OWNER: the matcha you would hand a first timer] You can come in and ask us anything about it.
