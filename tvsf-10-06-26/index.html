1
On the city council at nineteen
Origin
▾
Nobody walking into an insurance office behind the HEB would guess the man at the desk was on a city council at nineteen, then mayor of Fort Stockton, then in a national State Farm commercial. That is a rare, specific story, and it puts a face on a category full of interchangeable offices.
Spot A
At nineteen years old, I was on the Rocksprings city council. At twenty one, I was the youngest county commissioner in Texas. But my career started somewhere much smaller than that. It started in my family's business, back in the town where I was raised. I'm Tony Villarreal, and I've been an insurance agent for more than twenty five years.
Spot B
In 1987, I moved to Fort Stockton and opened a satellite office. I served on the school board there, and on the Pecos County Commissioners Court. Then the voters made me mayor, the first Hispanic mayor that town had. I'm still proud of that. And in 2005, I had the honor of appearing in a national State Farm commercial.
Spot C
Later on, I moved to San Angelo to keep working with State Farm customers across West Texas. My office is on College Hills Boulevard, behind the HEB. I've served with the Southside Lions Club and the Angelo State University Athletics Foundation Board. In 2017, I ran for mayor of San Angelo, and my slogan was I'm With Tony. These days, when somebody becomes a customer, we tell them they can say it too. Outside of work, I'm with my own family. And you will rarely catch me traveling without my golf clubs.
2
The people in my office come from all over West Texas
Local anchor
▾
His team comes from Winters, Fort Stockton and San Angelo, and his office has an address anybody in town can find. Naming those places tells a West Texas viewer the video was made for them, and it shows the bilingual office before anyone has to ask.
Spot B
The people in my office come from all over West Texas. But we all end up in the same place every morning. It's on College Hills Boulevard, behind the HEB. Parking is right out front, and there are no steps to climb.
Spot A
Jose is from Winters. Annalisa is from Fort Stockton, and she has twenty one years of insurance experience. Maggie and Paris were both born and raised right here in San Angelo. Maggie refurbishes furniture in her spare time, so ask her about that. Rosa grew up here and in Fort Worth, and now she calls San Angelo home. Between us, we speak English and Spanish. Rosa and Jose both help our Spanish speaking customers understand their policies and get past the language barrier.
Spot C
I'm from Rocksprings, and I spent years in Fort Stockton before I came here. So I know what it's like to be the new person in a West Texas town. When I got to San Angelo, this town welcomed me with open arms. These days I serve San Angelo and the communities around Tom Green County. If you moved to Texas from another state, we welcome out of state transfers. If you moved across town, come by anyway. We'll go through your options with you, so bring your questions.
3
Ask every insurance company who picks up
Competitor contrast
▾
Everybody shopping for insurance eventually wonders who they will reach when they call. This answers it with facts from his own pages, an agent who runs his own office and hires his own team, Saturday hours and his name on the building, without naming a company or quoting a price.
Spot C
Call your insurance company right now and see who picks up. I've been an agent for more than twenty five years, and that's the first thing I'd ask anybody. At a call center, it's whoever is next in line. At my office, it's a person on my team, and you can call or text. My name is on the front of the building, in big red letters. If you have a question, you know whose door to knock on.
Spot A
Every State Farm agent is an independent contractor who runs their own office. Mine is run by me, and I answer for what happens in it. I hire my own team, and they work for me. So when you call, you reach a person who answers to somebody you can find. When customers write about us online, they name the person who helped them. That tells me they know who they talked to.
Spot B
We keep Saturday hours, ten to two, and we make appointments after hours. If you buy insurance online, that's your call, and plenty of people do. But when a question comes up, it helps to know where the office is and whose name is on the door. So ask every company the same thing, me included.
4
Call your agent before you unpack
Useful customer education
▾
Most people only think about insurance after something goes wrong. This gives them the ordinary moments worth a call first, drawn from the seasonal check-ins on his own site, and it costs Tony nothing to give away.
Spot A
Before you unpack the first box in a new place, call your insurance agent. I know, you've got a couch in the driveway and a lease to read. But a move is one of the moments worth a coverage review. Your address changed, and your belongings may be new.
Spot B
It's the same when you renew a lease, buy new furniture or electronics, or finish a renovation. Getting married, starting a family, buying a home, changing careers, or starting a business are good times too. If someone depends on you financially, it's worth thinking about life insurance as early as you can. And around here, football season and holiday travel put extra miles on the car. That's a good time to look at your auto policy and make sure it fits the vehicles you drive now.
Spot C
If you run a business, the end of the year is a good time to look at your business coverage, since your equipment, your employees and your plans may have changed. A review is a conversation. You tell us what changed, and we go through your policy together. Every policy is different, so what makes sense depends on your situation. You can call the office, text us, or stop by. And if you already have an agent, ask them for the same review. It's a fair thing to ask.
