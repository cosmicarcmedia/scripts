1
Food, fun, freedom, fairness, finance, and firearms
The origin and the face
▾
His own bio line is the most memorable thing about him, and it comes with a hometown résumé: Central High, Angelo State and a wife born here too. A loan officer with a personality and roots is rare in a field that sounds the same.
Spot A
If you read my bio, it lists my passions as food, fun, freedom, fairness, finance, and firearms. That's a lot of F words for a mortgage guy. [OWNER: the story behind that list] I'm Levi Calhoun, and I run the Geneva Financial branch in San Angelo. We do purchase loans, refinances, VA and USDA loans, and a good bit more. My NMLS number is 1174441.
Spot B
I was born and raised right here. I went to Central High and then Angelo State, where I got a degree in finance. My wife Liz was born and raised here too. We've been married since 2009, and we've got a little girl and a little boy. My loves in life are my faith, my family, and my friends.
Spot C
I call my clients pals, and I mean it. Most of them start as folks who want to buy a house, and the good ones turn into friends. That's what I like best about doing this in my hometown. The branch is on West Concho, and the door's open Monday through Saturday. Sunday, too, if you set it up with me. If you grew up in San Angelo, you probably know somebody I went to school with. Come see me.
2
My office sits on the first street in San Angelo
The local anchor
▾
Local history from the street his branch is on, told by a native. A downtown story like this gets forwarded between people who care about San Angelo, and it plants the branch's address in their heads.
Spot C
My office is on West Concho, and that street has a story. It used to be called Concho Street, named for the river. The river is why there was a fort, and the soldiers' pay is a big part of why there was a town. It was the first street in San Angelo, and the center of downtown.
Spot A
In the 1880s it was the main road running east and west through town. Over at 18 West Concho, there's a two story sandstone building from 1887. It was built for a Danish blacksmith named Peter Freeze. It started as a blacksmith and wheelwright shop. By 1888, he had eight men working for him, and he started adding a third floor he never finished.
Spot B
The railroad came into town in 1888, and that made San Angelo a hub for the whole region. But people were building on Concho before any train showed up. I think that's my favorite thing about this street. It's where a young town bet on itself. I'm glad to do business on a street with that kind of history. Now I help people take that same kind of bet, a home of their own. If you want to look at the old buildings and talk about a loan, stop by.
3
Three questions to ask any lender before you hand over your paperwork
The thing the big competitor will not say
▾
Sharpest of the four, and it turns his branch's stated setup (in house underwriting, Saturday hours, a family who comes back) into a buyer's checklist with no competitor named. It is built to be shared with a friend who is about to apply.
Spot B
Before you give any lender your paperwork, ask three questions. First, who is going to underwrite my loan, someone in house, or is it going to get sent out. Second, what days can I reach you. I'm in the branch Monday through Saturday, and our operations team doesn't clock out at five. When your closing date is close, that matters a lot.
Spot C
Third, what's the difference between a pre-qualification and an approval. A pre-qualification is a first look. It tells you what you might be able to borrow, and it doesn't mean you've been approved or that underwriting has signed off. You deserve to hear that before you fall in love with a house. Ask for the next step in plain English, and ask who is going to take it.
Spot A
I'm a direct lender with Geneva Financial, and our underwriting is done in house. One family told me I'd done two loans for them and one for each of their kids, and that kind of loyalty is what I work for. I'd like to earn it with you. Ask me those three questions. Ask me how the online portal works, too, because most of the process happens through it. Then ask anybody else the same ones, and pick the lender who answers plainly.
4
The six steps from first call to getting your keys
The useful one
▾
Free, step by step and straight from Geneva's own process, so it carries no new claims. A first time buyer who understands the order is far less scared to start, and that makes him the first call.
Spot A
Buying a home feels like a black box until somebody walks you through it. So here are the six steps. One, we talk. How long you plan to stay, how much you'll put down, and what you want the monthly payment to feel like. Two, a pre-qualification, which is a first look at what you may be able to borrow.
Spot B
Three, processing. You give us your documents and a processor goes through your file. Then they order the appraisal. If we need anything else from you, we ask. Four, underwriting, where our underwriter reviews your file to make sure it meets the rules for your loan program. Five, pre-closing, where you get the commitment letter that lays out the details and anything still outstanding.
Spot C
Six, closing. Bring a photo ID and the right form of payment for your down payment, closing costs, and other costs due that day. Once the title company has disbursed the funds, you get the keys. One more thing, since I hear it a lot. Plenty of folks believe you need twenty percent down. That isn't true, and our company says so, so ask me about your options. I'm Levi Calhoun, and I'm happy to walk through it with you.
