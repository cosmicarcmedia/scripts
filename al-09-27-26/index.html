Seventy five years of experience, standing in one yard
The origin and the face
▾
Introduces the crew as real people with real years in the dirt before anything gets sold, since no single founder name is published to hang the story on.
Spot A
Add up the years everybody on this crew has spent doing this work and you get past seventy five years, standing in one yard.
Spot B
[OWNER: what got you personally into landscape design in the first place] None of us started out drawing pool decks on a laptop. Most of us started with a shovel, a trailer, and somebody else's yard that needed fixing. You learn soil before you ever learn design. You learn where water wants to go on a piece of West Texas caliche before anybody hands you a rendering to build from. That's still true for every one of us on this crew today, creative thinkers and problem solvers who happen to also love being outside for a living.
Spot C
We still do design now. Pools. Fire features. Full outdoor kitchens. Turf for the parts of the yard that need to survive a dog and a Texas summer. But every one of those still starts with somebody walking the property first, checking the drainage and the soil before a single line gets drawn. We didn't skip the shovel part to get here. We just got better at everything that comes after it, and we took that same approach out past San Angelo, into Abilene, Midland, Del Rio, and a handful of other West Texas towns that needed somebody who walks the yard first.

The yard at Capital Farm Credit
The local anchor
▾
A real, named commercial project at a well known West Texas agricultural lender, plus a residential story pulled from an actual review, showing the work holds up in public and in a backyard.
Spot B
You've probably driven past Capital Farm Credit here in San Angelo without thinking twice about the landscaping out front. That's ours. A full commercial transformation, done for a company that spends its whole day financing land and agriculture, which means they know exactly what good ground is supposed to look like. People walk in there to talk about their livelihood. The last thing that building needs is a front yard that looks like nobody's paying attention.

That's the part that keeps us honest on a commercial job. You're not just landscaping a building. You're landscaping the front of a company whose own clients judge land for a living. There's no cutting a corner on a job like that.

That's a house on a quarter acre, not a bank on the loop, and the site still got walked the same way, the plan still got built around the same questions, and it still got finished like somebody's going to judge it, because somebody always is, whether that's a loan officer or your own family. We treat both jobs like they're the last one anybody will ever see our name on.
3
Why we walk your yard before we quote it
The thing the big competitor will not say
▾
This is the one that gets forwarded. A national lawn franchise sends a crew and a flat rate, not a design built around your actual soil and drainage, and this says so without naming anyone.
Spot C
Call one of the big national lawn franchises and you'll get a price over the phone before anybody has ever seen your yard. Same crew, same price, same plan as the house down the street.
Spot A
Here's why we don't do that. Every yard in this part of Texas sits on different ground. Some of it drains fine. Some of it turns into a bathtub after one hard rain. A flat rate crew treats both the same way, because they were never trained to look at the difference, and they're not coming back next month if the grading was wrong. We'd rather find that problem walking the yard with you than have you find it after the first storm.
Spot B
We walk the property first. Every time. Soil, drainage, the direction the sun hits it, before we ever draw a plan or hand you a number. Then we build a plan around your yard specifically, hardscape, irrigation, and planting sized to how your land behaves, not a template that gets used on every stop that week. We even set up financing through a local bank for the bigger builds, because a real plan sometimes costs more than a template, and you shouldn't have to choose the template just to afford it.
4
The turf question we get from people with dogs
The useful one
▾
Costs nothing to give away, grounded in a real customer's project (a pet's allergies) and the water conservation angle already central to the company's own service list, in a region where every homeowner is thinking about their water bill.
Spot A
If you've got a dog that tears up the yard, or worse, a pet with allergies to real grass, here's something most people never think to ask about.
Spot B
We put in a full artificial turf yard recently specifically because the family's pet had allergies to real grass. No more mud dragged inside. No more flare ups. We use ForeverLawn's premium product line, including their pet specific turf, which drains straight through the base instead of pooling on top, so it doesn't turn into standing water in a West Texas downpour either. And it's not the fake feeling stuff from years ago. It's built to take a dog running full speed on it every single day.
Spot C
The other side of that same coin is your water bill. A xeriscaped bed and the right turf in the right spots means less irrigation running every single week, which matters here more than almost anywhere else in the state. We can also design the irrigation zones around whatever real grass stays, so you're not watering turf that never needed it in the first place. You don't have to turf the whole yard to feel that difference. Ask us where it makes sense on your property and where real grass still wins.
