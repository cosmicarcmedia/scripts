1
The wedding beauty industry, then a venue of my own
The origin and the face
▾
The owner already wrote this story on her About page. On camera it turns a rental lawn into a person a bride wants to meet, which is the thing a hotel ballroom cannot offer.
Spot A
For several years in San Antonio and the Austin area, I worked in the wedding beauty industry. I was there in the hours before the ceremony. I watched brides sit down nervous and stand up glowing. That feeling is what I fell in love with. And I always dreamed of owning a venue.
Spot B
Then I moved to San Angelo. My husband and I started Reynas Tacos, and that put us in the wedding world as caterers. It gave me a broader view of what a wedding takes. I met a lot of people, and I built a network of vendors along the way. [OWNER: the moment you decided to buy the venue].
Spot C
Today we own Casa Rosa, right downtown. We've been hosting weddings and events here since 2021. Some brides are busy DIYers, and I love that. But if you want to hand it over, it's okay to leave it all to me and relax. I take the time to learn your vision, and then I build a plan around it. Experienced planners handle every detail. If you want to see the place, call 325 500 5379 and book a tour. Come stand on the lawn and tell me what you picture.
2
A downtown San Angelo wedding, start to finish
The local anchor
▾
Naming real local vendors from her own list shows engaged couples that the venue is the center of a San Angelo wedding scene, and it puts every named vendor in the position of sharing the video.
Spot B
Casa Rosa sits in the heart of San Angelo's downtown district. And a wedding here is made by San Angelo businesses. Say you want a ceremony in a chapel. Our preferred vendor list has Ruffini Chapel. Then the reception moves to the lawn here, for a crowd of up to a hundred and fifty.
Spot C
Dinner and dessert come from people in town. Texas Oak BBQ or City Cafe and Bakery for catering. The Chocolate Drip Bakery or Carters Sugar Shop for the cake and dessert tables. Boujee Floral Shop for fresh flowers. Angelo's Lights in Heaven for the marquee letters. DJ Thomas keeps the dance floor going.

Out of town guests need somewhere to sleep. We have room blocks with the Angoria Hotel and Candlewood Suites. All of that is on a preferred vendor list, and it's on our website. [OWNER: your favorite local vendor story from the past five years]. I put that list together because I met these people along the way. They're good at what they do, and they're from here. From a small rehearsal dinner to a full reception, we can plan around it. Come see the lawn, and bring your list. Call 325 500 5379 and book a tour.
3
Four questions to ask any venue before you tour
The thing the big competitor will not say
▾
The one meant to be forwarded. A bride choosing between a hotel ballroom and a barn gets four questions, and the owner's own site answers every one without naming anybody.
Spot C
Before you put a deposit down on any venue, ask four questions. Number one. Who is going to be my coordinator, and will I talk to the same person from the tour to the last dance? Here's mine. At Casa Rosa, you talk to me.
Spot A
Number two. Are the rates posted? Ours are on our website, with two rate sheets. One for events under seventy five guests, and one for over. You can read both before you call. No phone tag to find out what it costs. And it holds up to a hundred and fifty, from a small rehearsal dinner to a full reception.
Spot B
Number three. Does the venue take the planning off my plate? We build a customized plan around your vision. Experienced planners handle every detail. We can do the decor too, flowers and lighting included. Number four. What does catering look like? There's a downloadable catering menu on our site. The rental rules and the preferred vendor list are there too. Everything you'd want to know before a tour is already on the page. If you ask these four at the next venue, I'd like to hear what they say. Then call me and ask again, and I'll give you the same answers.
4
What to bring when you tour a wedding venue
The useful one
▾
Free planning help from the person who has hosted weddings since 2021. A bride who uses this list shows up to the tour ready to book.
Spot A
You booked a venue tour. Great. Here's what to bring so you don't waste it. First, your guest count. Even a rough one. Our rates change at seventy five guests, so that number decides which sheet you read. Our lawn holds up to a hundred and fifty. Pick the mood next. We have three packages to start from. Celebrate Love on the Lawn and Under the Star. Black and White Affair. Perfectly Pink and Passionately Planned.
Spot B
Second, a few photos of what you picture. Flowers, lighting, colors. We can customize the decor, so the photos tell me more than a description. Third, your date, and your backup date. Fourth, ask for the rental rules ahead of time. We post ours on the website. Fifth, the people you've already picked. A DJ, a baker, a photographer. If they're on our preferred vendor list, great. If they aren't, tell me, and we'll talk.
Spot C
Last, your biggest worry. Everybody has one. Rain. Cost. Your mother in law. Say it out loud, and I can build the plan around it. [OWNER: the worry brides bring most often, and how you answer it]. If you want to book a tour, call 325 500 5379. Bring your list, and bring your people. I'd rather meet them now than on the wedding day.
