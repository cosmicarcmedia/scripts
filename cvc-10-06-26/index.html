4
When it gets cold in San Angelo and your joints ache, arthritis means a lot of things
Useful customer education
▾
The practice's own arthritis post already says the cold weather brings the same comment into the office every fall, and a plain explanation of what the word covers is the kind of thing a patient saves and sends to a parent.
Spot A
Every fall, when it gets colder here in San Angelo, we hear the same sentence in our office. "My joints are starting to ache. I probably just have arthritis." Arthritis is one word for a lot of different conditions. Arthro means joint, and itis means inflammation. A lot of things can inflame a joint, and there are many more types than the three I'm about to mention.
Spot B
Osteoarthritis is the most common form. It involves mechanical damage to the surfaces between the bones, and it tends to get worse with age. Common sites are the knees, the hips, and the hands. Rheumatoid arthritis is the most common inflammatory form. That's when the body's own immune system attacks the tissue of the joints, and it commonly affects the hands, wrists, and neck. Psoriatic arthritis is less common, and it shows up in some people who have psoriasis.
Spot C
Those are different conditions, and knowing which one you have plays a big role in your care. If you're worried about changes in your joints, a trusted healthcare provider is a good next step. And if you have questions about what chiropractic care involves, we're glad to answer them. I'm Dr. James Vlcek at Concho Valley Chiropractic.
