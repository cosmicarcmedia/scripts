So why isn't LDL enough on its own? Because I don't see an elevated LDL as the sole driver of disease. A colleague of mine put it this way, and I think it's right. LDL is the wood. Wood with nothing sparking it behaves very differently than wood with a few sparks around it. Here's the picture I use. If you're driving down the highway at seventy, you have time to react. What would be irritating it? High blood pressure does it, with tiny injuries every time it runs high. Metabolic dysfunction does it, and so can heavy metals. Your body patches each injury with cholesterol, like spackle over a hole, and it thinks it's doing the right thing. Do that chronically, and plaque builds in the wall of the artery. So the number I need to find is whatever is sparking it in you. That's what the workup is for.

The first thing I add is ApoB. LDL measures how much cholesterol the particles are carrying. ApoB counts the particles. Every particle that can build plaque carries one ApoB protein, so it works like a head count. I can't tell you ApoB replaces LDL for everyone. I can tell you I like it better, because it tracks the cholesterol that's atherogenic, the kind that makes plaque. Do the two ever disagree?
