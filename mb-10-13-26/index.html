3
Ask who owns the store you're standing in
The thing the big competitor will not say
▾
This is the line a chain cannot match. The owners are on the floor, a customer wrote a review about it, and the video says so without naming anyone.
Spot C
Walk into a chain store and ask who owns it. The owner isn't standing there. At Mr. Boots, the owners are on the floor. Laura and I are in the store [OWNER: how many days a week you two are on the floor]. When you ask for something, you're asking the person who decides.
Spot A
A customer wrote an online review about a pair of Wranglers. He asked us to hold his size, and we did. That's the whole story, and he liked it enough to write it down. I can't speak for anybody else's store. I can tell you what happens in ours.
Spot B
When you have a question about an order, you're writing to the store on Beauregard, and somebody who works here writes you back. Even a return comes back to our front door at 2130 West Beauregard. The Better Business Bureau lists the owners by name, and that's Laura and me. And here's our fine print out loud. You have thirty days to return something, unworn, with the tags on. Sale and clearance items are final. This store has been accredited by the Better Business Bureau since 1983. It holds an A plus rating, with no complaints filed in the last three years. [OWNER: a habit or policy you keep that explains that record] So if you want to buy boots from the people who own the store, come see us. The number is 325-949-3921.
4
What to do when new boots slip at the heel
The useful one
▾
Everybody who buys a pair of boots wonders if the fit is right, and the answers cost nothing to give away. It earns the follow from anyone planning to buy, here or anywhere else.
Spot B
If your new boots slip at the heel, hold off on sending them back. A quarter to a half inch of heel slip is normal in a new pair. The sole is still stiff. After a few days of walking, it flexes, and the slip goes away. As long as the rest of the boot fits, that slip is nothing to worry about.
Spot C
Now check the ball of your foot. It should sit at the widest part of the boot. If it lands too far forward or too far back, try a different size. Pinch the leather over your instep. A big wrinkle means the boot is too loose, and a very slight wrinkle means it fits. Leave about a thumb's width between your longest toe and the end of the boot.
Spot A
Try boots on late in the day, when your feet have swelled. Wear the socks you plan to wear. For a first pair, boot socks or calf-high athletic socks work well. When you pull a boot on, you should feel some resistance, and then the heel drops into place. That drop is the feeling you're listening for. If you're not sure about any of this, come on in and try a few pairs. We'll help you work through it.
