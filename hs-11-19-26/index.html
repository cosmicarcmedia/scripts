3
Do they show up when they say they will
The thing the big competitor will not say
▾
Names the one thing every customer worries about with a fence or gate job and lets Heare Services answer it with what past customers already wrote. It draws a line against crews that disappear after the estimate, without naming anyone.
Here's the question I'd ask any welder or fence contractor. Will you show up the day you said you would? It sounds like a small thing, and it is the most common complaint about anybody who works with their hands. One customer put it in writing about us. Daniel shows up when he says he will, and he gets the job done. I'd like that to be the most boring sentence anyone ever writes about me. At our shop, the owner is a welder, and the lead welder has a name. It's Thomas Ogle, and he works right alongside the rest of the crew. And here's the third. Can you fix what I already have? Gate repair is one of the things we do best. If it can be saved, we'll weld it back together and hang it straight. We'll tell you honestly if it can't. 4
Why your gate drags and what to check first
The useful one
▾
Gives a homeowner a quick, free diagnosis for the most common gate problem. It costs the shop nothing, it shows welding knowledge on camera, and it sends the people with a real sagging gate to the phone.
If your gate drags on the ground, here's what to check before you call anybody. Open it and look at the post. Most sagging gates blame the gate, but the post is leaning. If the post moved, the gate is only following it. Next, look at the hinges. Wiggle the gate by hand. If the hinge pin has worn, or a bolt has worked loose, you'll see the gate shift before the post does. Tighten what's loose, and look for rust at the bottom of the hinge, because that's where it starts. Then look at the gate frame itself. Check the corners. A crack in a weld at a corner lets the whole frame drop on one side. That's a welding repair, and a lot of the time the gate can be saved without buying a new one. So don't throw it out before somebody looks at it. [OWNER: what a typical gate repair involves and how long it takes] One more thing, and it's free. Wire brush the rust spots on a black metal gate or fence and touch them up with paint before winter. A little bare steel stays a little problem for a while, and then it doesn't. If you're not sure what you're looking at, call Daniel at 325 234 1630. Describe what you see, and we'll tell you what it sounds like. Sometimes the answer is a wrench, and sometimes it's a welder.
