3
Before you sign for blinds, ask who installs them
The thing the big competitor will not say
▾
Four plain answers from the shop's own FAQ, exact measuring, a quote with everything in it, custom made product and no middleman, give homeowners the questions to ask any window covering company. It lets Transcend stand on policy without naming a rival.
Before you sign for new blinds or shutters, ask four questions. One, who's measuring? We don't order from rough measurements. One of our experts comes out and takes exact measurements before anything gets ordered, so it fits the way it should. If a window is a half inch off, you'd see it every day for years. Two, what's in the quote? Ours includes the product, shipping, taxes, installation, and any trip fees. You see it all before you say yes. So you know the full cost up front. Three, is it made for my window? Everything we sell is custom made to order. We don't have a rack of pre-made blinds in the store. We work with brands like Norman, Graber and Exus Shutters. Four, who's installing it? We are. We're a family run business, and the people who sell it are the people who hang it. No middleman, and no stranger on the porch. You work with the same people from the consultation to the last screw. [OWNER: what customers say when they realize the same person is back at the door] And if you're military or a first responder, mention it at the free in-home consultation. We add the discount to your quote. That's it. Ask those four questions of anybody you call, and you'll know a lot about who you're hiring.
4
Which window covering goes in which room
The useful one
▾
A homeowner staring at a wall of options mostly wants to know what goes where. This is that answer, built from the shop's own product pages, and it ends on the free guide and the free consultation.
Picking window coverings for a whole house can feel like a lot. Here's how I think about it, room by room. Kitchens and bathrooms get moisture. Faux wood blinds resist it, and you can wipe them clean with a damp cloth. Bedrooms are about light. A roller shade comes in everything from sheer to blackout, and cellular shades have little honeycomb cells that trap air, which helps insulate against the heat and the cold. Big windows and sliding doors are a different job. Vertical blinds work well there, and so does drapery, with a lot of styles and fabrics to choose from. [OWNER: the room you see people get wrong most often] If you've got small kids or pets, ask about motorized shades. Zebra shades are one example, and motorized versions can be safer for a house like that. If you want the shutters look, they come in wood, faux wood or composite. Wood is the classic, and you can stain it or paint it. They can even be carved or shaped to fit a window that's an odd size. Faux wood usually costs less. There's a free window treatment guide on our website, and the consultation is free too. Come see the options before you decide. Stop by the showroom, or have me come to you.
