3
Three steps to a quote, and a person on the other end
The thing the big competitor will not say
▾
It makes the company's own three step quote process concrete, then gives a homeowner the questions to put to any contractor. The insured and bonded line is something the site already says.
Getting a quote from us takes three steps. Send us a text. Talk on the phone. Get your quote. We do free estimates on construction and remodeling projects. Here is what to ask any contractor before you hire them. Are you insured? Are you bonded? When I text you, who answers? We are insured and bonded. One customer wrote that we expect our crews to take care of customers, and they deliver. Customers tell us that mattered. One wrote that from the first phone call until the final cleanup, they knew the project was in great hands. Another wrote that we were quick to respond to questions, quotes and timelines. He said we were willing to stay within or below his budget. Our site lists roofing, siding, windows, flooring, kitchens, bathrooms, decks, foundations and driveways. Send us a text and tell us what you need. Ask for the quote in writing. Ask what happens to the old materials and who cleans up. Ask who is on the crew. [OWNER: one thing you do on every job that other contractors skip] A contractor who answers all of that without hedging is one worth calling back. Those questions are fair. Free estimates mean you can ask before you commit. We like them. We would rather you ask them than guess.
4
Questions to ask before anybody climbs on your roof
The useful one
▾
Everybody in Ballinger needs a roof at some point, and few know what to ask. Handing over the checklist is something the owner already does on every quote, and it costs nothing to say.
If your roof needs replacing, here are the questions to ask before anybody climbs up there. First, are you insured and bonded? A roofer is working on top of your house, so you want the answer to be yes. Second, is the quote in writing? Put the scope and the price on paper, so everybody remembers the same job. Third, what happens to the old roofing? From the first phone call until the final cleanup, you should know what the plan is. One of our customers said that is how she knew her project was in great hands. Fourth, who is on the crew? A name and a face are worth asking for. Fifth, what about the porch and the carport? A lot of houses out here have metal roofs on the porches and carports and shingles on the house. [OWNER: your own take on metal versus shingles, in a sentence or two] We have done shingles and metal on the same property. Plenty of our customers hired us for a roof and then called back for the next project. If you have a quote in your hand right now, read it against that list. If you want a second opinion, we do free estimates. Send us a text and we will come look at it.
