The origin and the face
▾

3
Who answers the letter from the IRS in October?
The thing the big competitor will not say
▾
Most people only think about their preparer in April. Rudy's site lists year round planning and IRS notice and audit support, and this puts that promise on video. It is the one built to be forwarded to a friend who just got a letter.

Here's a question to ask anybody who does your taxes. Who's going to answer the letter from the IRS? Think about October, when your preparer's busy season is long over. That letter does come, for more people than you'd think.

At our firm, tax preparation is only one piece of it. We plan all year, through every season. If you get an IRS notice, we help you respond to it. If there's an audit, we support you through it. If you owe back taxes or penalties, we work on that with you. We also represent corporations before the IRS. And we advise on business structure, so you know what an LLC or an S corp means for your taxes.

Some places will prepare a return and see you next year. We'd rather know your business well enough to catch a problem in June. Our clients get proactive check-ins and quick responses. You are never an account number. Our site says that, and I mean it. So before you hire anybody, ask them one thing. Who's going to pick up the phone when the letter comes? If the answer is we'll see you next April, give us a call. Our number is 325 944 2536. We'll be here.
4
What a self-employed person should do every month so April is boring
The useful one
▾
Freelancers, contractors and small business owners are exactly who Rudy serves, and a short monthly checklist costs him nothing to give away. It earns the follow and shows a stranger how he thinks.
Spot A
If you work for yourself, here's what I'd do every month so April is boring. First, reconcile your bank and credit card statements. Match your records against what the bank says. Catching a mistake in March beats finding it the next spring.
Spot B
Second, categorize your expenses as you go. Don't leave a shoebox of receipts to sort in April. Organized and detailed beats a big pile every time. Third, if you pay people, keep track of who gets a 1099. Contractors, freelancers, gig workers, you've all got the same homework. Fourth, remember that estimated payments come four times a year. They land in April, June, September, and January. Keep your business money in its own account, so none of this gets tangled up.
Spot C
So set aside some of every payment you receive. [OWNER: the rough percentage you tell self-employed clients to set aside] If you run a business that charges sales tax, file it on time. It's easier to do these things monthly than in one weekend. And if all of that sounds like a lot, that's what we do. We keep the books, we prepare the returns, and we explain what the numbers mean in plain English. Call our office, 325 944 2536, and we'll walk through your first month with you.
