3
The wind picks up on pour day. Ask what your contractor does.
The thing the big competitor will not say
▾
West Texas heat, dry air and wind can crack a slab before it gains strength, and a bid never says what the crew does about it. Putting that question out there shows local judgment without naming another contractor.
Spot C
Every concrete contractor knows West Texas weather is hard on fresh concrete, and a lot of bids never mention it. Heat, dry air, and wind pull water off the surface faster than it can come up from below. When that happens, the surface can crack in the first day, before the concrete has gained its strength. Engineers call them plastic shrinkage cracks. Today's concrete mixes bleed less water than they used to, so the risk is higher. Dry, windy cold fronts in late winter and spring make it worse.
Spot A
There are ways to beat it. Start early, before the heat and wind pick up. Put up wind screens, or use a fog mist over the slab. Put extra people on the finishing and the curing so the crew keeps up. An evaporation retardant holds the moisture until the curing compound goes on, and it never replaces the compound. After the compound, a water cure follows. [OWNER: the wind speed or temperature where you move a pour or call it off] [OWNER: your curing routine]
Spot B
That takes judgment, and judgment comes from pouring here. We've been doing it in this part of Texas since 1960. So when a bid looks cheap, ask the contractor one question. What do you do when the wind picks up on pour day? [OWNER: your answer, in your own words] If a contractor can't answer that, keep looking.
4
How a tilt wall building goes up
The useful one
▾
People drive past a tilt wall job and wonder how a crane stands up a whole concrete wall. Explaining it takes ninety seconds, uses your own cranes and crew as the picture, and tells a commercial customer you know every step.
Spot A
If you've ever seen a crane lift a whole concrete wall into place, that's a tilt wall. It's one of the things we build. First the footings and the floor slab go in. Then the wall gets poured flat, right on that floor slab. Tilt wall is a common way to build warehouses and big box stores.
Spot B
The crew builds a form out of lumber on the slab. Steel rebar goes in, along with inserts for the crane and for the roof to connect to later. A bond breaker goes on the slab so the new wall won't stick to it. Then we pour the panel and finish it. The inserts are cast into the panel, and they're where the crane hooks on. Panels can weigh anywhere from fifty thousand to a hundred twenty five thousand pounds or more.
Spot C
When it's cured, a crane stands each panel up and sets it on the footing. Braces hold it while the next panel goes up. When the roof goes on, it ties the panels together. Some panels have insulation sandwiched between two layers of concrete. [OWNER: the biggest tilt wall building you've poured] If you're planning a building and want to know how the walls will go up, call us at 325 234 6696.
