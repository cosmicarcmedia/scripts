1
I came home to San Angelo to work next to my dad
The origin and the face
▾
People choose an eye doctor the way they choose a family doctor, by trusting the person. A second generation story that starts in San Angelo, leaves for school, and comes back to the office her father opened in 1983 is the fastest way to be known before anyone walks in.
Spot A
I grew up in San Angelo and graduated from Central High. Then I left for a while. The University of Texas in Austin for biology and chemistry, and the University of Houston for optometry school. In 2007, I came home. I came back to practice alongside my dad, in the office he started in 1983. [OWNER: what that first week next to your dad was like] Eventually, I bought the practice from him.
Spot B
[OWNER: why you came back to San Angelo] In 2019 we built a larger clinic on West Loop 306, so we could take care of more of this town. We're across the Loop from Texas Roadhouse, if you need directions. There are three of us here now. Dr. Hejny grew up in St. Lawrence, and went to Garden City High. Dr. Kirgis grew up here and went to Miles High School. So when you sit in our chair, you're sitting with people who grew up around here too.
Spot C
I'm a board certified therapeutic optometrist and an ocular glaucoma specialist. That means I can check your eyes, treat many eye diseases and infections, and fit your glasses and contacts, all in one place. This practice has been taking care of San Angelo for over forty years, and we see patients of all ages. [OWNER: a family you've seen across two or three generations] I'm Dr. Ashley Lamm David, and this is Lamm David Eye Care.
2
West Texas is hard on your eyes
The local anchor
▾
The practice's own dry eye page says dry eye is very common in West Texas because of sun, wind and allergies. A video that names the weather every San Angelo patient already lives in tells the town this clinic knows where it is, and sends people with scratchy eyes to the Dry Eye Clinic.
Spot B
You live in West Texas, so your eyes deal with sun, wind, and allergies all year. Dry eye is very common out here because of it. It can feel like dryness, scratchiness, burning, sometimes blurry vision. A lot of people just live with it. They figure it's part of the deal. Many of the people who come see us didn't know it could be treated. [OWNER: a patient story, no name, of someone who put up with it for years]
Spot C
Wind and allergies aren't the only causes. Dry air, getting older, hormone changes, and some medications, prescription and over the counter, can all play a part. Artificial tears can ease the scratchy feeling. They don't always do enough for the long run, because the cause is different for different people. That's why one bottle of drops from the drugstore doesn't settle it for everybody.
Spot A
We built a Dry Eye Clinic here for that reason. We look at what kind of dry eye you have and how severe it is, and we build a plan around you. Dr. Hejny and I both see dry eye patients. That plan might be prescription drops, tiny plugs that slow the drainage of tears, or a warm treatment for your eyelids. We're on West Loop 306, across from Texas Roadhouse. If your eyes feel like sandpaper by the time the wind kicks up, come see us.
3
Ask who is keeping the picture of the back of your eye
The thing the big competitor will not say
▾
Daytona retinal imaging with a saved record is part of the annual exam here, and the practice explains costs at the start of the visit. Putting both into one question patients can ask any eye office sets this practice apart without naming a single competitor.
Spot C
Next time you get your eyes checked, ask one question. Did anyone take a picture of the back of my eye, and is it going on file? Here, the answer is yes. It's part of our annual eye exam.
Spot A
It's a quick, wide picture of your retina. No drops, and in most cases no dilation. It's fast, and it's comfortable for all ages. Then you and I look at it together on the screen. Many eye diseases, glaucoma among them, are found by spotting changes over time. That's what the saved picture is for. Next year, we compare. If the place you go can't show you that picture, ask them why.
Spot B
Here's the other thing I'd ask anywhere. What is this going to cost? We explain what an exam and treatment will cost at the beginning of your appointment. And if you'd like a breakdown, just ask. We work with Medicare, Tricare, VSP, and a long list of other plans. Call the office and we'll tell you if we take yours. Even without vision insurance, some medical plans cover a yearly eye health exam. A good eye exam checks for glaucoma, cataracts, macular degeneration, and what diabetes and high blood pressure are doing to your eyes.
4
If glaucoma runs in your family, here is when to get screened
The useful one
▾
The practice's own September newsletter covers family history and screening age, and glaucoma is the condition this office is known for. It costs nothing to give away and gives every San Angelo family with a relative who has glaucoma a reason to call.
Spot A
If somebody in your family has glaucoma, here's what I'd want you to know. It can happen at any age, and for most people there's nothing to notice at the start. No pain, no blur. Once glaucoma causes vision loss you can notice, that vision doesn't come back. That's why we look for it before you can tell.
Spot B
Having a relative with glaucoma raises your risk. It doesn't mean you'll get it. If it's a brother or sister, the risk may be as much as ten times higher, according to the Glaucoma Research Foundation. The American Academy of Ophthalmology recommends a baseline screening at forty for everyone. With family history, your optometrist may recommend yearly screenings starting in your twenties or thirties. Especially if your relative was diagnosed young.
Spot C
So what happens at a screening? We check the pressure inside your eye. We take pictures that map your optic nerve. We run a visual field test, which measures how far you can see left, right, up and down while you look straight ahead. Then we talk about how often you should come back. If glaucoma runs in your family, bring that up at your next visit. We'll go through your family history together and set up a plan.
