1
What you get when you call Hog Creek Trailers
The origin and the face
▾
Shows San Angelo that the person on the other end of the phone is a named man at a family lot, and that Hog Creek sells and services what it sells. It gives the owner a face for a business that today is just a price-by-call page.
If you buy a trailer from us, you're buying it from a family. My name is Mike, and this is Hog Creek Trailers on North Chadbourne in San Angelo. It's family owned and family run. Raymond and John's numbers are on our page right under mine. [OWNER: the year you opened and how a family ends up in the trailer business] It's one lot, and you can walk the whole thing in a few minutes. New and used utility trailers, ten feet up to sixteen, with ramps or gates. Eighteen foot car haulers. Cargo trailers too, single axle and tandem. Steel built, with a one year warranty. I can tell you about every trailer on it. Here's how we do prices. The pictures on our page are examples, because what's on the lot changes with steel and parts. So if you want to know what we have today, call me. I'll tell you what's here and what it costs, and you don't have to guess from a photo. We do service and repair on utility and cargo trailers too. If we don't have a part on the shelf, we'll order it. So come by, tell me what you're hauling, and we'll find the one that fits. Then when you need us again, you already know who to call.
2
Trailers earn their keep out here
The local anchor
▾
Ties the lot on North Chadbourne to the way people in West Texas use a trailer, and gives the owner a place to drop in a real customer story that only he has. Ranchers, contractors and homeowners all recognize themselves in it.
West Texas runs on trailers. Hay, feed, fence posts, a four wheeler, a lawn mower, a whole house of furniture on moving day. If it has to get somewhere out here, it usually rides on a trailer. We're at 5260 North Chadbourne in San Angelo, on one small lot. [OWNER: a real example of what somebody hauled off this lot last week] Around here a trailer doesn't sit in the garage for show. It goes to the ranch, the job site and the feed store, and it goes a long way on roads with not much around. That's why we keep the lineup simple. Utility trailers from ten to sixteen feet, with ramps or gates, so you pick how you want to load. Cargo trailers when the load has to stay dry and locked up. We have them in a few sizes, from five by ten up to seven by fourteen. Eighteen foot car haulers for the truck, the car, or the project that doesn't run yet. [OWNER: your hours, and whether Saturday is open] Call Mike at 325 212 2282, or come on out. Tell me what you haul and how far you haul it. If it's the same ten miles every weekend, you need one trailer. If it's three hundred miles of highway, you probably need another. I'll tell you which trailer holds up to that, and we'll get you down the road.
3
Ask who you call when the trailer needs work
The thing the big competitor will not say
▾
Draws a fair line against the big sellers by laying out what Hog Creek already does: a warranty, service and repair, ordered parts, honest inventory, and the owner answering the phone. It is the one a neighbor forwards before a trailer purchase.
Here's a question to ask anybody you buy a trailer from. When something goes wrong in a year, who do I call? At our lot, it's a phone that rings on North Chadbourne. Mine is 325 212 2282, and Raymond's and John's numbers are on our page too. Some places sell you a trailer and you never see them again. We put a one year warranty on what we sell, and we do the service and repair ourselves. If a part isn't on the shelf, we'll order it. You aren't driving to another town to find somebody who knows trailers. Here's one nobody likes to say out loud. Right now we don't always have the size you want. Steel and parts have been hard to get, and our own page says so. Some sizes and styles are limited, and the pictures are only examples. I'd rather tell you that on the phone than watch you drive across town for a trailer that isn't here. So call first, and I'll tell you what's on the lot. And ask about the price. You get it from the person who sets it. No chat window, no form, no waiting two days on a callback. You call, I answer, and we talk about the trailer.
4
Pick the right trailer before you call
The useful one
▾
Gives anyone shopping for a trailer a simple checklist that costs Hog Creek nothing, and every point ties back to a real model on the lot. It earns the follow and sends the right buyer to the phone.
Before you buy a trailer, answer three questions. What are you hauling, how heavy is it, and how often are you hauling it. Start with the weight. Add up the load, then add the weight of the trailer itself. Look at the rating tag on the trailer and keep the total under it. Check what your truck is rated to pull, too, because the trailer is only half of that number. Next, ramp or gate. A ramp lets you roll on a mower or a four wheeler. A gate is easier for a load you stack by hand. We have both on our utility trailers, ten to sixteen feet. Then, single axle or tandem. A single axle is lighter and easier to move around the yard. A tandem carries more and rides steadier. Our six by twelve single axle cargo trailer rides on one 3,500 pound axle. Our tandem cargo trailers run two of them, with brakes. More axles and brakes means a steadier trailer behind you on a long West Texas highway. Last thing, and it's cheap. Ask about a spare tire mount. Nobody plans a flat, and the highway between towns is a long walk. It's a small add-on, and it's a lot easier to put on at the lot than on the shoulder.
One more idea
What to add the day you buy

Walk the lot and show the three small things buyers wish they had added: a spare tire mount, a bug guard and a V-nose strip, all on your cargo trailer page. Pick one trailer, point at each add-on, and say what it costs to put on at purchase versus later.
