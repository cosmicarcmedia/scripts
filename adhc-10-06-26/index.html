4
How to find a hidden leak with your water meter and food coloring
Useful customer education
▾
The leak checks are already on the company's blog, so on camera they earn the follow from every homeowner with an unexplained water bill, and the ones who can't find the leak know who to call.
Spot A
Your water bill went up, and nobody in the house changed anything. So where is the water going? But water travels along the framing before it shows, so the stain isn't always where the leak is. A small drip can turn into real damage faster than most people expect. You can find it yourself with the water meter and a bottle of food coloring.
Spot B
Turn off every water source in the house, then watch the meter. If it keeps moving, water is flowing somewhere. Next, put a few drops of food coloring in the toilet tank and don't flush. If the color shows up in the bowl, water is slipping past the flapper. Silent toilet leaks are common, and they can waste a lot of water over time.
Spot C
Then look behind the dishwasher, the washing machine, and the water heater. Cracked hoses and loose fittings are common leak sources. Press on the floor near sinks and tubs. If it feels soft or springy, water has been there a while. Check the ceilings for yellow spots, peeling paint, or bubbles. Then look at your last few water bills. A steady climb with no explanation is a sign of a leak. If the meter is still moving after all that, call a plumber and tell them what you already checked.
