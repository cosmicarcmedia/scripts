The sausage the hunters kept coming back for
The origin and the face
▾

Since 2005, hunters all over West Texas have brought us their deer. I'm Jack Graves, and Kim and I own J-Bar Meat Company here in San Angelo. Back then we were J's Processing. 3
Ask who made the box before you buy it
The thing the big competitor will not say
▾
Gift boxes are where a small shop with real owners beats a big mail-order name. You can name the aging, the suppliers and the people on the phone, and you never have to name a competitor to make the point.
Spot C
If you're buying a meat gift box this year, ask one question first. Who made it? Can you call them, and will they know what's in the box? When you call J-Bar Meat Company at 325 777 0404, the phone rings in San Angelo. Somebody on our team answers. Ask where the beef comes from. Ask how long the steaks were aged. And ask what happens if something goes wrong with the order.
Spot A
In our boxes and on our counter, you'll find sausage we make and steaks aged sixty days. Our steaks include sixty day aged ribeye, strip, and filet. [OWNER: where the aging happens and why sixty days changes a steak] Sixty days of aging takes time and space, and the price reflects it. We carry prime steaks too. Prime is the top USDA grade, and it comes down to the marbling. That's the kind of thing you can ask us at the counter.
Spot B
We buy our beef and pork from suppliers we trust, and we pay close attention to freshness. [OWNER: which items in the case you make yourselves] [OWNER: what you do when an order arrives wrong] A customer wrote that we're a family owned business with years of knowledge and experience. Another one said we have the friendliest and most helpful staff. We'll take both. So when somebody asks who stands behind the box, I can say my name and Kim's.
4
How to cook a thick steak so the middle stays pink
The useful one
▾
People spend real money on a ribeye and then ruin it in the first two minutes. Teaching the reverse sear costs nothing, makes viewers better at the thing you sell, and one of your own customers already described it in a review.
Spot A
If you bought a thick steak, don't ruin it in the first two minutes. Most people throw it on a screaming hot grill. The outside burns and the middle stays cold. The thicker the steak, the worse that gets. The fix is called a reverse sear.
Spot B
Let the steak sit out for a bit before you cook it. Pat it dry and salt it on both sides. Thick means an inch and a half or more. Now cook it low. A 250 degree oven works, or the cool side of your smoker or grill. Pull it when the middle is about fifteen degrees short of where you want to end up. [OWNER: the temperature you pull a ribeye at for medium rare] Let it rest a few minutes. That rest is part of the cooking.
Spot C
Now get a pan or a grill as hot as you can. Sear it for a minute or so on each side. That's all the crust it needs. A customer told us he smoked a Wagyu tri tip roast for an hour, then reverse seared it. He said it came out a perfect medium rare. Slice it against the grain. And use a thermometer. This works on a thick ribeye or a strip. Then tell me how it turned out.
