The scripts

1 The town name is a corrected typo Chadbourne block

The people who founded this place wanted to call it Santa Angela. The post office turned it down in 1883. Not because of a duplicate name, and not for anything political. They rejected it on grammar.

Santa Angela was the wrong construction in Spanish, so the name became San Angelo, and that has been it ever since. A hundred and forty some years of a city carrying a name it got because a federal clerk marked somebody's paperwork wrong. I think about that every time a person tells me a West Texas town has no story in it.

2 Conrad Hilton built this and you can live in it Cactus Hotel

Frasier spent eleven seasons in an apartment with a view he would not stop talking about. You can do a version of that here, in the tallest building in town. The Cactus Hotel opened in 1929 as Conrad Hilton's fourth hotel. Fourteen stories, a hundred sixty-five feet, and almost a century later it is still the tallest building in San Angelo.

Hilton sold it during the Depression in 1932 and it picked up the Cactus name in 1934. It went on the National Register in 1984. Here is the part people do not know. It stopped taking overnight guests a long time ago, and it is not a museum.

There are offices in it, the symphony is in it, and there are apartments in it. Some of the units on the fourth and fifth floors still have the original 1929 bathroom tile. People walk past that building every day of their lives and assume it is closed.

3 This building was boarded up eighteen months ago Roosevelt Lofts

The Roosevelt opened as the Rainbow Hotel in 1929 and got renamed in 1933. Then it sat. It just came out of a six and a half million dollar renovation as twenty-five loft apartments, and it opened last September. People live there now.

Between this building and the apartments inside the Cactus, there is a real answer to a question I get asked constantly, which is whether anybody actually lives downtown. They do, the number is going up, and it is happening inside buildings that had been written off. If you have been picturing downtown San Angelo as a place you visit and then drive home from, that picture is about two years out of date.

4 One man designed forty buildings down here Cactus Hotel

An architect named Oscar Ruffini designed something like forty buildings in downtown San Angelo. Several of them are still standing and still in use. Then there is Anton Korn, who designed the two buildings most people would point at first if you asked them to describe this skyline. The San Angelo National Bank building in 1927.

The Cactus Hotel in 1929. Same architect, two years apart. That is the thing about a downtown that got built fast in one stretch of years. It is more of a set than it looks like.

These buildings talk to each other because a handful of people drew all of them, and once you know that, you cannot unsee it walking down Chadbourne.

5 Empty since 1983 and still looking Texas Theatre

The Texas Theatre opened on Thanksgiving Day in 1929. Two thousand seats, three hundred thousand dollars, billed as the biggest theater in West Texas. It closed in 1983. It is still sitting here, and it is still listed as looking for a tenant.

Forty some years empty. Every town has a building like this one. The thing I would say about it is that a building sitting empty is not the same as a building with no value. It is a building waiting on somebody who has a use for it and the patience to get through the approvals.

Those two things almost never show up in the same person at the same time. When they do, that is how a downtown turns around.

6 Forty-five cents back on every rehab dollar Roosevelt Lofts

If you buy an old building downtown and fix it up right, the government pays you back a chunk of it. The federal historic rehabilitation credit is twenty percent of qualified rehab spending on a certified historic structure. Texas has its own credit on top of that, twenty-five percent, against state franchise tax. Stack them and you are looking at up to forty-five percent back on one project.

The Texas credit takes at least five thousand dollars of qualified spend, it is transferable, and it carries forward up to five years. There is also a downtown reinvestment zone here that funds facade and preservation grants, and it is a big reason the Chadbourne corridor filled up with bars and restaurants. This is the part of downtown real estate nobody brings up at a dinner party. The buildings look like liabilities.

For the right buyer with the right plan, the math on them beats anything new construction can offer.

7 The city told a bank no Chadbourne block

In January of 2025, a bank asked the city for permission to tear down a building downtown. The commission said no, six to nothing. The council backed them up unanimously. The building sits at 231 West Beauregard, and the widely held belief was that it was coming down for parking.

The mayor's line about it was that the city cannot keep taking out buildings and leaving empty lots. I bring that up because it is the clearest answer I can give you about what buying inside the downtown historic district actually means. There is a district down here with real boundaries. If you own a designated building in it, exterior changes, new construction and demolition all run through a certificate of appropriateness.

That is not a formality somebody rubber stamps. So if you are buying down here planning to change the outside of the building, find out what is designated and what is not before you make an offer.

8 A whole neighborhood here does not own its own dirt Chadbourne block

There is a neighborhood in San Angelo where the people living in those houses do not own the ground underneath them. Out at Lake Nasworthy, the lake and the land around it belong to the City of San Angelo. The city runs a lot lease program through its real estate division, so residents own the house and lease the dirt. There is an annual lease fee on top of whatever the house cost.

And it is a real lease with real teeth. In December of 2024 the city council took up terminating one of them and evicting the leaseholder over about three thousand dollars in unpaid fees. I am not telling you that to scare anybody off the lake. Plenty of people out there love it.

I am telling you because if you are looking at lake property and nobody has walked you through the difference between owning and leasing yet, you are not being taken care of. Ask what conveys. Ask what the annual fee is. Ask what happens at renewal.

9 Every branch of the military trains in this town Neutral downtown

Goodfellow is not a small thing sitting on the edge of San Angelo. Every branch trains there. Air Force, Army, Marines, Navy and Space Force. The base contributes about three point eight billion dollars to the Texas economy, supports right around thirteen thousand jobs counting the ripple effects, and the training group graduates close to fourteen thousand students a year.

That does something to a housing market, and most people here stopped noticing because it has always been true. Here is the part that matters if you served. Nearly ninety percent of VA backed home loans close with no money down. A VA loan never requires private mortgage insurance, which is a line most buyers pay every single month and never get back.

There is a funding fee, and it depends on your down payment and whether it is your first time using the benefit. If you are stationed here, or you got out and stayed, and you have been assuming you need twenty percent saved, go find out what you actually qualify for.

10 You can own the property and not what is under it Neutral downtown

Everybody has watched a show about West Texas oil by now. Here is the part those shows skip. In Texas you can own the house, the fence, the well and every inch of the lot, and still not own what is underneath it. The mineral estate can be split off from the surface and sold separately.

Once it is, the law treats the mineral estate as the dominant one. Not equal to the surface. Dominant. Which means whoever owns the minerals under your yard has an implied legal right to come onto your surface and go get them. They do not need your permission.

There are real limits on that. They have to give you notice before drilling a new well, they cannot be negligent, and there is a doctrine that protects you when they have a reasonable alternative and you do not. But the starting point is not what most buyers assume when they hear the word own. When you are under contract, read the deed for reservation language and look at Schedule B of your title commitment.

If the minerals were reserved out of that property at some point in the last hundred years, that is where it shows up.

11 Ron Swanson would move here for the water law Neutral downtown

Ron Swanson spent seven seasons arguing the government should have as little to do with your property as humanly possible. I think about that man every time I explain Texas groundwater to somebody moving here. Because in Texas, the water under your land belongs to you. Not the state. You.

You can pump it, sell it, lease it, or haul it off the property. It is called the rule of capture, and people around here call it the law of the biggest pump. That is the part Ron would like less. Your neighbor's water is his in exactly the same way, and he can pump as much of it as he wants.

If he drills deeper and pumps harder and your well runs dry because of it, that is generally his water now. He does not owe you anything unless he did it out of spite, or waste, or plain negligence. What keeps it from being chaos is the local groundwater district, and the one for this county is headquartered out in Wall. So the water is yours.

So is the risk.

12 Your tap water drives sixty-two miles to reach you Neutral downtown

Here is one that surprises people who have lived in San Angelo their whole life. Part of this city's drinking water does not come from around here at all. It is pumped out of the Hickory Aquifer from wells about fifteen miles south of Melvin, over in McCulloch County, and piped sixty-two miles to get to town. That water comes up carrying naturally occurring radium, so the city has to treat it and strip that out before it ever reaches a faucet.

Meanwhile a well drilled out in the county is most likely pulling from the Edwards-Trinity, a completely different aquifer. Same general part of Texas, two entirely different water stories. That is worth knowing when somebody tells you country water and city water are the same deal. They are not.

13 This dirt paid for two universities Neutral downtown

About an hour and a half west of here, outside Big Lake, there is a patch of ground nobody wanted. The state had handed it to the University of Texas back when West Texas land was considered close to worthless. In May of 1923 a well called Santa Rita Number One came in on it. That was the first commercial oil well in the Permian Basin.

The first royalty check the university got was five hundred sixteen dollars. In the twelve months after that well hit, the fund behind it grew four hundred forty-five percent. It had never cracked a million dollars in the forty years before. That fund is worth better than forty billion dollars today, and it still splits between the UT system and the A&M system.

All of it off land people had written off as good for nothing but goats. That is the thing about ground out here. What it is worth on top and what it is worth underneath are two different questions. Most people only ever ask the first one.

14 The joke asset was the only one left standing Chadbourne block

In Schitt's Creek, Johnny Rose buys an entire town in 1991 as a gag birthday present for his son. For years it is the dumbest thing he ever spent money on. Then the family loses everything, the mansion gets seized, and that town is the one asset still standing. It is a comedy premise.

It is also the most honest thing television has said about small town real estate. Ninety-five percent of the land in Texas is privately owned. More than a hundred forty-two million acres of it is somebody's farm, ranch or timber ground. It does not trade like a stock, it never shows up on the news, and people from bigger markets look out here and see nothing happening.

Then something goes sideways somewhere else, and the boring asset in the country is the one that is still there. I am not going to tell you land only goes up, because it does not. I am telling you the thing everybody laughs at is worth understanding before you laugh at it.

15 Hank Hill would understand this question Neutral downtown

Hank Hill sold propane and propane accessories in a fictional Texas town called Arlen, and nobody watching that show ever thought it was a strange way to make a living. Out here it makes even more sense. The second you cross the city limit, there is no gas line running to the house. The stove, the water heater, the furnace, all of it runs off a tank sitting in the yard, and somebody drives out and fills it.

So if you are shopping outside of town, there is a question most buyers never think to ask. Do you own that tank, or is it leased from the propane company? If it is leased, you buy your propane from that company at their price, and that is the arrangement. If you own it, you can shop it around every fall.

Over a cold winter that is a real number. Ask before you close. Hank would.

16 Your school taxes just got cheaper Neutral downtown

This one is money in your pocket and plenty of people have not caught up to it. The homestead exemption in Texas is the amount knocked off your home's value before school taxes get calculated. It is now a hundred forty thousand dollars. Voters raised it again in November.

It was a hundred thousand before that, and forty thousand before that, so it has gone up three times in three election cycles. If you are sixty-five or older, or disabled, you get another sixty thousand on top of that, which puts you at two hundred thousand exempted from school tax. Your city and county can each add a local exemption of up to twenty percent of appraised value on top of that. Two things to do with this.

If you bought recently and never filed for your homestead exemption, go file it. It is free and people forget. And if somebody is quoting you a monthly payment on a house, make sure they used the current number and not an old one.
