1
I went to business school because I wanted to open a flower company
The origin and the face
▾
Rachell's path from Lubbock and a business degree to a one-woman floral studio in San Angelo gives brides and everyday customers a face to trust before they ever inquire.
Spot A
I went to business school because I knew I wanted to open a flower company. That's Texas Tech, the Rawls College of Business, and I grew up in Lubbock. Then my husband had the chance to open a branch of his company in San Angelo, and I moved here to help him build it. I came to build a dream of my own, too. [OWNER: the year you opened Dahlias & Daisies, and your very first order]
Spot B
Dahlias & Daisies started with a love for people first, and flowers a very close second. My style is whimsical and artful, a modern take on garden style flowers. Every arrangement starts with the person it's for, the space it lives in, and the feeling it needs to create. Dahlias & Daisies is a private studio, locally rooted and growing right here in the Concho Valley. Birthdays. Anniversaries. A Tuesday that needed something beautiful. [OWNER: a bouquet or an arrangement you're proudest of, and who it was for]
Spot C
I'm also a businesswoman. I understand budgets and timelines, and what it means to show up when someone is counting on you. Artistry and accountability. That's what I bring to every wedding, every delivery, every stem. So when you work with Dahlias & Daisies, you work with me, from the first inquiry to the moment the bouquet is in your hands. I'm Rachell Vaden, and I hope it shows.
2
What flowers look like in the Concho Valley's wedding venues
The local anchor
▾
Your venue pages already show you know Christoval Vineyards, Ruffini Chapel and Star Ranch, and talking about them by name makes local couples feel you belong to their setting.
Spot C
Fifteen minutes from San Angelo, there's a vineyard in Christoval with a limestone chapel and tall oak trees. At golden hour, the light in that place is the best thing about it. So I design flowers that belong in the landscape, with loose, airy shapes and a lot of movement. [OWNER: your favorite Christoval Vineyards wedding detail]
Spot A
Back in town, Ruffini Chapel has tall windows and soft natural light. It's a blank canvas, so I keep the flowers garden style and let the light do half the work. Out at Star Ranch, it's open sky and long views, and the flowers get to look relaxed and a little wild. Every venue tells a different story, and I design for the one you're in.
Spot B
Here's a tip that works at all three. A lot of your ceremony flowers can move to the reception. That stretches your budget, and it keeps the same look from the aisle to the dance floor. I deliver and set up at the venue for you. I also design weddings in Fredericksburg, Abilene, Lubbock, Midland and Odessa. And on an ordinary weekday I'm delivering all over San Angelo, and out to Wall and Christoval. If your day is somewhere else in West Texas, ask me.
3
Three questions to ask any florist before you order
The thing the big competitor will not say
▾
Your own pages answer all three: you arrange the flowers, you send a photo, and you print your prices, which is the line a catalog service or a grocery counter would rather not draw.
Spot B
Before you order flowers from anybody, ask who is going to arrange them. When you order from me, you're working directly with me. I design every order fresh in my San Angelo studio. And you get a photo when it's ready, before it goes out the door. Order before three o'clock, and I can deliver the same day in San Angelo while slots remain.
Spot C
Then ask if they'll look like the picture. Mine won't look identical to anything, because I work with a living product. I buy the best of what's in season, and I don't repeat a design. My Daily Bloom arrangement is made from the freshest flowers in the studio that day. Your colors and your style get matched, but I can't promise you a particular stem. Anybody who promises you every single stem is promising more than a living product can deliver.
Spot A
Then ask what it will cost, and whether they'll tell you before you fill out a form. Everyday arrangements here start at fifty dollars, and the price is on the page. Full service wedding florals start at three thousand dollars. That number is on the first page of my website, and I wrote a whole cost guide so couples know what they're walking into. I'm glad to answer every one of those.
4
What wedding flowers cost in San Angelo, line by line
The useful one
▾
Your Wedding Flower Cost Guide already publishes the ranges, and saying them out loud turns a scary question into the thing that makes couples trust you before the first call.
Spot A
Most couples I work with spend between three and ten thousand dollars on wedding flowers. Here's where it goes. A bridal bouquet runs about two hundred to three hundred fifty dollars. Bridesmaid bouquets are ninety five to a hundred fifty each. Boutonnieres are eighteen to thirty dollars. Ceremony flowers start around two fifty and can go past nine hundred.
Spot B
Reception centerpieces run about seventy five to three fifty and up. A statement installation, like an arch or flowers hanging overhead, starts around a thousand and can reach five thousand. Which flowers you pick matters, because garden roses, peonies and orchids cost more. So does the season, because some flowers only grow at certain times of year. How lush you want it matters too, because abundant designs take a lot more stems. And so does how much setup the design needs.
Spot C
If your budget is smaller than three thousand dollars, that's fine. I have a simpler à la carte option for elopements and small weddings. And remember, many ceremony arrangements can be reused at the reception. Fill out the inquiry form with your date and your budget, and I'll tell you what I can do. I'd rather you hear the numbers from me.
