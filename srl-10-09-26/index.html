1
Sonora kid, two deployments, and a shop on Concho Avenue
The origin and the face
▾
A veteran owner with a hunting family and a mechanic's training is the whole brand. This puts the man behind the shop name in front of Sonora before anyone has to guess who Sergeant R is.
Spot A
I graduated from Sonora High School in 2005. I went to Texas State Technical College and got my automotive technology degree in 2007. That same year I joined the Army infantry.
Spot B
In 2009 I deployed to Iraq with the 10th Mountain. In 2011 I deployed to Afghanistan with the 504th Battlefield Surveillance Brigade. I got out in 2012 as a sergeant, with an honorable discharge. [OWNER: what you did between the Army and starting the shop.]
Spot C
In late 2021 I opened Sergeant R, at 115 North Concho in Sonora. Veteran owned and operated. [OWNER: where the name Sergeant R comes from.] My family has roots in this country going back to the late 1800s. I've hunted our family ranch in north Sutton County since I was a kid. Now I own that land, and I plan to pass it down to my son. That's why I do what I do. Sergeant R sells and services for all your farm, ranch, and hunting needs. Farm, ranch, and hunting people are my people. I know what it costs when a machine goes down in the middle of the season. Come in and say hello. Stop by 115 North Concho and ask for Richard. We're open Monday through Friday, eight to six.
2
Can't haul it to town? We'll come to the ranch.
The local anchor
▾
Ranch country makes a field service call a real advantage, and the shop's hunting competition gives the town a reason to know the name. Together they make a local anchor nobody online can copy.
Spot B
Sonora sits in ranch country. When a skid steer or a four wheeler goes down out at the ranch, hauling it to town costs you a day. We know that, because we hunt and work ranch land ourselves. So we offer field service. That's on site repair for people who can't get the machine to us.
Spot C
Here at the shop on Concho Avenue we work on automotive, ATVs, UTVs, and small engines. We use Alldata, and we keep parts for Echo and Briggs and Stratton. We rent skid steers, augers, trenchers, and Ditch Witches. Zero turn mowers too. It's all in one place downtown, and the parts department keeps growing.
Spot A
We've also held a varmint hunting competition at the shop. It runs twenty three hours, starting at 12:01 on Saturday afternoon and ending at the weigh in Sunday morning. Teams of two to four. [OWNER: how many teams came out last time, and is another one planned.] If you grew up out here, you know what that weekend is like. Sonora is a small town, and I like it when a lot of it shows up in one parking lot. 115 North Concho, 325 387 3400. Ask us about field service when you call.
3
Ask the website to drive out to your ranch
The thing the big competitor will not say
▾
The sharpest of the four. It never names a retailer, and every question in it is one an online ranch store or big box cannot answer by phone.
Spot C
Anybody can sell you an auger on the internet. The internet is a good place to order a part. It can't tell you why your machine won't start. So ask the website a few questions. Can you fix my four wheeler when it breaks? Can you rent me one while mine is down? Will somebody drive out to the ranch?
Spot A
At Sergeant R, we work on vehicles, ATVs, UTVs, and small engines. We offer field service for people who can't get their equipment to town. We rent skid steers and the attachments to go with them. We also do custom welding and CNC plasma cutting. That means a bracket or a gate hinge can get made in the same building where you picked up the parts.
Spot B
I'm a veteran and a mechanic by training. I learned this trade at Texas State Technical College. I know a machine, and I know the country it works in. [OWNER: one repair that surprised a customer.] When your machine goes down, you don't need a tracking number. You need somebody who answers when you call 325 387 3400. Call and ask what's on the shelf, or what's available to rent. I'm at 115 North Concho in Sonora, Monday through Friday, eight to six.
4
Before hunting season, check these five things on your UTV
The useful one
▾
Free, seasonal, and the exact thing a hunter wants to hear the week before the lease opens. It earns the follow and puts the shop's name in front of every UTV owner in Sutton County.
Spot A
Hunting season is coming. Before you haul the UTV or the four wheeler out to the lease, check five things. It takes about twenty minutes, and it can save your whole weekend. Do it the weekend before, so you have time to fix what you find.
Spot B
First, the tires. Check the pressure and look for cuts on the sidewalls. A flat on a rocky lease road will ruin a good morning. Second, the oil. Look at the level and the color, and change it if you can't remember when you did last. Third, the air filter. West Texas is dusty, and a clogged filter is the fastest way to choke an engine. If it's packed with dust, clean it or swap it.
Spot C
Fourth, the battery. If it sat all summer, check it and charge it up before you leave the house. Fifth, the gas. Gas that sat since last season can gum up a carburetor. [OWNER: the one thing you see break most often right before season.] If any of that looks bad, bring it by. I'm at 115 North Concho in Sonora. You'd much rather find out in the shop than ten miles from the nearest road. Call us at 325 387 3400, and we'll tell you what to do.
