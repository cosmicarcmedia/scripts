1
My dad started this roofing company in 1981 with a small idea
The origin and the face
▾
Customers already ask for Brandon by name in their reviews, and the company's story is a father who started it in 1981 and a son who joined in 2011 after Texas A&M. This puts the family and the face on camera.
Spot A
My dad started this roofing company in San Angelo in 1981 with a small idea. I'm Brandon, and I joined him in 2011, after I graduated from Texas A&M. My dad is Jim Cain, and he and I lead it together. Forty-five years in, a lot of customers write that I'm the one who came out to look at their roof.
Spot B
We're a full-service roofing and construction company. Reroofs, new roofs and repairs, for homes and for commercial buildings. We're fully licensed and insured, and we work with TAMKO, Owens Corning, IKO, GAF and metal. We work out of San Angelo, and our service area runs from Ballinger and Brady down to Sonora and Ozona. Our mission is to perform excellent work, safely, at reasonable prices and on schedule.
Spot C
One customer wrote that she called three or four roofing companies in San Angelo, and we were the only one that returned her call. Another likes that we're family owned. A roof we did in 105 degree heat went on in a single day, and the owner wrote about it. A couple wrote that they'd give us ten stars, that people keep complimenting their new roof, and that I communicated well during and after the job. I treat your roof like it's mine, from the first call to the final cleanup. The estimate is free.
2
On May 28, 1995, hail was six inches deep in parts of San Angelo
The local anchor
▾
San Angelo has had two historic hailstorms in this company's lifetime, 1995 and 2020, and the 1995 storm hit fourteen years in. Opening on it tells the town this company was here for it, then ties it to Class IV shingles and a free inspection.
Spot B
On May 28, 1995, a supercell hit San Angelo. It brought extreme winds and giant hail. At least eighty people were hurt, and the damage came to about a hundred and twenty million dollars. Sixty-one homes were destroyed, and more than nine thousand were slightly damaged. In some places the hail was six inches deep, with drifts to two feet.
Spot A
My dad's company was fourteen years old that day. In May of 2020, San Angelo got golf ball hail, some of it tennis ball size. It lasted about three hours on a Thursday night and damaged home roofs and car windshields. People called it the worst since 1995. We were here for both. One customer wrote that after a storm we put on a new roof and explained everything upfront, with no surprises.
Spot C
We offer Class IV shingles. They're built for hail country, and many insurers offer premium reductions for them. They're typically rated to resist winds of 110 to 130 miles an hour or more. They can also be a selling point in a storm-prone market. Owens Corning and IKO both make Class 4 shingles, and we can show you the brochures. Not sure whether your roof took damage? We'll come out and inspect it for free to see if there's anything that qualifies for a repair or an insurance claim.
3
After a hailstorm, look at the license plates on the roofers' trucks
The thing the big competitor will not say
▾
The company's own Roofing Basics page already tells homeowners how to spot an out of town storm chaser, down to the license plates on the trucks. Putting that on camera in Brandon's voice draws the fair line without naming anyone.
Spot C
After a hailstorm, look at the license plates on the roofers' trucks. Sounds like a strange thing to check. But it's on our website. Some roofing outfits go from hailstorm to hailstorm, and they can leave homeowners holding the bag. Make sure your roofer works full time, year round, with long roots in San Angelo and the Concho Valley.
Spot A
Where is your roofer from? Don't rely on a salesman to tell you. Ask for a two page reference sheet, and call the references. Call a local roofing supply house and ask about the company's history.
Spot B
And don't give any roofer a down payment. If a contractor can't finance your job himself, he has no business working on your house. You pay when the work is finished. Our office is at 1144 Glenna Street in San Angelo. Drive by. We've been family owned since 1981. A customer with a metal roof wrote that when a leak showed up in a closet, I traced it to his chimney, which needed a construction contractor, and I told him there was no charge. That's one of the things we do for our customers. A customer in Brady wrote that I sealed both her skylights and fixed a few other things at no added charge. Tell the out of town storm chasers to hit the road. And share this with your neighbors, your church, and the seniors you know.
4
Think hail hit your roof? You can't tell from the ground
The useful one
▾
The company's own Roofing Basics page lists where to look for hail damage before anyone climbs a ladder. Giving the yard-level checks away earns the follow and the free inspection call.
Spot A
Think hail hit your roof? You can't tell from the ground. A roofer has to get up there and look at the shingles. But there are signs you can check from the yard. Look at your cedar fence for impact marks. Look at your roof vents and turbines for dents.
Spot B
Look at your car, the windshield, the hood, the roof and the trunk lid. Look for branches and leaves the storm knocked off your trees. If you see any of that, your roof may have taken hail.
Spot C
What a roofer wants to see is the protective granules knocked off the shingle, exposing the fiberglass mat underneath. The mat shows a bruise, it's soft to the touch, and it can even be torn. That mat isn't supposed to sit in direct sunlight. If it stays exposed for long, you'll get a leak. If you're not sure your roof has storm damage, we'll inspect it for free. Multiple impact marks on two or three slopes is the kind of damage that can qualify for a replacement. And if the damage is real, turn in the claim. You can ask for a roof inspection, a repair estimate or a replacement estimate, and they're all free. Give us a call, and we'll send someone out promptly to inspect your roof.
