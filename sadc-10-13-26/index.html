3
Ask your garage door company what it charges at two in the morning
The thing the big competitor will not say
▾
This is the question a customer rarely thinks to ask until the door is stuck at night. The shop's own pages promise the same rate around the clock, honest cost and time up front, and trim included on standard installs. Laid out as questions, it draws the line without naming anyone.
Spot A
Here's what I'd ask, if it were my door. Before you call a garage door company in the middle of the night, ask what the call costs. Ask if the rate changes after hours. Ours doesn't. It's the same rate, twenty four seven.
Spot C
Ask if they'll tell you the cost and the time before they start. Our technicians are up front and honest about both. Ask if they'll repair it or only replace it. We service, repair, or replace all the components of your door, and we'll tell you which one it needs. Ask if they'll look for problems that could come back later. Ours do. [OWNER: how you vet and train the technicians who come to the house] Honest service for a fair price means you get answers to every one of those questions before you say yes. 4
Four garage door checks you can do this weekend
The useful one
▾
Safety checks cost a customer nothing and keep a family safe, and each one comes from the garage door industry's own trade association, DASMA. It shows the shop knows doors and sends the one dangerous job, springs and cables, to a trained technician.
Spot A
Once a month, test your garage door's safety reversal. Lay a two by four flat on the floor, under the door. Close the door with the opener. It has to reverse when it touches the board. A roll of paper towels is no substitute, because it can compress too far. Do it again after any repair, too. If it doesn't reverse, don't turn up the force setting. The sensitivity adjustment may be set wrong, and that's a job for a trained technician.
Spot B
Next, balance. If your door feels heavy, or takes two hands to lift, it's probably out of balance. Openers are built for doors that are already balanced. Then look at the photo eyes by the floor. They should be no higher than six inches above it. And the wall button belongs at least five feet up, out of children's reach.
Spot C
Now the one to leave alone. The industry association DASMA says springs are arguably the most dangerous part of your door. Frayed cables can break too. If your door has two springs and one breaks, DASMA says replace both. Both springs and cables are a job for a trained technician. Never put your fingers between the door sections. DASMA also recommends a yearly inspection by a trained door technician. [OWNER: one thing your techs find most often on an inspection]
One more idea
From stuck to working

Film one repair from the moment the door or gate won't move to the moment it does. Open on the problem, say what you found, and finish with it running. Melony C.'s thank you is the closing line.
