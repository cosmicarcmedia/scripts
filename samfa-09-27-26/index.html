The scripts

1
A supply building at Fort Concho to a building people fly in to see
The origin and the face
▾
Tells the real arc from a repurposed Army supply building to an internationally recognized building on the river, since most locals only know the museum as it looks today and never hear how it got there.
Spot A
This museum's story starts somewhere else entirely. It started inside Fort Concho, in an old Army quartermaster building from 1868. A supply depot, turned into an art museum in 1981.
Spot B
Four years later, in 1985, we opened to the public with pieces on loan from the National Portrait Gallery and the Library of Congress. Two Washington institutions, lending real work to a museum inside an old fort in West Texas. [OWNER: what made you want to work in a museum, and what keeps you here] By 1999 we'd outgrown the fort completely and moved into this building on the Concho River, which went on to get international recognition for its design. We've held over three hundred and fifty exhibitions since we opened, everything from Renaissance printmaking to Spanish Colonial religious art to contemporary ceramics. Along the way we built a permanent collection of our own. Eight hundred contemporary ceramic pieces. More than sixty Texas paintings and sculptures. Over a hundred pieces of Spanish Colonial religious art.
Spot C
Most people who walk in today only know us as the building on the river. They don't know we started in a supply depot four blocks from here. That's the part worth knowing. So the next time you're standing in a gallery looking at the river, you're standing on the other end of something that started in an army supply room.
2
Everything we are happened inside about five blocks
The local anchor
▾
Anchors the museum's whole history in two real, walkable San Angelo landmarks, Fort Concho and the Concho River, both places anyone in town could point to.
Spot B
If you want to see this museum's whole life, you can walk it in about ten minutes. Start at Fort Concho. That's where we opened in 1981, inside a building the Army used to store supplies. Fort Concho itself is a National Historic Landmark, one of the best preserved frontier Army posts left standing in the country. Walk a few blocks down to the river, and you're standing where we've been since 1999.
Spot C
The building on Love Street sits right on the Concho, and the design got international attention when it opened. Not because somebody wanted a landmark. Because the people who built it wanted the river itself to be part of what you see when you're inside. Somebody named that street Love a long time before we ever moved onto it, and people still smile the first time they hear it.
Spot A
Eighteen counties send people through these doors every year, close to two hundred thousand people across West Texas. About half the schoolkids in this district come through on a field trip at some point. All of that, still inside the same five or six blocks where it started. That's real reach for a museum you could still walk to from almost anywhere downtown, on your lunch break if you worked nearby.
3
Why we've never charged you to walk in
The thing the big competitor will not say
▾
Contrasts the museum's permanent free admission against big city museums that gate everything behind a ticket price, without naming a specific one. Likely to get forwarded by anyone who has paid thirty dollars to stand in a museum line somewhere else.
Spot C
Walk into most museums in a big city and you're paying twenty or thirty dollars before you see a single piece. Then you're handed a map for a building so big you'll never meet anyone who works there. That's a real experience. It just isn't the one we offer.
Spot A
Admission here has always been free. Not a discount day. Not a members only hour. Free, every time the doors are open. You can see what's on view in about forty minutes if you're moving with a purpose, or take all afternoon. Either way, somebody on staff will walk over and talk to you about what you're looking at. People write that exact detail in their own reviews before we ever ask them to say it. Right now that means you can walk in and see Discovering Global Treasures in Concho Valley Collections without paying a cent to look at it.
Spot B
We've won a National Award for Museum Service, the highest honor the field gives out, and the Texas Historical Commission's Award of Excellence. That museum service award came from the Institute of Museum and Library Services in Washington, the same federal office that reviews museums nationwide. Those came from the same size museum that never charged you a dime to get in. Big doesn't automatically mean better. Sometimes it just means a longer line.
4
What happens here on a Thursday afternoon
The useful one
▾
Corrects the assumption that a fine arts museum has nothing for a family or a kid to do, using the museum's own free weekly and monthly programming, at zero cost to give away.
Spot B
If the only thing you picture when you hear art museum is quiet rooms and don't touch signs, here's what you're missing. Every Thursday afternoon we run Art Thursdays, free, no reservation needed. Once a month it's a full Family Day, with a different hands on theme every time. Bring the kids on a Thursday, or bring them to a Family Day on a Saturday. Either one works, and neither one needs you to plan ahead.
Spot A
This September's theme was Sound into Color, kids turning music into shapes and color with their hands. The next one, October in the Open Air, moves the whole thing outside. [OWNER: a specific detail about what a kid does during a typical Family Day, from your own experience running one] None of it costs a family anything. Same as walking in on a random Tuesday.
Spot C
We're in the middle of the twenty sixth year of our ceramic competition too, pieces from all over North America, on view through most of the summer. Ceramics Monthly has called it the best clay show in the country. There's also the Concho Clay Studio on site, where people take real hands on ceramics classes throughout the year. You don't need an appointment for any of it. Just come on a Thursday and see what's out.
One more idea
Building the clay show

Follow the crew from empty gallery to opening day of the ceramic competition. Show the crates coming in, the pieces going up, and who decides where each one sits. It gives people a reason to come see a show that already has a national name.
