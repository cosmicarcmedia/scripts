The scripts

A first pass. I made some assumptions about your business to write these. On a shoot we'd dial them in to your goals.

1
There are two plates on our menu named after my kids
The origin and the face
▾
John has a family story and a menu that proves it: two plates named for his kids, a father and uncles who opened The Original Fuentes on Avenue K, and a head cook who started as the dishwasher. San Angelo knows the name already. This puts the face and the family in front of a stranger in under a minute.
Spot A
There are two plates on our menu named after my kids. One is the Olivia. The other one is the John Ross. Those were their favorites, so they ended up on the menu with their names on them. My name is John Fuentes, and this is Fuentes Cafe Downtown, at 101 South Chadbourne. It's a family place, and it has been for a long time. For more than fifty years, my family has been serving the people of San Angelo.
Spot B
It started with my father and my uncles. They opened The Original Fuentes over on Avenue K. Then, with my father's help, my wife Lisa and I opened our own place downtown. That was November the first, 1984. The day after we opened, a man named Martin started washing dishes in our kitchen. He worked his way up to head cook. [OWNER: what Martin means to this kitchen, and how many years he has been with you]
Spot C
Over the years, we've hired a lot of relatives to help run this place. My daughter Olivia has worked here. So have my sister-in-law and my nephew. There's even a Grandkid Special on the menu. We still have the neon sign from The Original Fuentes on display. [OWNER: the story behind that sign] Someday I hope a family member takes over and keeps the tradition going. So come on down to 101 South Chadbourne, and order an Olivia. I'd like you to eat what my kids ate.
2
On the corner of Chadbourne and Beauregard since 1984
The local anchor
▾
Downtown workers walk past this corner every day, and many have never heard the 2018 comeback or seen the weekday lunch plates. This puts the corner, the rebuild and the lunch specials in front of the people who can walk in at noon, and it works in his 2025 service award without bragging.
Spot B
I've been on the corner of Chadbourne and Beauregard since 1984. If you work downtown, you've probably walked right past our front door. Inside, it's brick walls, a lot of color, and murals. We decorate for every holiday, so the place changes with the calendar. It's a semi-historical building, and we've been in it a long time.
Spot A
In September of 2018, a wall from the building next door came down on us. We were in the kitchen, cooking for a meeting with the City of San Angelo. We heard a loud boom. We had forty two people working here at the time. We rebuilt, with a bigger bar and a patio style front. We opened the doors again on December 2, 2019. [OWNER: what that first day back was like]
Spot C
On weekdays, from eleven to three, we run lunch specials. Monday, it's a chile relleno or a guiso plate. Thursday, beef enchiladas, green chicken enchiladas, or chicken fried steak. Friday, fish tacos, or an Olivia. Each one is twelve dollars, and you can walk over from your office. In 2025, the San Angelo Hispanic Heritage Museum gave me its service award. It was for volunteering and for helping preserve Hispanic culture. I'm proud of that, and I'm still right here on the same corner. Come see us at 101 South Chadbourne.
3
A chain can't name a plate after your kid
The thing the big competitor will not say
▾
John told San Angelo LIVE his one great challenge is competing with the big chains. This script turns that into the sharpest comparison his own facts allow: kid-named plates and a head cook with a name and a start date. No competitor is named.
Spot C
Here's something a big restaurant chain can't do. It can't put a plate on the menu named after the owner's kid. We did it twice. There's an Olivia and a John Ross, and both are named for my children. There was no committee. It was just us.
Spot A
The day after we opened in 1984, a man named Martin started washing dishes in our kitchen. After three months, he was a chef helper. Then he worked up to head cook. [OWNER: how long Martin has been with you now, and what he cooks best] I can tell you his name, his start date, and how he got where he is. Ask a chain about the people making your dinner and see what you get.
Spot B
I keep it simple. A good product, with good flavor, served hot and on time. Our customers should be happy with the food and with the service. We have to offer you what you won't get anywhere else. So we do it with family. We do it with a head cook who started at the sink. And we do it with a menu that has our kids' names on it. People know us for the chips and hot sauce, the traditional plates, and the margaritas. If that sounds like your kind of lunch, we're at 101 South Chadbourne. And ask about the Grandkid Special.
4
Planning a party or an office lunch? Start here
The useful one
▾
People planning a birthday or an office lunch look for exactly these answers: room size, what the form asks for, how much notice catering needs. Giving them away builds trust and points the easiest bookings straight at the phone.
Spot A
If you're planning a birthday or an office lunch downtown, here's what to know before you call us. Our private room holds fifteen to thirty people. The rental is a hundred dollars, with a fifty dollar deposit. If you need more space than that, we can talk about the whole restaurant. We can hold up to a hundred and fifty.
Spot B
When you ask for a date, have your details ready. The form on our website wants your name, your phone number and your email. Then the date, the time, how many people, and what you're celebrating. Birthday party, office function, or any other special event, it all works. We'll do our best to fit your date, and we'll get back to you soon.
Spot C
If you'd rather have us bring the food to you, that's catering. We need twenty four hours' notice. We cater in San Angelo, Wall, Mereta, and Water Valley. Plates, napkins, and silverware don't come with the order, so ask us if you need them. And if anybody in your group is gluten free, vegan, or has an allergy, call us first. We handle those whenever we can. The number is 325 658 2430. We'd love to feed your people.
