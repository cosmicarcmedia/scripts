1
I learned to bake in my grandmother's kitchen
The origin and the face
▾
People hire the person behind a wedding cake, and Susan has a real story: a grandmother's kitchen, a Southern Living cookbook, a son who grew up in the bakery. This puts her face and her forty years in front of a stranger in under a minute.
Spot A
I learned to bake in my grandmother's kitchen. That's where all of this started. My name is Susan Ball, and I own City Cafe and Bakery here in San Angelo. In high school I took a job at a small bakery. I sharpened my cake decorating at Angelo State. Then I baked desserts at Bentwood Country Club.
Spot B
Back when I was working downtown, some editors from Southern Living came through. They tasted what we were making, and they liked it. One of my recipes ended up in a Southern Living cookbook. A recipe from a San Angelo kitchen, in a national cookbook. I still think about that. [OWNER: the name of the recipe that made it into the cookbook]
Spot C
I raised my son Bradley in this bakery. He started coming to work with me when he was three years old. Now he works here full time. We have been feeding San Angelo for over forty years. Our team has handled thousands of events. Business lunches, banquets, holiday parties, wedding receptions. Every one of them started with a phone call. Somebody tells me what they're picturing, and we build it from there. If you've got something coming up, call me at 224 4471. Or come by 108 North Pierce and say hello. I'd love to hear what you're planning.
2
The fire that sent me to Pierce Street
The local anchor
▾
A downtown fire that took six businesses is a story a lot of San Angelo remembers, and it explains why this bakery sits on Pierce Street. It tells locals this business came back from the worst day and kept feeding the town.
Spot B
There was a fire in the Three Sisters Building downtown. It destroyed six businesses, and mine was one of them. [OWNER: what you remember about the day of the fire, and the year it happened] My bakery had grown up in that building. Then one day it was gone. I was starting over, with a lot of people still counting on me to feed them.
Spot C
So I opened a small shop on Pierce Street. That's when I started doing catering full time. [OWNER: one of the first catering jobs you took after the fire] That's how a bakery became a caterer. Eventually we moved to 108 North Pierce, where we are now.
Spot A
In 2007 we expanded and added a showroom. Linens, china, and floral rentals, all in one place. So if you're planning an event, you can walk in and pick things out with your own hands. A bride can choose her linens, her china, and her flowers in one visit. From setup to last call, we have you covered. Our own staff handles the service. We're still downtown, still on Pierce Street, and still baking from scratch. We're open Tuesday through Friday, and Saturday until two. We're closed Sunday and Monday. If you've been in San Angelo a while, you probably remember that fire. Come see what came after it. Bring your appetite, and bring your calendar. You'll leave with something good.
3
One phone call for the whole wedding
The thing the big competitor will not say
▾
Most couples juggle a cake person, a caterer, a florist, a rental company and a bartender. City Cafe does it all in one building, and a past bride said Susan changed things days before the wedding. That is the line a national bakery cannot say, and it is the one that gets forwarded to a friend who is engaged.
Spot C
Here's what planning a wedding usually looks like. You call a cake person. Then a caterer. Then a florist, a rental company, somebody for the bar. Five phone calls, and none of those people have ever talked to each other.
Spot A
We do it all in-house. Flowers, decorating, catering, and cake. Our own wait staff. Our own bartenders. Corporate events, weddings, private parties, same kitchen. Linens, china, and rentals from our own showroom. You can ask about a package deal to save money. Ask about it when you call.
Spot B
And here's what that looks like when something changes. One bride wrote that we were happy to make changes even days before her wedding. That's what happens when the person who made your cake is the same person standing in your event. Another bride told people we made her Italian cream wedding cake, and she called it the best cake she'd ever eaten. Another couple chose us because we make the cake from scratch. Our event team has handled thousands of events, so very little surprises us. So before you start the five phone calls, try one. Call us, tell us the date, and see what it feels like to talk to one place. [OWNER: how far ahead couples should call]
4
What to ask before you order a wedding cake
The useful one
▾
A lot of couples have never ordered a cake and do not know what to ask. Giving away the list builds trust, shows Susan's experience, and answers the exact question brides search for. One reviewer said the covering surprised her, so the list starts there.
Spot A
If you're about to order a wedding cake, ask three questions before you put down a deposit. First, what is it covered in? Buttercream and fondant look and taste very different. Ask to see a cake that is covered the way you're picturing yours.
Spot B
Second, ask how it's made. Is it baked from scratch, or from a mix? You can taste the difference, and a good baker will tell you straight. Ask how many people it serves, and compare that to your guest list. Ask how far ahead they need your final guest count. Third, ask about changes. The guest count changes, and sometimes the design does too. Ask how late you can change it, and who you're going to be talking to.
Spot C
Ask to see photos of finished cakes from real weddings. Bring a picture if you have one. Bring your colors, and your guest count if you know it. Bring your venue too, because the room decides what size cake looks right. [OWNER: how many months ahead you want couples to come in for a cake consultation] We bake Italian cream, red velvet, carrot, German chocolate, strawberry, and more. Come by 108 North Pierce and ask me anything. Bring a friend who will tell you the truth about the cake. That's how I'd want to be treated if it were my wedding.
