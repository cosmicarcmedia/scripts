What a day on the farm costs
The thing the big competitor will not say
▾

4
What to put on your kid for a first riding lesson
The useful one
▾
Parents with a kid who wants to ride are already googling this. The farm teaches weekly lessons, and a clear answer on jeans, boots and helmets saves a first lesson from going wrong and puts Kim in front of the next rider.
Spot C
Don't put leggings on your kid for a first riding lesson. They slide in the saddle. Put them in jeans, because denim grips. Flat seams are better, since seams rub. A longer inseam helps too, since the extra length stacks at the ankle and the jeans stay down.
Spot B
Next, boots with a heel. Cowboy boots are the usual pick for western riding. Look for a roper heel, set straight down under the boot, so a foot can't slide through the stirrup in a fall. Leather soles slip in and out of the stirrup easily, and synthetic soles grip a little more and last longer. Tall socks keep the boot seams off their ankles. No sneakers and no sandals around horses.
Spot A
A fitted shirt stays put and keeps their shoulders covered. Skip the hoodie, because a hoodie can catch on the saddle horn. Riders sign a waiver and wear a helmet at our farm. [OWNER: whether you loan a helmet for the first lesson] A bicycle helmet is no substitute for a riding helmet. Lessons are weekly, for riders six and up, western or English, beginner to advanced. We teach barrel racing and gymkhana too. Day camp dates are posted on our Facebook page. If you're not sure what to buy, ask us before you spend a dime.
