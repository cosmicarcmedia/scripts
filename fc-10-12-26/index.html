3
Ask any cabinet seller for the spec sheet
The thing the big competitor will not say
▾
The Products page lists every part of the cabinet in plain numbers. Reading it aloud and inviting viewers to ask any seller for theirs draws a fair line against a big box catalog without naming one.
Spot C
Before you buy cabinets from anyone, ask for the spec sheet. Here's ours. The doors are three quarter inch solid wood, full overlay, so they sit over the face of the cabinet. The hinges are six way adjustable, European style, with soft close. That means a door can be lined up after it's hung, and it closes without a slam. The shelves are three quarter inch cabinet grade plywood, with edge banding on the front.
Spot A
The drawers are dovetail, with solid wood on all sides. They're full extension, so they pull out all the way, and they run on concealed under mount glides that soft close. The box is half inch cabinet grade plywood, with a UV coated natural plywood interior. The base cabinets get bracket reinforcement.
Spot B
The face frame is hardwood, and the joints are double dowelled. Every door has bumpers. Now ask any cabinet seller for their list, line by line. Ask what their drawers are made of, and how the corners are joined. [OWNER: what you check first when you pick up a cabinet] Then ask for the price. One customer wrote that they got the best prices. Everything I just read is on our website, on the Products page. We offer a free bid and a 3D design, so you can put the numbers side by side before you decide.
4
Five things a cabinet bid should spell out
The useful one
▾
It teaches a buyer what to ask for in any bid, costs nothing to give away, and walks through the shop's own free bid and 3D design offer, so a viewer who follows the advice ends up on his calendar.
Spot A
A cabinet bid should spell out what you're getting. If it doesn't, ask for it. Here's my list of five things. One. The door style and color. We offer Shaker White, Shaker Gray, Shaker Navy, Shaker Espresso, Antique White, and two slim styles. Make sure the bid names the exact one, so there are no surprises on delivery day. Two. The construction. Ask for the spec sheet. Solid wood doors, dovetail drawers, plywood boxes. If the bid just says cabinets, ask for more detail.
Spot B
Three. The countertop. Quartz or granite, and which one. We offer both in a variety of selections, with a quick turnaround time. Ask how long it takes, and get the answer on paper. Four. The design. We do a 3D design of your kitchen or bathroom, so you can see it before the first cabinet goes in. [OWNER: how you use the 3D design with a customer]
Spot C
Five. The installation. Ask who installs, and when. [OWNER: whether installation is included in your bid] One customer wrote about how it went from the initial consultation to the final installation. That's the order it should go, with no gaps in between. And one more thing. A free bid costs you nothing, so get one. Then get a second one, and put both on the table. We're happy to be one of them.
