3
Three questions to ask any welder before the arc is struck
The thing the big competitor will not say
▾
A larger supplier or fabrication shop can claim certification and insurance in a brochure. The BBB record, accredited since 1997 with an A plus rating and no complaints in three years, is public, so this script hands the customer a way to check it without naming anyone.
Spot C
Before anybody strikes an arc on your job, ask them three questions. Are your welders certified, and certified in what. Are you insured. And can I look up your record. Most people never ask any of the three until something goes wrong. Ask for specifics. A certification has a name, insurance has a paper, and a record has a date on it.
Spot A
Our welders are certified. [OWNER: the certification your welders hold and who tests them] We're fully insured, shop work or field work. That matters, because we're working with hot metal and heavy loads on your property. And the Better Business Bureau record is public. Accredited since February of 1997. An A plus rating, and no complaints filed in the last three years. Go look it up. It takes two minutes.
Spot B
On structural jobs we work with contractors and engineers. The weld has to meet the regulatory standard on paper. Some welds end up under paint, inside a frame, or holding up a load. Nobody ever looks at them again, which is exactly why they have to be right. That's one reason the owner leads the crew here. When somebody has a question about a job a year later, there's one name to call, and it's mine. Travis Ahlers, J and T's Welding.
4
Grab a magnet before you call a welder
The useful one
▾
A magnet test is something every customer can do in ten seconds, it tells a welder a lot before the first phone call ends, and it shows the shop handles steel, stainless and aluminum without selling anything.
Spot A
Before you call a welder about something that broke, go find a refrigerator magnet. Or the one off your toolbox. I'm serious. Hold it to the metal. If it sticks, you're almost certainly looking at steel. If it doesn't, you've got aluminum or stainless, and that tells me a lot before I ever see the part.
Spot B
Stainless can go either way, so don't be surprised if it only half sticks. That's fine. Just tell me what you saw. Aluminum is its own animal. It's the metal in a lot of trailers and boats, and it has to be welded gently to keep its strength. Steel, carbon or stainless, we repair and build frames, equipment parts, and custom pieces. If you want something built from nothing, we start with a consultation.
Spot C
So when you call, tell us four things. What it's made of. What it holds up or hauls. Where it is. And how much it hurts to leave it broken. Describe the break as plainly as you can. Don't worry about the words. Keep every piece that came off, even the small ones. If it's too big or too heavy to move, say so. That's what the mobile unit is for. 325 651 3200, Monday through Friday, eight to five.
