1
What I did before I ever touched a home air conditioner
Origin
▾
Nobody driving past a heating and air van in Ballinger has any reason to guess the man behind it spent eight years fixing Apache helicopters, then years working child protection cases, before he ever opened this shop. That is the strongest asset this lead has.
Spot A
Long before I ever touched a home air conditioner, I was an Army mechanic working on AH 64 Apache helicopters. Sergeant E5, more than eight years in the service. That's where I first learned the basics of air conditioning, keeping cooling systems running on a helicopter, not a house, in the same West Texas heat that doesn't care whether you're flying or standing still.
Spot B
When I got out, I didn't go straight into HVAC. I got a criminal justice degree from Angelo State. Worked as a case manager at a juvenile facility for the Texas Youth Commission. After that, I worked cases for Child Protective Services. Those jobs and this one have more in common than you'd guess. In both, you walk into somebody's worst day and you're the one who's supposed to make it better. That's a strange resume for a guy who fixes your air conditioner now.
Spot C
In May of 2010, I came back to the trade the Army had given me first, and opened this shop. Veteran owned, family run, right here in Ballinger. These days it's not just me either. We're a Carrier Factory Authorized Dealer now, still family run. All these years later, we still fix things the same way I learned to keep an Apache flying. Careful, and right the first time. That's not a slogan. That's just the only way I know how to do this job.
2
Ballinger is a phone call, not a hold line
Local anchor
▾
Ballinger is a town of about four thousand people. A shop that answers the phone at any hour and still shows up is a different promise there than it is in a city, and naming that directly tells the town this video is for them.
Spot B
Ballinger is a small town. About four thousand people. When your air conditioner goes out here in July, you're not calling some call center in another state, hoping somebody local eventually calls you back. You're calling a shop ten minutes from your house, and somebody who might already know which street you live on.
Spot A
We answer that phone every hour of every day, because in a town this size, an air conditioner going out at midnight in August isn't a small thing. It's dangerous, especially for older folks. So we run twenty four seven, out of one address on South Eleventh Street, the same one since 2010. Some weeks that call is a house. Some weeks it's the ice machine at a restaurant down the street, or the walk in cooler at a local store that can't afford to sit broken for a day.
Spot C
We don't just cover Ballinger either. San Angelo, Coleman, Grape Creek, all the towns wrapped around us, plus free estimates inside twenty five miles of here. That's not a marketing line. That's just how far a truck can go and still get to you fast. Sixteen years in, we're still the closest real answer to that phone call, not a dispatcher somewhere else deciding who gets sent out.
3
Every job here gets inspected. Ask the next guy if his does.
Competitor contrast
▾
The permits, the inspections, the insurance, the background checks, and the stated refusal to oversell a replacement job are real, stated practices most homeowners never think to ask about. Laid out plainly, they draw a sharp, fair line without naming anyone.
Spot C
Here's something most people never think to ask a heating and air company. Do you pull a permit for this job. Does anybody inspect it when you're done. We do, every single time. We're a state licensed mechanical contractor, license number right there on the truck. That license is what says the state itself checked our work, not just our sign.
Spot A
We also carry two million dollars in insurance. Every employee is covered by workman's comp, passed a background check, and gets randomly drug tested. None of that is required just to hang a sign on a building. We do it anyway, because it's your house, not just a job site, and the person walking through it should be somebody you'd trust around your own family.
Spot B
And here's the one that costs us money to say out loud. We'd rather fix the part that's actually broken than sell you a whole new system. Our own policy says it plainly. Fair pricing, done right, without overselling. If your unit really does need replacing, we size the new one with real load calculation software, not a guess based on whatever was on the truck that day. That's the difference between a company and a guy with a van, and it's the same standard whether the job is fifty dollars or five thousand.
4
Why a bigger air conditioner isn't always better
Useful customer education
▾
Explains a real, common HVAC misunderstanding using the shop's own stated practice of calculating the right size instead of guessing. Costs nothing to give away and helps anyone shopping for a new system, here or anywhere else.
Spot A
If you're shopping for a new air conditioner, bigger sounds like better. Most people think a bigger unit cools a house faster. That's backwards, and here's why it matters for your own house.
Spot B
An oversized unit cools the air fast, then shuts back off before it ever pulls the humidity out of your house. So the number on the thermostat looks fine, and the air still feels wet and clammy. It also cycles on and off more, which wears the system out faster, not slower. That's why we run an actual load calculation on your house first, square footage, insulation, windows, before we ever recommend a size. It's the same math the industry calls a Manual J. Most of the time it tells you to buy exactly the size you already have, just a better one.
Spot C
So before you let anybody sell you a bigger unit, ask them one question. Did you run a load calculation on this house, or are you just matching the size of the old one. If they can't answer that, they're guessing with your money. If they can, you know you're dealing with somebody doing this the right way. Either way, now you know the right question to ask, here or anywhere else in town.
