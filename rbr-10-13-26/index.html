1
A bike shop of my own, March of 2007
The origin and the face
▾
Most people who ride past 1717 South Oakes have never heard that the shop began as one man's dream of running his own bicycle shop, with a grand opening on March 19, 2007. His own old About page tells it, and the video puts it in his voice.
Spot A
In March of 2007, I opened a bicycle shop on South Oakes Street. That was the dream. Open my own bike shop, and run it myself. The grand opening was March nineteenth. We called it athletes serving the community, and I have been working to live up to that line ever since. Nineteen years later, I am still at 1717 South Oakes.
Spot B
[OWNER: what you did for work before the shop opened] [OWNER: why you picked bikes, and why running went in the name] You can see the answer on the shelves. Bikes, running shoes, helmets, sunglasses, socks, and the parts and tubes that keep a bicycle rolling. Come in and try the running shoes on. Take your time with it. Our regulars even have a photo on our page called the Randy's Bike Shop Gang.
Spot C
A customer wrote on our Facebook page that we were friendly, quick on the turnaround, and fair on price, and that they would bring us all their cycling needs. Ninety six percent of the people who reviewed us on Facebook recommend the shop. I would put that on the wall if I had the wall space. Someone else wrote that the owner takes his time with people. [OWNER: the first question you ask a new customer] Then we start there.
2
Closed on Sundays, and here is where to go
The local anchor
▾
The Sunday line on his hours, go ride your bike or run, already carries his sense of humor. The dam ride, the Tuesday and Thursday loop and the Road Lizards are real San Angelo groups most newcomers never find, so this one gives the town something useful and ties it back to his door.
Spot B
My hours say closed on Sundays. Right after that, they say go ride your bike or run. So here is where. Sunday mornings at eight, riders meet at the south end of the O.C. Fisher dam, off Mercedes Avenue. You ride across and back, about thirteen miles. No traffic, which on a bicycle is the best part.
Spot A
Tuesday and Thursday evenings, there is a loop ride. It leaves from Knickerbocker, crosses the bridge toward Wall, comes down Paint Rock Road, and brings you back into town on Schwartz Road. Both rides are no drop. That means an experienced rider stays back with whoever is slowest. If you have never ridden in a group, show up and see. Some of the most regular riders are in their seventies. The local club is the San Angelo Biking Association, and one of its members has said he would like to see more younger riders.
Spot C
Runners have a group here too. The San Angelo Road Lizards are our running club, and I share what they post on the shop page. Whatever speed you go, somebody will go with you. If you need a bike for it, or shoes, or a flat fixed before Sunday, I am at 1717 South Oakes. [OWNER: a favorite Sunday ride or race memory from a customer] Then come tell me how it went.
3
The only name on my sign is mine
The thing the big competitor will not say
▾
A store with one brand on the sign has one brand to recommend. His old site listed several brands and a rider fitting, and reviewers say the owner takes his time, which a branded chain store cannot promise about its counter.
Spot C
Walk into a store with one brand on the sign, and every answer you get is that brand. The only name on my sign is mine. So when you ask what bike you should buy, I pick from more than one catalog. I carry Jamis. [OWNER: the other brands on your floor right now] I can sit you on a few and tell you what I see.
Spot A
I fit road bikes and mountain bikes to the rider. [OWNER: what a fit costs, or whether it is free with a bike purchase] A bike that fits you is a bike you will ride. That is what the fitting is for. Then there is the repair side. One customer said the turnaround was quick. Another brought in a kid's bike with brake trouble and got it back fast. Someone called the prices unbeatable for service and repairs. A rider on a bicycle trip wrote that I kept it going.
Spot B
And I take my time. A reviewer on Yelp said the owner takes his time with people. Another called this shop very military friendly. I will take both of those. Loitering is free here, and so is the television. If you want to talk bikes for half an hour, pull up a chair. There is a running side to the shop as well, shoes and socks from the same counter. I would rather you leave knowing what you bought.
4
The two minute check before you ride
The useful one
▾
Air, brakes and chain are what any rider can check before every ride, and teaching it costs the shop nothing. A customer review mentions a kid's bike with brake trouble coming in, so it also gives parents a reason to walk through his door.
Spot A
Before you ride, give your bike two minutes. Bike shops call it the ABC check. A is for air. Squeeze the tire. If it gives, it needs air. Road tires can feel firm and still be low, so use a gauge, at least once a week. B is for brakes. Squeeze the lever hard. It should stop well short of the handlebar. Look at the pads. If there is less than a quarter inch left, replace them.
Spot B
C is for cranks and chain. Grab a crank arm and wiggle it. There should be no play. The chain should look like clean metal. Rust or black gunk means trouble. Then make sure the quick release levers on your wheels are closed. Last, take a short slow ride, and listen. Rubbing, grinding or clicking means something is off. If you find something, adjusting brakes and gears, and truing a wheel, are everyday jobs for us.
Spot C
Run the same check on your kid's bike too, tires first. One customer brought a child's bike in with brake trouble, and we turned it around fast. If your bike has sat for several months, bring it to a shop before the first ride. [OWNER: how fast you turn around a brake fix] You know where we are. 1717 South Oakes.
One more idea
Drop-off to pickup

Film one repair from the moment the bike comes in to the moment it rolls out. Open on the problem the rider described, show what you found, and finish on the first pedal stroke. Say how long it took only if the clock agrees.
