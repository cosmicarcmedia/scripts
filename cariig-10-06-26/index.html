2
My office is on a road with a name from a book
Local anchor
▾
A local road name with a storybook origin is the kind of small, surprising fact that gets saved. Every date and name comes from the state historical handbook, and it ties straight to his address, so it puts the firm on the map for a San Angelo viewer.
Spot B
My office is on Knickerbocker Road, which heads southwest out of San Angelo. Eighteen miles west of town, Farm roads 584 and 2335 meet at a community called Knickerbocker. Its name comes from a book. It was named for Diedrich Knickerbocker, the narrator of Washington Irving's History of New York. Two early settlers who were related to Irving picked the name. So a character from a book gave a West Texas community its name.
Spot A
In 1875, three brothers dug a ditch there to grow hay, vegetables and melons to sell at Fort Concho. They called it the Baze ditch. In 1877, four men started the Knickerbocker Ranch and brought Mexican sheep into the valley. The post office opened in 1881. Cotton farming started there in 1887. In 1889, the whole town moved around a hill to find better water. By the late 1890s, about two hundred fifty people lived there.
Spot C
Then automobiles and all weather roads came along, and the town shrank. By the year 2000, fifty people lived there. My firm has been in San Angelo since 1988. The community sits in the southwest corner of Tom Green County, six miles from the Irion County line. We cover homes, cars, businesses, farms and ranches. If you drive out Knickerbocker Road, stop in at Suite 110.

4
Replacement cost or actual cash value
Useful customer education
▾
Most homeowners learn the difference between these two when they file a claim. This gives them the plain definitions from the Texas Department of Insurance before that day, and it costs the firm nothing to give away.
Spot A
There are two ways your home insurance can be written, and the difference is one word. That word is depreciation. Replacement cost coverage pays to repair or replace your house and your belongings at current prices. Actual cash value coverage pays replacement cost minus depreciation. Depreciation is what something has lost in value from age and wear.
Spot B
Both apply to your belongings as well as the house. So which one your policy has changes how a claim is calculated. That's something to know before you ever need it. The Texas Department of Insurance says to read your policy or talk to your agent to be sure of your exact coverages. Ask what your deductible is while you're at it. And if you own expensive jewelry or art, ask about adding more coverage.
Spot C
Try a small project this weekend. Make a list of everything you own, and use the Home Inventory Checklist from the Texas Department of Insurance. A complete list helps you decide how much coverage you need, and it makes filing a claim easier. Keep a copy of the list where you can find it. Every policy is different, so your policy is the final word. If you want a second set of eyes on yours, call us or stop by 5301 Knickerbocker Road.
