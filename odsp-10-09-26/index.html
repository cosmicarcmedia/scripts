The scripts

1
Raised in Coleman County, now behind the Owl counter
The origin and the face
▾
A new pharmacist in a hundred year old store is a reason to look twice. This puts a local face and her own story in front of the town before a chain does the talking.
Spot C
I grew up in Coleman County. I went off to the University of Texas and earned my pharmacy degree. Then I spent years in pharmacy management, at Rising Star Pharmacy and at a big store pharmacy. Now I'm the pharmacist in charge at Owl Drug, on Commercial Avenue. [OWNER: what made you choose Owl Drug.] It's a good building to come to work in every morning.
Spot A
Owl Drug has been downtown in Coleman since 1923. That's a hundred years of prescriptions, milkshakes, and people running into somebody they know. The pharmacy counter is in one corner. The soda fountain and grill are right there too. And on most days the whole place smells like the grill.
Spot B
Coleman is my home county, and I intend to do right by it. If you need a prescription filled, I'll fill it. If you need it compounded, we do that. If you need a flu shot or a Shingrix, call us and we'll set up a time. If you can't get out of the house, we deliver, anywhere in Coleman, free. Come meet the new pharmacist. Stay for a burger. You've earned one. I'm easy to find. I'm at the pharmacy counter, and I'd like to learn your name.
2
Count the owls
The local anchor
▾
Everybody in Coleman has a burger memory, and this script spends a minute in the building they all share. It tells the town this store is theirs and sells the pharmacy by way of the fountain.
Spot B
There's a story around Coleman that if you count every owl in this store, your order is free. [OWNER: is that story true, and how many owls are there?] I'll let you find out. What I can tell you is that Owl Drug has been on Commercial Avenue since 1923. That's a long time to keep a burger counter and a pharmacy under one roof.
Spot C
The Owl Burger has been on the menu since the doors opened. We make the shakes and malts by hand. Folks come in for a Black Cow, which is chocolate syrup, milk, and shaved ice in a malt glass. Some people say Elvis stopped in back in 1955, and we named a burger for him. The Owlvis Burger. Try it and decide for yourself.
Spot A
The walls are covered with things customers gave us over the years. People wanted to leave a little of themselves behind in this place. And there's a gift shop, espresso drinks, and Blue Bell ice cream. Then, tucked in with all that, there's a pharmacy. So you can pick up your prescription, a birthday gift, and lunch in one stop. That's the whole idea of a downtown drugstore. Come by 312 South Commercial. Sit at the counter, and tell them the pharmacist sent you. The burger comes first, the prescription second, whichever order you like.
3
Three questions for whoever fills your prescriptions
The thing the big competitor will not say
▾
This is the sharpest one. It gives every customer a short list of questions that a big chain pharmacy has a hard time answering well, and it never names one.
Spot C
Here are three questions to ask whoever fills your prescriptions. Will you deliver it to my door, and what does that cost? If I have a question at nine at night, who do I text? And does the pharmacist know my name?
Spot A
Here's how we'd answer. We deliver anywhere in Coleman, and it's free. You can call us at 325 625 2178, or text 325 200 4340. A real person from the pharmacy answers. And we're a small shop on Commercial Avenue, so yes, you'll see the same faces. [OWNER: one thing you can do here that you couldn't do in a bigger pharmacy.]
Spot B
Then ask about compounding. That's when a pharmacist mixes a medicine to fit what you need. Plenty of pharmacies don't do it. We do. If your doctor has told you a standard pill won't work for you, ask us. Ask about your refills too, and whether we can set up a reminder so you never run out of pills. We have an app that does that. You can order refills and message the pharmacist from your phone. The pharmacy has been downtown since 1923, locally owned, locally loved. Ask those three questions wherever you go. We'd like you to ask them here first.
4
Bring the whole bag of pills
The useful one
▾
A simple ask that costs the store nothing, shows what a pharmacist does beyond counting pills, and points straight at a service the site already lists.
Spot A
Next time you come in, bring a bag. Put every pill bottle in it. The prescriptions, the vitamins, the supplements, the stuff you take for your knee. Everything. If you take it, it goes in the bag.
Spot B
Here's why. Most people see more than one doctor. Each of them writes a prescription, and each of them only sees their own part of the picture. The pharmacy is the one place all of it comes together. So when you set that bag on the counter, a pharmacist can look at all of it at once. Is anything doubled up? Does anything fight with something else? Is there something you could drop? The name for that is medication therapy management, and we offer it.
Spot C
It's free to ask. Call us at 325 625 2178, or walk in. We're at 312 South Commercial in Coleman. [OWNER: what the visit costs, and how long it takes.] And if you want your flu shot while you're here, we do those too. Shingrix, pneumonia, RSV, and Tdap as well. Call and make an appointment. Bring the bag. Let's go through it together and make sure everything you take is helping you. That's what a hometown pharmacist is for.
