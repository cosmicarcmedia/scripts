3
The questions to ask before anyone puts a septic system on your property
The thing the big competitor will not say
▾
This is the one built to get forwarded. It hands a homeowner a list of questions to take to any septic company, and the shop's own list of services answers each of them with one family. Nobody is named, and it draws a plain line without criticizing a single outfit.
Spot B
Before anybody puts a septic system on your property, ask who does what. Who looks at the site? Who supplies the tank? Who puts it in the ground? And who comes back to pump it? If the answer is four different companies, you've got four phone numbers when something goes wrong. And each one of them can tell you it was somebody else's part. Ask how they decide when your tank needs pumping. It depends on how many people live in the house and how big the tank is. Ask if they're licensed and insured. Ask what the Better Business Bureau says about them.
Spot C
Here's how we do it. We do the site evaluation. Our concrete products include septic tanks. [OWNER: confirm that the tanks you install are the ones you make] We install the system. We pump it when it's time. That's four steps, and one family does all four.
Spot A
After install day, the tank still needs pumping about every three to five years. So we're going to see each other again. When we do, we'll know what we put in the ground. We're licensed and insured. We've been Better Business Bureau accredited since 2015. If you're getting quotes, ask everybody the same questions. Then call us at 325 658 5079 and ask us. We'll answer every one.
4
Don't pour concrete on your septic drainfield
The useful one
▾
A concrete company warning people off pouring concrete is a hook nobody scrolls past, and every rule in it comes from the EPA's septic care guidance. It costs nothing to give away, and every rural homeowner with a septic tank needs it.
Spot C
Here's one from a concrete company. Don't pour concrete over your septic drainfield. Don't lay asphalt there either. Don't park on it. Don't drive equipment across it. Most drainfields aren't built to carry that kind of weight. It packs down the soil, and it can damage the pipes.
Spot A
Skip the trees and shrubs on top of it, because the roots can get into the pipes. Native grass is the best thing to put over it. Keep roof drains and sump pump water pointed away from it. Then there's what goes down the drain. No cooking grease or oil. No baby wipes or wet wipes. No paper towels, no cat litter, no coffee grounds.
Spot B
Skip the chemical drain openers. Boiling water or a drain snake does the job. And have the tank pumped about every three to five years. How often depends on how many people live in the house and how big the tank is. A pro will pump it sooner if the sludge gets within twelve inches of the outlet, or the scum within six. Have a septic professional inspect it at least every three years. If your system has a pump or a float switch, get it checked every year. Keep a record of every visit. If you can't remember when yours was last pumped, call us at 325 658 5079.
