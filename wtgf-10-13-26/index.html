The scripts

A first pass. I made some assumptions about your business to write these. On a shoot we'd dial them in to your goals.

1
A family shop in San Angelo since 1958
The origin and the face
▾
The site says the shop is family owned and operated and has been since 1958, but never says whose name is on it or how it started. Nobody pulling up to the building on North Chadbourne knows that, and a face and a founding story are what a hunter remembers.
Spot A
West Texas Game Feeders opened in 1958. Sixty eight years later, we're still a locally owned family business. [OWNER: who started it in 1958, and what the shop sold at the beginning] I'm [OWNER: your name], and [OWNER: how you came into the business]. When people ask what we do, the short answer is blinds, feeders, towers, and the parts and electronics to keep all of it running.
Spot B
The long answer is that we build some of it ourselves. The Original Ground Hugger was designed and manufactured right here in San Angelo. It's a ground feeder that holds four hundred pounds of corn. It sits on the ground, so nobody has to climb up to fill it. It comes with a timer, a twelve volt battery, and a solar panel. We also fabricate a road feeder in our own shop that fits a standard two inch receiver and holds three hundred twenty five pounds.
Spot C
Our shop does welding, punching, shearing, and fabricating, so custom orders are possible. Sometimes the wait runs long, and we'll tell you that up front. We also stock Ranch King blinds, and feeders from All Seasons, Crossfire, and more. Whatever you buy, you leave with something that works and a local number to call. We're open Monday, Tuesday, Thursday, and Friday, and Saturday mornings. Find us at 3312 North Chadbourne Street.
2
Hunters and landowners both call this shop
The local anchor
▾
A shop like this sits in the middle of ranch country, and the site mentions leases, in-field feeder service and delivery within a hundred miles that nobody driving past would guess. Naming the service area tells the whole Concho Valley this video is for them.
Spot C
Here's something we do that you won't find on a price tag. Hunters call us about leases. Landowners ask us about matching hunting groups. If you have land and you want the right people on it, or you're looking for a place to hunt, call the shop. [OWNER: a lease or a hunting group you matched that worked out] We serve the Concho Valley, from 3312 North Chadbourne Street in San Angelo.
Spot A
Most of what we sell can be delivered within a hundred miles of the shop. Most blinds are the exception, and we'll tell you that up front. If you'd rather we handle the filling, we'll fill your feeders in the field, deer corn and protein, by the ton or by the fifty pound bag. While we're out there, we can repair and maintain the feeder too. The price depends on how many feeders you have, how hard they are to reach, and how far we're driving.
Spot B
If you can't make it to the store during the day, call ahead. We'll take the order and the payment over the phone and set up a pickup time that works for both of us. Our hours are nine to noon and one to four thirty, Monday, Tuesday, Thursday, and Friday. Saturday we're here nine to noon. We're closed Wednesday and Sunday. You can also message us on our Facebook page. [OWNER: the farthest town a customer has driven in from]
3
Ask whoever sold you the feeder what happens when it breaks
The thing the big competitor will not say
▾
This is the question a box store will not answer well. The shop's own pages say it repairs most feeders, stocks the parts, warranties its own feeder for life at the cost of parts, and fabricates what it does not stock. Laid out plainly, it draws a fair line without naming anyone.
Spot B
Before you buy a game feeder anywhere, ask what happens when it breaks. Here's our answer. We can service and repair most feeders. We price each repair one at a time, based on what's wrong with it. And we carry the parts: motors, slingers, funnels, cones, galvanized lids, leg brackets, bolt kits, and hanging brackets for seventeen, thirty, and fifty five gallon barrels.
Spot C
Our own Ground Hugger has a lifetime warranty on the feeder itself. The timer has five years. If something happens to the feeder, we repair it for the cost of the parts. Bullet holes, water damage, we don't judge. It's printed right on our Ground Hugger page. We can say that because we design and build it here in San Angelo.
Spot A
New, unused products can come back within thirty days with your receipt. And if you need something we don't stock, custom orders are possible. It can take a while, and we'll say so. There's rarely something we can't make happen, and our shop does the welding, punching, shearing, and fabricating to do it. We take Discover, MasterCard, Visa, cash, and checks. Credit cards carry a three percent fee, and we say so before you pay. Ask the next place if they'll tell you the same.
4
What's inside a game feeder and what you can check yourself
The useful one
▾
Gives a hunter what a box store never will: a plain walk through the parts that run a feeder, each one named from the shop's own parts list. It costs nothing to say and shows this shop knows what is inside the thing it sells.
Spot A
Before the season starts, check your feeder. A game feeder is a handful of parts working together. A barrel to hold the feed. A motor, with a slinger that throws it. A timer that tells the motor when to run. A battery, and a solar panel to keep the battery charged. Our Ground Hugger ships with the timer, a twelve volt battery, and a solar panel already installed. [OWNER: the part that quits most often, and how you spot it]
Spot B
Start with the battery. We sell battery testers, and chargers for six volt and twelve volt. Check the solar panel next. Ours ship with brackets and a one year warranty. Then the timer. Ours, The Timer, has six feed times and a five year warranty. If yours is hard to set, we carry a digital timer with four feed times and adjustable duration that we call the simplest on the market to set.
Spot C
If it's the motor or the slinger, those are replaceable too. Six volt or twelve volt, with a bracket or without. Same goes for lids, funnels, cones, and leg brackets. Bring the old part in and we'll see what you need. [OWNER: the feed time you set for deer this time of year] We sell deer corn, protein pellets, and deer blocks too, so you can get the feeder fixed and filled in one stop.
One more idea
The part that fixes it

Film one real repair at the counter, with the customer's okay. Someone walks in with a broken blind window or a dead feeder part, you find the match, and you show how it goes on. Open on the broken piece and use Chris G.'s line, exactly what I needed to fix my blind.
