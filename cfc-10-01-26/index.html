1
I was born in San Angelo, and I've been fencing it for more than twenty years
The origin and the face
▾
Your About page already says you were born here and built your shop in town, and this puts your face and voice on that story so the next customer knows who they are calling.
Spot A
I was born and raised in San Angelo. I've been putting up fences here for more than twenty years. I'm Antheny Pape, the owner and manager of Concho Fence. [OWNER: how you got into fencing] I always wanted to be part of the business community here. So I built my office, my shop and my yard right here in town, on Devonian Drive.
Spot B
I believe a fence ought to be beautiful, durable and well done. I also started ASAP Pipe with my partner, Sam Janca. We carry pipe and tubing in several sizes and lengths. At Concho Fence, we build cedar picket, chain link and ornamental iron fences, in several sizes and styles. We build custom fences too. We've fenced homes, businesses, industrial businesses, farms and ranches. Small jobs and large ones, in San Angelo and the towns around it.
Spot C
One customer wrote that we looked at the area and came up with a design that was perfect. It was a kennel for their little dogs, and they had a hard deadline, the Thanksgiving holiday. They wrote that we came through, and that we wanted their furry guests to be safe. Another customer wrote that the estimate was fair and stayed at that price. Estimates are free. If you need a fence, call me.
2
Watching San Angelo grow from the fence line
The local anchor
▾
Your pages cover yards, job sites, business lots and ranches around San Angelo, and your Houzz page says you have enjoyed watching the town grow, so this lets you talk about the whole town from the fence line.
Spot B
I've been watching San Angelo grow from the fence line. I was born here, and I've enjoyed working in this town. [OWNER: one thing in San Angelo that has changed since you were a kid] In the neighborhoods, it's yards. That means wood privacy fences, cedar pickets and chain link. A fence keeps the kids and the dogs in, and it can add to what the property is worth. It can also help the yard fit in with the rest of the property.
Spot A
Then there's new construction. Every new job site needs a fence around it to keep it safe from damage and theft. So we put up temporary chain link or plastic mesh around the site. When the work's done, we take it down. Once a business opens, it needs its property secured. Every business property is a little different, so we figure out what fits. That might be chain link with barbed wire along the top, or ornamental iron.
Spot C
Iron stands up to rust, and it needs little to no maintenance. We've done commercial and industrial jobs around town, small ones and large ones. Out past the city, it's farms and ranches. We've fenced them in San Angelo and the surrounding towns. [OWNER: the towns you drive out to the most] Whether you're a homeowner, a business owner or a rancher, tell me what you've got. I'll tell you what I'd build.
3
Ask any fence company what happens to your old fence
The thing the big competitor will not say
▾
Three reviews on your own site describe the same thing, tearing out the old fence and hauling everything away, so this hands homeowners a question to ask any fence company and lets you answer it on camera.
Spot C
Ask any fence company one thing before you sign. What happens to my old fence? Tearing it out, trimming back the brush and hauling it off is a big part of the job. On an old fence line, there can be small trees and shrubs to trim and dead branches to pull out. One customer wrote that we tore down the old fence, trimmed what needed trimming, and set the posts on a Tuesday.
Spot A
We came back Thursday to fill in the fence and build the gate. They said we were clean and neat and left nothing behind. Another customer had dead branches all through the fence line. They wrote that we took all of it out and carried it away. We put up the new fence in a day and a half. [OWNER: whether tearing out and hauling off the old fence is in your estimate]
Spot B
A third customer wrote that the crew even helped spread the chat for their drive and cleaned up after themselves. So when you get a quote, ask what's in it. A good quote tells you all of it. Is the old fence coming out? Is the brush getting trimmed? Who's hauling it all away? Our estimates are free. A customer wrote that ours was fair and stayed at that price. Call me and I'll give you a number.
4
Pick the fence by the job it has to do
The useful one
▾
It costs you nothing to give away and comes straight from your own service pages, so a homeowner still choosing between cedar, chain link and iron leaves with a way to decide.
Spot B
A fence has one main job. Decide what it is before you call anybody. Want privacy? We build wood privacy fences. If you want a friendly, traditional look for the yard, that's cedar picket. Each picket has a peak at the top, and they're spaced evenly, so sunlight and wind pass between them. You can get regular pickets with some knot holes, or clear pickets with none.
Spot C
Cedar is a strong wood that lasts, and it stands up to harsh weather. We also build cedar presealed and stained, and with a top cap and trim. For strength and a clear view, it's chain link. It's one of the most popular choices for homes and businesses. It comes in several heights and in several colors, including black, green and brown. We can run barbed wire along the top for more security.
Spot A
To keep people out, it's ornamental iron. It's very hard to get through, and very hard to climb. Fencing a job site? We put up temporary chain link or plastic mesh, and we take it down when the work's done. Want something nobody else has? We build custom fences, in a lot of styles and colors. Tell me what the fence has to do, and I'll tell you what I'd build. The estimate is free.
