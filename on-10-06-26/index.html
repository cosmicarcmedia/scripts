1
My granddad drove into San Angelo with enough money for gas
The origin and the face
▾
Tommy already wrote the best origin story in the file, a banker ruined in 1929 who bought a seed store in 1942, and it sits on a page almost nobody reads. This puts his face and voice on it, which the daily product reels do not.
Spot A
My granddad, Walter Eric Olive, was a wealthy banker in the twenties. Then came the black Friday of 1929, and the bank failed. He took care of his depositors. Then he drove to San Angelo with enough money for gas and a place for his family. My dad, John, was eleven when they moved. My granddad got a job as a credit manager at an implement company. [OWNER: whether to tell what happened in 1936, the way you wrote it on your website]
Spot B
In 1942 he bought a seed store at 42 East Concho Avenue. He called it Olive Seed Store. He hoped his two sons would come home from the war in Europe and join him. My dad joined him around 1951. My mother, Mary, joined about 1955. She started the gifts and the Christmas, and that never went away.
Spot C
I'm Tommy Olive. I studied engineering at Texas Tech and worked at Exxon for two and a half years. I came back to San Angelo in 1976 to take over the family business. In the seventies I sold organic cures and native plants, totally unsuccessfully. Then I figured out the answer was in the soil. Debbie has taken the gift shop further and added a boutique. It's been eighty-four years. West Texas has been good to us, and we've tried to be good for West Texas. [OWNER: what you remember about your first year back]
2
October at the corner of Horn and Sherwood
The local anchor
▾
A garden center that turns into a Christmas room every fall is a local ritual people already write reviews about. This puts a date and a place on it, and adds the fall planting advice that fits the same month.
Spot B
We're on the corner of Horn Road and Sherwood Way. It's almost two acres of garden center. This time of year, the shop starts changing from fall to Christmas. Our own website calls it a Christmas wonderland. It also says our Christmas trees will last you a lifetime. Twelve of our Google reviews mention the Christmas decor. We carry koi and water garden supplies too. [OWNER: the day the Christmas room opens this year]
Spot A
One customer wrote that they can hardly wait to see our Christmas area this year. Another wrote that they remember this store from when they were a kid. In 2017 the Chamber of Commerce held a ribbon cutting for our seventy-fifth year. We've helped thousands of West Texans with their yards and gardens. Come meander through the gift shop and the boutique too.
Spot C
Fall is also for planting. Trees and shrubs grow roots all winter long, so don't wait for spring. Broccoli, cauliflower, Brussels sprouts and cabbage go in the ground now. Most of them can take freezing weather and snow. Onions come in January and February. Work compost into the soil first, and top it with pine bark mulch. We're open nine to six Monday through Friday, nine to four on Saturday, and closed on Sundays. Come see the shop turn into Christmas.
3
Will it grow here? Ask whoever's selling it
The thing the big competitor will not say
▾
Olive's says on its own site that it knows which plants do well here, which are a fight and which are impossible. Saying that on camera, with customers naming the staff who talked them out of a wrong buy, draws the line against any seller who only wants the sale.
Spot C
Wherever you buy a plant, ask one question. Will this grow in San Angelo? Some plants do great here, some are a fight, and some are impossible. We know which is which. When you've done something for over forty years, you pick it up. We answer questions on lawn care, garden health, pest problems, plant issues, water gardening and even Christmas trees.

Customers name our staff in their reviews. Rolando sat down, listened to a customer's story, and answered every question. Steven handled a sod order and delivery. Another customer called us a great place with great people, family owned. Troubled lawn turning yellow? Sickly shrub or tree? Not sure what to plant? 4
Push a nail into your soil
The useful one
▾
Water is expensive in West Texas and Tommy has a simple test and a funny eggshell story on his site that nobody sees. It costs nothing to give away, and it ends with the one product he has made for thirty years.
Spot A
Try this in your yard when the soil is dry. Push a nail into the ground. If you can't get it in without a hammer, your water can't get down either. Neither can the oxygen, and neither can the roots. Water costs money in West Texas, and we're told how much of it we can use. So where's yours going?
Spot B
Good soil is alive. It's full of fungus, bacteria and other tiny living things, and they need feeding. Sugar, cornmeal, compost and beer all feed them. A friend's son tossed the eggshells over the side fence every morning. A few months later his dad wondered why everything on the other side was growing so well. I've been making a soil bio-stimulant called Olive's OIL for over thirty years. You apply it with a hose end sprayer once or twice a month for six months.
Spot C
When you water, water deep. Put mulch on your beds and gardens. In a ten by ten foot area, sixty gallons of water can evaporate in a day. Mulch it, and almost none does. If you want help with your own soil, call us at 325 949 3756. Or push the nail in first, and tell me how it went.
