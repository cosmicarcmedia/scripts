3
Ask one question before anyone prepares your business return
The thing the big competitor will not say
▾
Reconciliation, the monthly or quarterly cadence and the question about who moves money in the office are all stated on the firm's own bookkeeping page, and most owners never think to put them to a tax preparer. It draws a clear line from the books to the return without naming anyone and without promising a result.
Spot C
Before anyone prepares your business tax return, ask them one question. Have my bank and credit card accounts been reconciled? That means somebody matched every line in your books to what your bank says happened. When we reconcile an account, here's what it can catch. Lost checks. Lost deposits. Transfers and wires nobody authorized. Bank charges that shouldn't be there, and transactions the bank posted wrong. It can also catch money going missing from inside the company.
Spot A
It also tells you how your business is doing. You can't know until every account has been matched and the numbers land on your financial statements. We do this every month or every quarter, depending on the package you pick. We check your mail, handle your payables and receivables, and clean up your general ledger. Then we hand you a profit and loss statement and a balance sheet.
Spot B
One more thing to ask any firm. Who in your office can move money, and who checks the bank statement? We look at how the jobs are divided between your staff and ours. We pay attention to segregation of duties, so the same person isn't handling everything. That's an internal control, and it protects the business. We can review your operations and recommend which jobs your staff keeps and which ones we take.
4
Three questions to answer before you set up QuickBooks
The useful one
▾
QuickBooks setup decides whether the numbers can be trusted for years, and the three questions come straight from the firm's own QuickBooks page. It costs nothing to give away and the owner has been a ProAdvisor since 2005.
Spot A
QuickBooks is easy to use. The setup is where people get into trouble. It takes all the same information you'd need to keep paper books. If the setup isn't done correctly, nothing it tells you later can be trusted. I've been a QuickBooks ProAdvisor since 2005, so I've seen how this goes.
Spot B
Which accounting method will you use? What chart of accounts does your business need? And how will you handle your beginning balances? If you don't know the answers, ask your tax preparer before you enter a single transaction.
Spot C
Then pick a starting date. Enter every outstanding balance and every opening balance as of that date. After that, enter your transactions from the starting date forward. If you're switching from paper books in the middle of the year, it's more complicated than starting in January, so give yourself time. A good setup also covers payroll, receivables, payables and inventory, if your business uses them. Train your staff on it too. After that, have someone review your QuickBooks data every month, every quarter or once a year. That review helps with your management reports and with your taxes. If you'd like help with any of that, setup and training is what we do.
