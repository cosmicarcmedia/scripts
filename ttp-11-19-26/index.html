3
Before you hire a plumber, ask who owns the truck
Competitor contrast
▾
Their own pages say the pumping runs on their own trucks and crews, and a customer wrote that they came back the next day at no extra charge. Those two facts give homeowners a pair of questions any big plumbing company has to answer.
Before you hire a plumber, ask a question most people never think of. Who owns the truck that's coming to your house? Ours are locally owned, with our own crews. When you call us for septic, grease trap or holding tank pumping, the same company that took your call sends the truck. Nobody at the other end of the phone has to track down a subcontractor and ask them to call you back. Next, ask if they'll show you what caused it. We run a camera down the line, find the cause, and show you. Then we clear it. Second question. What happens if the problem comes back? One customer wrote that we cleared his main line on a Friday. It backed up again, and we came back Saturday and fixed it at no extra cost. He called us a company that stands behind their work. Third question, if you've got a water heater in trouble. Will they tell you whether to repair it or replace it? We give honest advice on that, and when we install one, it's sized right, installed to code and tested before we leave. We test every fix before we pack up, too. Ask us any of it. [OWNER: what you tell a customer who is deciding between you and a big franchise plumber]
4
What your house tells you before the pipes back up
Useful customer education
▾
Every sign in the script comes from the company's own drain, septic and water heater pages. It teaches a homeowner what to watch for, and it shows the shop checks for the cause before anything gets cleared.
Your plumbing talks to you before it fails. You just have to know what you're listening for. Is a sink draining slow? Does the tub gurgle? Is a main line backing up? Those are early warning signs. If the backups keep coming back, it can be grease, tree roots, or a damaged line. That's why we use machine cleaning and a camera inspection. We clear the line, and we show you what caused it. Septic tanks are the same. A tank that goes too long between pumpings can back up, drain slowly, or put strain on the rest of the system. If you can't remember the last time yours was pumped, that's your sign. Tell us about the property and we'll schedule around it, then leave it clean when we're done. [OWNER: how often you tell customers to pump, and why it varies] Now your water heater. Rusty water, popping sounds, or a lukewarm shower are early warnings. A failed tank can dump fifty gallons into your home in minutes. If you hear the popping, call before the day it floods the hallway. If it's already an emergency, burst pipe, sewage backup, no hot water, call right away. Every minute of an uncontrolled leak adds damage. We're on the phone twenty four seven.
